# Chapter 1: Intro & Creator Foundations

## Core Idea
The goal of the course is to hand you the *building blocks* of video creation — script, scene, camera, edit, package — so you can make your own high-quality creator videos. Oliur has made tech videos for nearly a decade; the method is a repeatable end-to-end pipeline, not a list of gear to buy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The end-to-end creation pipeline** — the spine of the whole course: **Idea → Script → Scene & Lighting → Camera Setup → Shoot A-Roll → Shoot B-Roll → Edit (cut + color + audio) → Package (thumbnail + title) → Upload → Monetise**.
  - When to use: every video. Treat it as a checklist so nothing high-leverage gets skipped.
  - How: pre-production decisions (script, title) feed shooting; shooting feeds editing; editing reveals the B-roll list.
- **The 80/20 of video quality** — "80% of a video is your scene, your lighting, and the audio," not the camera body.
  - When to use: any spend or effort decision. Put money/time into scene, light, and mic *before* a fancy camera.

## Key Concepts
- **A-Roll**: the talking-head footage — you facing camera, the spoken backbone of the video.
- **B-Roll**: supplementary footage (product shots, demos) laid *over* the A-Roll to hide cuts and illustrate points.
- **Building blocks, not rules**: the course gives a system to customise, not a rigid formula. Adapt every template to your content type.
- **Tech-channel reality**: covering products/experiences means less storytelling burden than narrative channels — the workflow is tuned to that, so adapt it for storytelling niches.

## Mental Models
- Think of a video as **A-Roll spine + B-Roll skin**: record the spine, then dress it.
- Treat the camera as the *least* important variable. Viewers tolerate average video; they abandon bad audio.
- "Practice really does make perfect" — confidence and speed are earned over 100–200 videos, not unlocked by gear.

## Anti-patterns
- **Buying a pro camera first**: spending your budget on a body before scene/light/audio is the most common beginner mistake.
- **Waiting to feel ready on camera**: nobody is natural at first; not shipping until you feel confident stalls the only thing that builds confidence — reps.

## Key Takeaways
1. Follow the pipeline end to end; each stage feeds the next.
2. Spend on scene, lighting, and audio before camera quality.
3. Start with what you have (an iPhone is fine); upgrade the body only when you specifically need its look.
4. Customise every template Oliur gives you to your own niche and aesthetic.
