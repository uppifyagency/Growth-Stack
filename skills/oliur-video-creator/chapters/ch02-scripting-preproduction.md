# Chapter 2: Scripting & Pre-Production

## Core Idea
There is no single "perfect idea" process — sustainable channels run on a light, repeatable idea-capture system and scripts written like you're talking to a friend. Structure the script just enough to read it naturally to camera; over-formality kills it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Notion Kanban idea pipeline** — capture ideas in Notion with three columns: **Ideas → In Progress → Uploaded**.
  - When to use: continuously, to remove "what do I make next" friction.
  - How: dump every idea (even random morning ones — they often become the best videos); move cards across as you produce.
- **The 3-part script structure** — every script breaks into **Intro → Main Script → Outro**.
  - How: in the Main Script, put **each sentence on its own line** for easy reading; use **H3 headings** to separate topic blocks (e.g. screen, battery, camera); **bold** the lines you read *to camera* vs. lines you read off-screen.
- **The video Notion template** — sections for: **title ideas**, **thumbnail variations**, **sponsor info**, **post-upload checklist**, **YouTube description**, and the **script** itself.
- **Script-first / mix approach** — choose per video between a fully written script and bare bullet points (or a mix). Bullets can spark a natural, conversational delivery.

## Key Concepts
- **Write like talking to a friend**: friendly, not formal, not "super smart." The viewer is a person you're speaking to.
- **Bold = to-camera**: the bolded lines are delivered looking at the lens; the rest can be read off the iPad while facing the camera (B-roll covers it).
- **Don't over-index on originality**: most ideas are recycled; chasing an "amazing" idea every time is unsustainable.

## Mental Models
- Think of the script as a **delivery aid, not a contract** — bullet points often produce more natural speech than a rigid full script.
- The title is part of pre-production thinking, not an afterthought — see Ch 4 (titles can frame the whole video).

## Anti-patterns
- **Demanding a brilliant idea for every upload**: it doesn't scale and stalls publishing.
- **Writing formal, essay-style scripts**: they read stiff and "unfriendly" on camera.
- **One undifferentiated wall of text**: no line breaks/headings makes reading to camera harder.

## Key Takeaways
1. Capture ideas in a Notion Kanban (Ideas / In Progress / Uploaded) so you never start from zero.
2. Structure: Intro → Main → Outro; one sentence per line; H3 headings per topic; bold the to-camera lines.
3. Write conversationally — "as if talking to a friend."
4. Mix written script and bullets; find what produces your most natural delivery.
