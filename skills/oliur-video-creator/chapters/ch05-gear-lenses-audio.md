# Chapter 5: Gear In-Depth — Lenses & Audio

## Core Idea
You need only one or two lenses to start, chosen by aperture and use-case — not a collection. And audio matters more than video sharpness: get the mic close and treat the room before spending on equipment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 5 lens-buying questions**: (1) **Budget**; (2) **What content** you'll shoot; (3) **Aperture** wanted; (4) **Prime vs. zoom**; (5) **Will you travel with it** (weight/size).
- **Aperture rule**: lower f-number = more light to the sensor + shallower depth of field. Want background blur → low aperture (f/1.4, f/1.8). Want everything sharp (landscape, top-down desk) → *raise* the aperture number.
- **The lens-by-scenario map**:
  - **One-lens starter / 90% of B-roll**: **Tamron 28–75 f/2.8** or **Sigma 28–70 f/2.8** (same lens, Sigma smaller) — versatile, reliable.
  - **Intimate talking-head A-roll**: **Sony 24mm f/1.4 GM** (wide, close, heavy blur, fast eye-AF).
  - **Tight spaces / interiors / landscapes**: **Tamron 17–28** wide angle.
  - **Macro / extreme close-ups**: **Sony 90mm macro f/2.8** (not essential — the 28–75/28–70 already focus close).
  - **Far subjects / sports / animals / portraits**: **70–200** (telephoto; flattering face compression).
  - **Cheap nifty-fifty**: **50mm f/1.4** — used for the finance channel (sit further back); cheap, not sharp, loud AF — fine for static talking, great photo lens.
- **Focal-length face rule**: wide lenses (≤~50mm) **warp** faces; **50–100mm** is the sweet spot for shooting people (flattering compression).
- **G Master = top tier** (sharpest glass, best AF) — but only worth it for high-end work; online video maxes at ~4K (≈12–13 MP), so most people won't see the extra sharpness.

## Key Concepts
- **Prime vs. zoom**: primes (single focal length, e.g. 50mm) are smaller, lighter, often cheaper, lower aperture; zooms are heavier and may have higher/variable aperture.
- **Avoid variable-aperture zooms for video**: brightness shifts as you zoom — always check the aperture stays static across the zoom range.
- **Build the kit over time**: don't buy many lenses at once; one or two cover almost everything.
- **Audio > video sharpness**: bad audio is painful and viewers click away; an old 1080p video with good audio is still watchable.
- **Mic types**: **pencil mic** (controlled studio/instrument, what Oliur uses), **shotgun mic** (directional, on-camera, windshield/dead-cat for wind), **lavalier** (clip-on, interview, close to mouth), **dynamic podcast mic** (close to mouth, rejects room noise, ~$250–350).
- **XLR vs. 3.5mm**: XLR is higher quality and resists interference (phones can induce noise in cheap 3.5mm cables); routes through a module (e.g. **Sony XLR-K3M**, ~$500–600, ships with the FX3) directly into camera — no syncing.
- **32-bit float** (newer DJI mic): records so much data you can fix clipping/levels after recording.

## Mental Models
- Choose a lens like solving an equation of **budget × scenario × aperture × portability**, not by brand prestige.
- Treat **room treatment + mic distance** as the real audio quality levers; the mic price is secondary.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing G Master / max sharpness** for online video you'll never blow up past 4K.
- **Buying the 90mm macro** when your 28–75 already focuses close enough.
- **Variable-aperture zooms** for video.
- **Recording with the on-camera mic**: too far away, picks up all room noise.
- **Untreated, echoey rooms**: no amount of mic spend fixes a live room.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with one versatile lens: Tamron 28–75 / Sigma 28–70 f/2.8 covers ~90% of shots.
2. Low aperture for blur; raise aperture for landscapes/top-down. Shoot faces at 50–100mm to avoid warping.
3. Avoid variable-aperture zooms for video; build the kit over time.
4. Recommended audio default: **DJI Mic kit** (wireless, records onboard, works on phone too). Step up to XLR + pencil/podcast mic for static studio work.
5. Get the mic close + treat the room (carpet, panels, soft furnishings) — that, not the mic price, drives clean audio.
