# Chapter 6: Shooting with an iPhone (A-Roll + B-Roll)

## Core Idea
The phone in your pocket is the right way to start. Because 80% of a video is scene, lighting, and audio, an iPhone (or recent Android) with good light and a real mic produces footage "very very close" to a mirrorless camera. Same principles, same edit, cheaper kit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The iPhone video settings** (Settings → Camera):
  - **Record Video: 4K at 30fps** (or 4K 24fps — either works).
  - **Turn OFF HDR Video** (looks worse; flat-off looks better).
  - Enable **Grid**; turn on **Preserve Settings** so the camera stays where you left it.
  - In the camera: shoot on the **1× lens** for best A-roll quality; ProRes disabled; frame rate toggle is on-screen.
- **The same 80/20 foundation as pro shooting**:
  - **Scene**: dedicated tidy setup; nothing directly behind your head; accessories to the sides/top/bottom for depth and personality.
  - **Separation**: sit a few feet off the wall.
  - **Top-third framing** (don't center your head).
  - **Lighting**: natural window light if possible; otherwise artificial through a **softbox** to soften shadows (budget options work).
  - **Audio**: spend here first — **DJI Mic** under the shirt on a magnet (~$200–300), plugs into the phone via Lightning/USB-C.
- **Apple Watch remote**: control the iPhone camera and see the framing on the watch; hit record remotely — useful for solo A-roll.
- **The iPhone B-roll multi-lens method**: mix the **1×, 2×, and 3×** lenses for variety; shoot **60fps** to slow down later; slow panning motions; tap to focus; **hold to lock AE/AF**, then drag exposure down for moody product shots.

## Key Concepts
- **Composition is learned over time**: figuring out angles/lighting on the fly — inspired by movies, TV, reviews, even magazine photos.
- **Offload + edit anywhere**: AirDrop to a Mac (or Dropbox/Google Drive/cable to a PC); edit in Final Cut Pro or any editor (even the TikTok/Instagram editor works).
- **Same grade as pro footage**: import, **mute**, slow 60fps clips to **50%** (60→30fps), add the **Oliur O1 LUT (~30%)** + light color-wheel highlight/mid-tone tweaks — the iPhone B-roll then looks near-professional.

## Mental Models
- Treat the iPhone as proof that **the camera body is not the bottleneck** — light, scene, and mic are.
- Think "**buy the mic before the camera**" — a DJI Mic on a phone beats a bare pro camera with weak audio.

## Anti-patterns
- **HDR Video on**: leave it off for better-looking, more gradeable footage.
- **Centering yourself / cluttering behind your head**: same framing mistakes as with a pro camera.
- **Spending on a fancy camera first**: the money is better placed on scene, light, and the DJI Mic.

## Key Takeaways
1. iPhone settings: 4K 30 (or 24) fps, HDR off, grid on, Preserve Settings on, 1× lens for A-roll.
2. Apply the same scene/lighting/top-third rules as pro shooting; softbox the light.
3. Spend on audio first — DJI Mic plugs straight into the phone.
4. B-roll: mix 1×/2×/3×, shoot 60fps, slow to 50%, lock AE/AF and pull exposure for product shots.
5. Edit identically (Final Cut Pro), slow-mo + Oliur O1 LUT at ~30% — output rivals mirrorless.
