# Chapter 3: Camera Setup & Shooting (A-Roll + B-Roll)

## Core Idea
The signature "Oliur look" comes from low-aperture lenses, manual control, a clean scene shot *into a corner*, and a frictionless setup that's always ready. A-Roll is recorded first (talking head); B-Roll is shot after editing the A-Roll, from a list of needed shots.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The manual camera baseline (Sony a7 IV)** — exact settings to replicate the look:
  - Resolution **4K** (XAVC S 4K); mode **Manual**; **24fps** (24p) for cinematic feel (30fps fine if preferred).
  - Recording: highest option — **100 Mbps, 4:2:2 10-bit** (more data = more grading leeway).
  - Picture profile: **S-Log3** (Picture Profile 8 on the a7 IV) — flat profile, easier to grade.
  - **Shutter = ~2× frame rate** (24fps → shutter 50).
  - **Aperture as low as the lens allows** (f/2.8 on a zoom, f/1.4 on the prime).
  - **ISO at base** (base ISO 800 on the a7 IV — the value with no lines under the number).
  - **White balance matched to lights**: custom **5600 K** (key light is 5600 K).
  - Shoot **60fps** for B-roll (the a7 IV supports it).
- **The A-Roll scene recipe** — frictionless + clean + separated:
  - **Frictionless setup**: a dedicated, always-ready space — sit down, mount camera, press record.
  - **Get the scene right before recording** ("a lot of the work has to be done before you record" — you can't fix a messy background in the edit).
  - **Shoot into a corner** to create depth/separation when you don't have a long room.
  - **Kill natural light**: blinds down, door shut — so you fully control color and exposure.
- **The 3-light A-Roll setup**: a **key light** close to the face (with grid + dome) plus two accent lights behind — typically one **warm** and one **bluish** — to light the back/side of the head and create separation.
- **Top-third framing**: camera slightly low so your head sits in the **top third** of the frame (like film/TV), not centered.
- **The B-Roll shooting system** — shoot B-roll *after* editing A-roll, working from a list of shots the A-roll needs.
  - **60fps + shutter ~125** for slow-mo and smooth pans/handheld.
  - **Shoot long (70mm) for B-roll** — long focal lengths add depth and look more commercial/cinematic than always-wide.
  - **Build the scene in layers**: add accessories (desk mat, books, headphones) for foreground/background depth; light only the product (one key + one tube/accent light) for a moody look.
  - **Replicate, don't invent**: study movies/TV/other videos and copy shots.

## Key Concepts
- **Shallow depth of field**: subject sharp, background blurred — only achievable with a pro camera + low-aperture lens; the background needn't be far (he can nearly touch the wall).
- **Coverage moves**: smooth tripod pans (fluid head), handheld (active stabilization, hold camera to chest, sway body L↔R), close-ups, and the **reflection-reveal** shot (pan so a light glare slides off to reveal product detail).
- **Microfiber cloth**: wipe products before every shot for a clean, dust/fingerprint-free look.

## Mental Models
- Think of the A-Roll setup as a **studio you never tear down** — friction removed = more videos made.
- Treat the **lens, not the room size**, as the source of background blur.

## Anti-patterns
- **"Fix it in post"**: relying on editing to clean a bad scene — most of the work must happen before recording.
- **Centering your head**: too much headroom looks amateurish.
- **Plain white background**: add a shelving unit/accessories instead; but don't over-clutter.
- **Variable-aperture zooms for video** (see Ch 5): brightness shifts mid-zoom.

## Key Takeaways
1. Shoot 4K, manual, 24fps, S-Log3, shutter ~2× fps, base ISO, WB 5600 K to match lights.
2. Make the setup frictionless and the scene clean before you press record.
3. Shoot into a corner + low aperture lens for separation; head in the top third.
4. Three-light A-roll (key near face + two accents); two-light moody B-roll (key + tube).
5. Shoot B-roll last, at 60fps/shutter 125, at ~70mm, in layers; replicate shots you admire.
