# Chapter 7: The Profile Funnel

## Core Idea
When you siphon audience members to your profile, they land somewhere — so the profile itself must convert. Treat your social profile as a **sales page** designed to capture followers. Visitors scan in seconds with short attention spans; every element must answer "what do you do / who do you help?" fast and align with the next.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Profile Funnel — the scan order Welsh uses to decide whether to follow** (each step is a checkpoint that either advances or loses the visitor):
  1. **Banner image** — in <2 seconds: *what do you do / who do you help?* If unclear, they click off.
  2. **Tagline** — does it align with the banner? Formula: *Who do you help · what do you help them do · how do you help them do it.*
  3. **Headshot** — clean and professional (no beer-in-a-bar shots); iPhone + a good friend is fine.
  4. **About section** — crisp, cohesive, easy to read; expands on what you do, adds granularity and social proof.
  5. **Featured section (LinkedIn)** — resources, YouTube, and especially a **website** (where creativity lives → email subscriber).
  6. **Pinned tweet (Twitter)** — must be both **popular** (shows engagement) and **informative** (shows you'll teach them).
- **Profile = Sales Page** — the governing reframe (carried from the LinkedIn Playbook): impress and capture, don't just exist.

## Key Concepts
- **Two-second rule** — banner + tagline must communicate what you do / who you help in two seconds or less, or you lose the visitor.
- **Alignment across elements** — banner, tagline, and about must tell one coherent story; misalignment breaks trust.
- **Micro-niche profiles convert hardest** — Caitlin Bourgyne ("Stop guessing. Market smarter." → helps product teams find buy-triggers) is a "no-brainer follow" for the exact audience because she niched all the way down.
- **Worked examples** — Madeline Mann (rapid-fire career advice), Ray Green (sales jargon tuned to revenue leaders), Caitlin Bourgyne (micro-niche + social proof in pinned tweet), Blake Emal (humor-as-brand banner + sharp tagline + strong-opinion pinned tweet).

## Mental Models
- **The profile is a funnel, not a bio** — each element's job is to advance the visitor toward the follow/subscribe.
- **Match jargon to audience** — sales jargon works for revenue leaders, but strip it for general audiences.

## Anti-patterns
- **Vague banner/tagline** that doesn't say what you do — instant click-off.
- **Unprofessional headshot** (beer, buddies, posing) — undercuts credibility.
- **Misaligned sections** that don't reinforce one message.
- **A pinned tweet that's neither popular nor informative** — wastes the highest-leverage Twitter real estate.

## Key Takeaways
1. Banner = what you do / who you help in two seconds.
2. Tagline = who · what · how. Headshot = clean and professional.
3. About = crisp, cohesive, social proof. Featured = website + email capture.
4. Pinned tweet = popular + informative. Nail these six and visitors convert to followers.
