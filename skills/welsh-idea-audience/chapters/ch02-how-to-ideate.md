# Chapter 2: How to Ideate

## Core Idea
Most people ideate badly because they start from a *product* or a generic problem they have no edge on. The reliable path is to start from yourself — your unique combination of skills and interests — so you create a category you alone own. This is the gateway into **A Niche of One**, the foundation of the entire course (IAPP — Idea Audience Proof Product).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Ways to Ideate** (introduced here, ranked worst→best):
  1. **Product-focused / product-led ideation** (the old way) — invent or improve a product. Worst, because most people don't build physical/software products, so you must outsource (risk), and you usually lack deep expertise in the thing itself. "Wouldn't it be cool if there was a ____" fails when the blank is unfamiliar.
  2. **Solving your own problems** (the recent way) — build around a problem you've solved or are solving. Strong *only if you already have distribution* (a large audience or paid-ads/marketing skill). The LinkedIn Playbook came from this — it worked because solving the problem *built* the distribution. Without distribution, a great solution can't reach buyers.
  3. **A Niche of One** (the new way, the course's foundation) — combine your **skills × interests** into a very specific creative or technical idea no one else is making. You become the only person in that micro-niche — the category creator. Comes with a better distribution method *and* built-in service and product ideas.

## Key Concepts
- **Distribution defined** — getting your product/idea in front of people via selling, audience, marketing, or advertising. A solution without distribution can't get traction.
- **Niche of One = a complex equation built over multiple chapters** — skills + interests stitched into a unique idea (Ch 3, Part 1), then a background and transformation story (Ch 4-5), then distribution (Ch 10), then a service business (Ch 3, Part 2), then a product (Ch 12-13).
- **The Skills Document** — a resource of ~100 skills; people vastly *underestimate* their own skills. Use the list to brainstorm skills you didn't realize you had (validated across 100+ coached people).
- **Micro-niche ownership** — by being the only one combining a specific skill and interest, competition drops to ~zero; "you're only working against yourself."

## Mental Models
- **Don't chase the product; start from the self** — your edge is the intersection of what you can do and what you're into, not a clever product idea.
- **Skill not unique + interest not unique = unique combination** — neither input needs to be rare; the *pairing* is what's rare and ownable.

## Anti-patterns
- **Product-led ideation when you can't build the product** — outsourcing/hiring is risk you don't want when starting.
- **"Solve your own problem" with no distribution** — you'll build something good that no one ever sees.
- **Underrating your own skill set** — assuming you have "no skills" without working through a brainstorm.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reject product-led ideation; start from your skills × interests.
2. "Solve your own problem" only works if you already own distribution.
3. The goal is a micro-niche you own — be a Niche of One.
4. Brainstorm 3-5 skill+interest combinations using the skills worksheet.
