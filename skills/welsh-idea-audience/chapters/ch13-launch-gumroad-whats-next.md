# Chapter 13: Launch — Building in Gumroad, Creating a Compelling Launch & What's Next

## Core Idea
Host the product on Gumroad (cheap, simple, fast), then launch it with a **14-day pre-sell** that builds excitement and stacks testimonials/social proof. The launch is itself a build sprint — the Four-Pass Process runs *during* the launch window. Close by collecting feedback, raising the price, and inviting sharing/affiliates.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Building in Gumroad** — free version (8.5% + $0.30/sale) or $10/mo (3.5% + $0.30/sale). Built-in tools: a **follow button** (collect email subscribers), a **sales widget** (buy button you embed anywhere), built-in emailing via **SendGrid** (high deliverability), and **marketplace discovery**. Three product types: **classic** (ready now), **pre-order** (pre-sell before completion — Welsh sold ~400 licenses pre-completion), **membership** (monthly subscription). Set **versions** (e.g. course $99 vs. course + 45-min coaching call $300). **Workflows** = email triggers fired by actions (e.g. buy the coaching version → auto-send a Calendly link).
- **The Three Launch Options**: (1) **Surprise** — "I have a product!" (doesn't work). (2) **Pre-sell** — announce weeks ahead, build excitement (the recommended method, taught here). (3) **Build in Public** — share everything (behind the scenes, metrics, revenue, expenses); powerful but time-consuming.
- **The 14-Day Launch Plan** (timed to a 2-week first course):
  - **Day 1** (biggest day) — finish landing page, finish artwork, test the Gumroad buy button on your own site, create a pre-sell discount/incentive (Welsh: $99 pre-sell → $149 at launch), write email + social messaging, review the communication plan, then **announce intentions** through the distribution network + email list.
  - **Days 2-9** — run the Four-Pass Process: Pass 1 brain-dump (days 2-3), Pass 2 notes + bullet summaries (days 4-5), Pass 3 media (days 6-7); **Day 8 (early)** share behind-the-scenes teaser; Pass 4 resources (day 8 latter-day 9).
  - **Days 10-14** — Day 10 send slides to trusted friends for review; Days 11-12 record screencasts + a short write-up to distribution/email; Day 13 request testimonials from reviewers, post them everywhere, run a full buyer flow check; **Day 14 = launch day** — blitz social, distribution network, and email; share testimonials; remind it's the last day for the discount.
  - **Post-launch** — keep collecting testimonials everywhere (FOMO from 35-45 five-star reviews lifts conversions), and **raise the price** (kill the incentive).

## Key Concepts
- **Pre-sell de-risks the build** — selling before completion (Gumroad pre-order) funds and validates the product.
- **Social proof compounds conversions** — testimonials on Gumroad/landing page/website spike conversion rates; collect ruthlessly.
- **What's Next (Ch 23)** — Welsh updates the course over a year using buyer feedback (free to existing customers); asks buyers to share it (affordable/accessible mantra), leave a Gumroad rating or email testimonial, or apply to be an affiliate.

## Mental Models
- **Launch = build sprint + marketing campaign** — you're creating and selling simultaneously across 14 days.
- **Blitz on launch day** — concentrate the distribution network and email list into one coordinated push.
- **The incentive expires** — scarcity (price rise after the window) drives the final-day surge.

## Anti-patterns
- **The "surprise" launch** — no runway, no excitement, weak results.
- **Waiting for a finished product to start marketing** — pre-sell builds momentum and revenue early.
- **Forgetting to remove the incentive / raise the price** after the window — leaves money and urgency on the table.
- **Skipping post-launch testimonial collection** — you forgo the FOMO-driven conversion lift.

## Key Takeaways
1. Host on Gumroad; use pre-order, versions, and workflow email triggers.
2. Pre-sell over 14 days; Day 1 sets everything up and announces intentions.
3. Run the Four-Pass build *during* the launch window; teaser at day 8.
4. Blitz on day 14; then collect testimonials relentlessly and raise the price.
