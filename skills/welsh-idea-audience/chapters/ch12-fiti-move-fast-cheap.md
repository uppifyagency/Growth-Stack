# Chapter 12: F.I.T.I + Move Fast & Cheap

## Core Idea
Two systems close the Proof and open the Product sections: **F.I.T.I** is how you climb the three service levels and 10× your rate by niching down; **Move Fast & Cheap** is how you build a minimum-viable digital product in ~two weeks so you can sell scalable, 24/7 income. Welsh used F.I.T.I to go from $250/hr to $2,500/hr in 18 months.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **F.I.T.I — Feedback, Iteration, Testimonials, Increase** (the engine for moving up the three service levels):
  - **Feedback** — ask customers where/how they get the most value and what they want more of (formal survey, quick email, or conversation; document it in your CRM). Also factor *what you enjoy* — Welsh moved from consulting (playbooks, decks — dreaded) to advising (loved). Ask: what gives me energy / feels like play / do I dread?
  - **Iteration** — find the **crossroad** where your most enjoyable activities intersect what customers want/need; develop those, eliminate/delegate the rest. Output is a smaller, more niched offering → revisit the Profile Funnel, Art of the Capture, and Rapid Relevant Content as you niche down.
  - **Testimonials** — collect ruthlessly at every level; ask when customers express gratitude, after a specific result, or screenshot social praise. Guide them to highlight a specific result. Use Welsh's template ("I have a quick favor… would you write a brief testimonial…").
  - **Increase** — raise prices as demand rises. Rule: *if you double prices and win-rate halves, that's fine — same money, half the customers, half the time.* Push lower-value customers to information products.
- **Move Fast & Cheap — building an MVP product** (4 steps):
  1. **Narrow the topic** to one specific problem you know cold ("teach in your sleep"). Don't build "a course on Spanish" — build "how to order a drink at a Spanish restaurant." Then **time-box** it (first course = 2 weeks).
  2. **Create an outline** — sections → chapters/lessons aligned to 1-2 outcomes; outline in Google Sheets; break work into daily bite-size tasks.
  3. **The Four-Pass Process** — Pass 1: brain-dump everything you'd say out loud onto slides (start→finish). Pass 2: move spoken words into presenter notes, pull high-level bullet summaries onto slides. Pass 3: add images/media/GIFs/videos. Pass 4: add resources, checklists, tools, links.
  4. **Get feedback** along the way from honest people (ideal buyers, current customers, friends) — never wait until everything's built.

## Key Concepts
- **Welsh's pricing climb** — $250/hr (broad: "SaaS founders to $50M") → $500/hr (early-stage SMB SaaS, pivot consulting→advising) → $2,500/hr (micro-niche: healthcare SMB SaaS, where he's "one of the best in the world").
- **Low production, high access, high affordability** — Welsh's product mantra: shoot one take per chapter (Loom/Zoom), little/no editing; price for the buyer's income; pick a short, memorable name; use Canva or Upwork/Fiverr for cover art.
- **Narrow beats broad** — better for students to learn 1-2 things deeply than to skim 150 topics.

## Mental Models
- **The intersection is the win** — do more of what you love + what customers want; charge more (win-win-win).
- **Same money, half the time** — price increases that shrink the client count can be a net win.
- **Document your own feelings** — track what energizes vs. drains you, on a schedule.

## Anti-patterns
- **Being shy about price increases or testimonials** — ask ruthlessly; demand justifies the raise.
- **Staying broad** in niche or product scope — both dilute expertise and value.
- **Building everything before getting feedback** — leads to demoralizing rebuilds and missed launches.
- **High-scope, high-production first product** (like this very course / the LinkedIn Playbook) — dip your toe; go cheap and fast.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run F.I.T.I in a loop: feedback → iterate to the love/need intersection → testimonials → increase prices.
2. Narrow the product to one specific problem; time-box to two weeks.
3. Build with the Four-Pass Process (brain-dump → notes+bullets → media → resources).
4. Get honest feedback continuously; price for your buyer; ship low-production, high-access.
