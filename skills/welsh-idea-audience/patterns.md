# Patterns & Techniques — Idea to Audience

## A Niche of One (the foundational play)
**When to use**: choosing what to build/create when you lack a product idea or distribution.
**How**: list your skills (use the ~100-skill worksheet) and your genuine interests; stitch one skill × one interest into a hyper-specific idea no one else makes (Excel × rap → weekly rhyme-density breakdowns). Extend the same combo into a service (freelance + 1:1) and later a product.
**Trade-offs**: niche feels "too small," but uniqueness = zero competition and built-in distribution; expect to fail before it lands.

## The Skill × Interest → Service → Product Ladder
**When**: turning a Niche of One into income.
**How**: idea (Part 1) → distribution network → service business (Part 2, time-for-money) → record learnings → productize the recurring asks (Move Fast & Cheap).
**Trade-offs**: sequential by design; don't stand up the service before you have distribution.

## Play Moneyball (daily get-on-base content)
**When**: building an audience to make income.
**How**: master one platform; post relevant content daily; build a social ecosystem (adjacent + contrarian voices); set notifications; siphon larger audiences via early thoughtful comments. Ignore virality.
**Trade-offs**: slow and unglamorous; compounds over months, not days.

## The LinkedIn Ecosystem Hack
**When**: you want to be first to comment on key creators' posts.
**How**: bookmark their *activity* page (not profile), note their daily post time, comment first with value that makes their audience click your profile.
**Trade-offs**: manual upkeep; pairs with the distribution bookmark folder.

## The Profile Funnel (profile = sales page)
**When**: turning profile visitors into followers.
**How**: optimize in scan order — banner (what/who in 2s) → tagline (who·what·how) → headshot (clean) → about (crisp + proof) → featured/website (→ email) → pinned tweet (popular + informative). Keep all elements aligned to one message.
**Trade-offs**: micro-niche profiles convert hardest but address fewer people.

## The Content Formula + Content Matrix
**When**: generating on-strategy content fast and consistently.
**How**: gut-check every post on Outcome/Focus/Structure/Opinions (aim sniper, not sprayer). For volume, build a 5-layer matrix: Mantra → Taglines → Pillars → Core Concepts (strong opinions) → Structures; combine a core concept × a structure to mint posts. Say the same thing 15 ways.
**Trade-offs**: requires defining a mantra and real opinions first; rotate structures to avoid staleness.

## The Scroll Test (pattern interruption)
**When**: getting anyone to stop scrolling.
**How**: text — first sentence lands hard, each sentence makes the reader crave the next (test near-identical posts; expect large engagement gaps). Images — be radically different vs. stock photos. Combo — color/highlight to pull the eye. Video — short, eye-catching, impactful. Own a distinctive style.
**Trade-offs**: polarization must be authentic — manufactured "wild takes" are easy to spot and backfire.

## The Art of the Capture (email landing page)
**When**: converting attention into owned email subscribers.
**How**: above the fold in ≤3s — bold primary statement (what they get) + secondary header (how, with social proof) + subscriber count + one bold CTA. Less is more; white space; no stock photos; one choice.
**Trade-offs**: simplicity beats feature-richness; resist adding multiple CTAs.

## The Content Web (multi-platform distribution)
**When**: expanding reach after mastering one platform.
**How**: map where your audience hangs out; tailor format + CTA per platform — long-form + polite ask (Twitter/Reddit/Indie Hackers), visual snippets + subtle CTA (Slack/FB groups), direct links (HN/Designer News). Route LinkedIn via the Featured section, not in-post links. Track rules in a labeled "content distribution" bookmark folder.
**Trade-offs**: complex to manage — the bookmark system is the memory aid.

## The MVP Service Business (7 components + automation)
**When**: standing up a service after building distribution.
**How**: website, service description, FAQs, inquiry form, booking, rate sheet, payments. Disclose pricing up front; FAQ + questionnaire to self-qualify/disqualify. Automate with Carrd + Airtable + Stripe + Zapier so the 35-45 min/client manual flow drops to ~4 min. Record discovery calls; store learnings as product seeds.
**Trade-offs**: build automation, not tutorials — optimize for time, not Zap complexity.

## The Automation Decision Rule
**When**: deciding what to automate/delegate.
**How**: set an aspirational rate ($100/hr). If a recurring task costs an hour but adds <$100 value, automate (preferred) or delegate. Anything routine >10-15 min/day → automate if possible.
**Trade-offs**: setup time up front for compounding time savings.

## F.I.T.I (climbing service levels, raising rates)
**When**: moving from low-cost (L1) to high-price micro-niche (L3).
**How**: Feedback (customers + what you enjoy) → Iteration (the love × need crossroad; cut the rest) → Testimonials (ruthlessly, guided to a result) → Increase (raise price as demand rises; double price / half win-rate is fine). Push lower-value clients to products.
**Trade-offs**: niching down shrinks your market but multiplies your rate (Welsh: $250 → $2,500/hr).

## Move Fast & Cheap + Four-Pass Process
**When**: building a first digital product.
**How**: narrow to one specific problem you know cold; time-box to 2 weeks; outline (sections → chapters, 1-2 outcomes) in Sheets; Four-Pass: brain-dump → notes+bullet summaries → media → resources. Get honest feedback throughout. Low production, short memorable name, Canva/Fiverr cover.
**Trade-offs**: narrow scope feels limiting but is more actionable than broad.

## The 14-Day Pre-Sell Launch
**When**: launching a product.
**How**: Day 1 set up + announce intentions (discount: e.g. $99 → $149); Days 2-9 run the Four-Pass during the window, day-8 teaser; Days 10-13 friend review, screencasts, write-up, gather + post testimonials, full buyer-flow check; Day 14 blitz social + distribution + email, share testimonials, last-day urgency; post-launch keep collecting testimonials and raise the price.
**Trade-offs**: intense two-week sprint (build + market simultaneously); pre-sell de-risks and funds the build.
