# Chapter 5: Creating Content (Idea Systems, Stories & Formats)

## Core Idea
The hardest part of posting daily is a *consistent stream of ideas*. Solve it with a capture system, then turn raw ideas into great **stories** (the engine of virality) and rotate non-story **content formats** so the feed stays fresh. Welsh posted Monday–Friday for a year on this system.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 5 Content-Idea Sources** (the capture pipeline):
  1. **Your experience** — log daily work wins/losses/lessons in a phone notes app folder.
  2. **Pocket** — Chrome extension to save articles you encounter for later reaction/reframe.
  3. **Feedly** — RSS reader that aggregates new blogs/podcasts from sources you respect (discovery of *new* ideas).
  4. **HubSpot Blog Idea Generator** — punch in keywords → titles/topics; spin a long-form blog into many micro-posts (the **Gary Vaynerchuk content model**).
  5. **Twitter + Screenshot** (Welsh's #1) — a curated, niche-only Twitter feed; screenshot good ideas, annotate, save to a G-Drive content folder.
- **The 5 Viral-Story Traits** — a story is more likely to spread when it's **Inspirational, Believable, Authentic, Relatable, Polarizing**. (Never write *crazy*; never write *vanilla* — **polarizing lives between them**, aiming for ~60–70% agree / 30–40% disagree.)
- **The 3 Parts of a Story — Hook, Format, Feel**:
  - **Hook** — the last line before "see more." On a *text* post you get **5 lines** (write 3 short lines + a blank line between → the hook lands on line 5); on *image/video* posts you get far less, so the hook lands around line 3. It must compel the click.
  - **Format = White Space + Wave** — a blank line between every line (white space = easier to read = more completions), and sentence lengths that rise and fall like a wave (long→short→long→short).
  - **Feel** — either **(1) polarizing** or **(2) an authentic story of overcoming adversity** (Welsh's "fired 3x by 28, never hit quota for 6 years" post: 1.246M views, 14,380 interactions, 840 comments — his most popular ever).
- **The Other-Content Format Menu** (rotate beyond stories):
  - **Checklists with action steps** (e.g., Gitano DiNardi's 12-point no-fluff checklist).
  - **Clickbait line + strong CTA** (Adi Suja: "most badass experiment in LinkedIn history" + a CTA inviting a specific response).
  - **Recognition-based listicles** (Brenda Mellor's weekly LinkedIn-trainer top-25 — tagged winners share it; built anticipation).
  - **Topical / newsworthy content** (Jamie Willis on the sub-2-hour marathon — react the day it happens).
  - **Vulnerability & mistakes** (Welsh's "perfect day vs. real day" post — relatable, authentic).
  - **Free resources** (Ashley Galina Dudarenak: bright image + free e-book linked in the first comment).

## Key Concepts
- **Don't write for virality — write a great story; virality follows.** Welsh never posts *intending* to go viral.
- **Save vs. discover vs. live-capture** — Pocket saves what you find, Feedly discovers what you'd miss, Twitter/notes capture in real time; together they guarantee a backlog.
- **Earn the right to your best story** — Welsh waited ~a year of writing before publishing his personal adversity story, so it would land. Don't burn your best material while you're still bad at writing.
- **The content calendar** — top LinkedIn creators assign formats to days (Mon checklist, Tue motivational story, Wed video, Thu free resource) and test what gets the most interaction.

## Mental Models
- **Ideas are a stock, not a flow** — build a reservoir (G-Drive/Pocket/Feedly) so creation day is selection, not invention.
- **Reading completion is engineered** — white space and wave aren't decoration; they're a psychology trick to get the brain through the whole post.
- **Polarizing = tribe-forming** — the people who agree become your tribe and feel the dissenters are wrong; agreement-only content forms no tribe.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to invent ideas on posting day** instead of drawing from a captured backlog.
- **Vanilla, middle-of-the-road content** (no opinion) — and its opposite, *crazy* content.
- **Long unbroken paragraphs** — they look complicated and kill read-through.
- **Hook on the wrong line** — runs past the visible cutoff and never earns the "see more" click.
- **Telling your best personal story too early**, before your writing can do it justice.
- **Never varying format** — only stories gets stale and hard to keep fresh.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run the 5-source capture system so you always have a backlog of ideas.
2. Make stories Inspirational/Believable/Authentic/Relatable/Polarizing; build them as Hook → Format (white space + wave) → Feel.
3. Engineer the hook to land on the visible line (5 for text, ~3 for image/video) and write in white space + wave.
4. Rotate the format menu on a content calendar; test what your audience rewards.
