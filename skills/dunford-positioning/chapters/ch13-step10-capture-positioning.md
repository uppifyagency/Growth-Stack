# Chapter 13: Step 10 — Capture Your Positioning so It Can Be Shared

## Core Idea
Positioning is useless until it's shared and adopted. Capture it in a document and a one-page canvas the whole company can use to drive branding, marketing, sales, product, and customer success.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Positioning Canvas** (one-page summary):
  - Product name + one-line description
  - Market category (and subcategory)
  - Four aligned columns:
    1. **Competitive alternatives** — what customers would use if you didn't exist
    2. **Unique attributes** — features/capabilities you have that alternatives don't
    3. **Value** — what those attributes enable for customers
    4. **Who cares a lot** — characteristics of customers who value it most
- **Positioning document**: longer than the canvas — breaks down each component with enough detail for marketing, sales, and product to use, and shows how components interact.

## Key Concepts
- **Why not the positioning statement**: too brief to carry the subtleties, yet too contrived to memorize or repeat. The canvas + document replace it.
- **Two artifacts**: a detailed document (component-by-component) and a concise one-page version (the canvas) for easy sharing.

## Mental Models
- The canvas lines up the four components so the *relationships* between them are visible at a glance — the through-line from alternatives → attributes → value → who cares.

## Anti-patterns
- **Leaving positioning in people's heads** after the workshop — it must be written and circulated.
- **Relying on a positioning statement** as the shareable artifact.

## Key Takeaways
1. Produce both a detailed positioning document and a one-page canvas.
2. Positioning needs company-wide buy-in to inform branding, campaigns, sales strategy, product decisions, and customer success.
3. The canvas is the quick-reference; the document carries the nuance.
