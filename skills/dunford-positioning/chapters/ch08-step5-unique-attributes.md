# Chapter 8: Step 5 — Isolate Your Unique Attributes or Features

## Core Idea
Once you know the true alternatives, list everything you can do that they can't. Stay on *features/capabilities* here — value comes next.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **List all unique attributes**: capabilities/features you have that the alternatives lack. Be broad — include technical features, delivery model, business model, expertise, partnerships, proprietary processes, special skills. List even things that *seem* negative or whose value is unclear.
- **Consideration vs. retention attributes**:
  - **Consideration attributes** — what customers weigh when *deciding to buy*. Focus here.
  - **Retention attributes** — matter at *renewal* (ease of doing business, support quality). Important, but a customer who never sees enough value to buy never experiences them.

## Key Concepts
- **Feature**: something your product or company *has or does* ("15-megapixel camera," "integrates with QuickBooks," "one-click installation," "metal construction").
- **Secret sauce**: the capabilities you have that alternatives don't.
- **Service-business attributes**: usually a combination of expertise + experience (e.g. "the only UX agency with enterprise training-design experience").

## Mental Models
- **"Your opinion of your own strengths is irrelevant without proof."** "Outstanding customer service" and "easy to use" are usually *values*, not features. Ask: what concrete, provable feature drives that? (More support staff? Certifications? No training required? Quantifiable time-to-proficiency?)
- Third-party validation = proof (independent reviewer, analyst, approved customer quote).

## Anti-patterns
- **Listing benefits/values as if they were features** ("we provide outstanding customer service").
- **Pre-filtering for importance**: capture the broad set now; group and prune in Step 6.
- **Ignoring "negative-seeming" attributes**: one team's "requires on-site install" was a *differentiator* for customers who wanted high-touch customization.

## Key Takeaways
1. Stay at the feature level; resist jumping to value.
2. Concentrate on consideration attributes — features tied to a goal the customer wants to accomplish *now*.
3. List broadly and creatively; prove each strength or it doesn't count.
