# Chapter 6: Step 3 — Align Your Vocabulary and Let Go of Your Positioning Baggage

## Core Idea
To imagine a new position, the team must share a common vocabulary and consciously set aside the old way of thinking about the product ("positioning baggage").

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Align positioning vocabulary first**: spend the first hour agreeing on what positioning means, the components and their definitions, and how market maturity/competitive landscape shapes the style — so you don't spend the day arguing about what positioning even is.
- **Let go of positioning baggage**: the history baked into how you describe the product. Customers carry none of it — they know nothing of the product's history when they first encounter it. Market confusion starts in the gap between how creators see the product and how customers first perceive it.

## Key Concepts
- **Positioning baggage**: assumptions about market, terminology, and feature descriptions inherited from the product's conception/launch.
- **Uneven baggage**: founders and long-time employees carry the most; newer employees carry less — surface this difference.
- **The reality**: most products can be many things to many types of buyers.

## Mental Models
- **Arm & Hammer**: baking soda (1846) → flatlining sales in the 1970s → repositioned as a fridge *deodorizer* → $16M (1969) to $318M (1987). The starting point of growth was willingness to let go of the old frame.
- **Clearpath Robotics → OTTO Motors**: "a robot" forced comparison to stationary manufacturing robots. Reframed as "self-driving vehicles for industrial use" (added headlights/taillights, car-maker-style website) → sales accelerated, raised $30M, won an Edison Award.

## Anti-patterns
- **Arguing about what positioning is** during the working session instead of pre-aligning vocabulary.
- **Defending the original frame** because "that's what we set out to build" — name where your history shows up in the current positioning.

## Key Takeaways
1. The most important outcome of this step: team agreement that the product may no longer be best positioned the way it was conceived.
2. Get the team to *suspend* their positioning opinions for the duration of the exercise so new ideas can surface.
3. Call out explicitly where product history is leaking into the current position.
