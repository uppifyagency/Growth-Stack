# Chapter 5: Step 2 — Form a Positioning Team

## Core Idea
Positioning is a business-strategy exercise that touches every function. It must be a cross-functional team effort led by the business leader — not owned by marketing.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Business-leader-led, cross-functional team**: the person who owns the business strategy (CEO/founder in startups; division/BU leader in larger companies) must drive it, or it won't be adopted.
- **Why marketing can't "own" positioning**: it's too broad and too important to live in one silo — just as marketing can't own overall business strategy.

## Key Concepts
- **What flows from positioning** (why each function must be in the room):
  - Marketing → messaging, audience targeting, campaigns
  - Sales/BD → target segmentation, account strategy
  - Customer success → onboarding, account expansion
  - Product/dev → roadmap, prioritization
- **Team size**: one or two senior people per function. Works with as few as 3, as many as 12. Too many people → some groups dominate.
- **Outside facilitator**: strongly recommended — keeps every voice heard and can *gently challenge* long-held assumptions that seem untouchable.

## Mental Models
- Different functions interact with customers at different points in the buyer journey, so they hold genuinely different views on alternatives, unique features, and best-fit traits. Surface and reconcile those views.
- "You want engagement from every group because you need buy-in from every group."

## Anti-patterns
- **Letting marketing run positioning alone** — it will fail without business-leader ownership and cross-functional buy-in.
- **Overstuffing the room** — dilutes each function's voice and lets one or two groups dominate.
- **Skipping product/dev** — they hold the truth on what is and isn't possible to build.

## Key Takeaways
1. A positioning exercise that isn't a team effort driven by the business leader will fail.
2. Every leader who must *execute* the positioning should help *develop* it.
3. Bring in an experienced outside facilitator for balance and to challenge assumptions.
