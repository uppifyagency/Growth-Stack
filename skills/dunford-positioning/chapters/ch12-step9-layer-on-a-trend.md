# Chapter 12: Step 9 — Layer On a Trend (but Be Careful)

## Core Idea
A relevant trend can make your offering feel important *right now* — but only if it sits at the intersection of your solution, your market context, and the trend. Optional, and easy to misuse.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Trends Venn diagram**: three overlapping circles — **Your Solution**, **Market Context**, **Trends**. Aim for the center. Miss it and you land in failure zones:
  - Trend + Solution, no market context → **Cool but Confusing**
  - Trend + Market, no solution link → **Lies / hype with no substance**
  - Solution + Market, no trend → **Good but Boring** (still fine!)
- **Trend as relevance amplifier, not category**: a trend tells buyers why to care *now*; the market category tells them *what you are*. Always declare the category first.

## Key Concepts
- **Relevant trend**: a new, noteworthy characteristic your target customers care about, with a clear link to your product (e.g. Redgate's "database DevOps").
- **Boring ≠ broken**: a well-positioned product can succeed with no trend at all. Trends just add gloss.

## Mental Models
- **"It's better to be a little boring than completely baffling."** Don't layer a trend for trendiness.
- Redgate wove "database DevOps" into well-established database-tools positioning → multi-product purchases up, **+100% inbound leads**. The trend reinforced (didn't replace) the category.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trend without a market category** → "the sharing economy for pets" / "Uber for cats" (cool but baffling).
- **Trend without a product link** → Long Island Iced Tea → "Long Blockchain": stock spiked, no real strategy, delisted from Nasdaq.
- **Getting carried away with content about the trend** while forgetting you sell an actual product.

## Key Takeaways
1. Position in the right market category first; *then* look for a trend that reinforces it.
2. Use a trend only when it has a clear link to both your product and your market.
3. If no trend fits, don't force one — successful-and-boring beats fashionable-and-bewildering.
