# Chapter 1: Introduction — Why Positioning Matters

## Core Idea
Positioning is the foundational input to every marketing and sales tactic. Get it wrong and everything downstream gets harder; get it right and the wind is at your back.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Positioning, defined**: "The act of deliberately defining how you are the best at something that a defined market cares a lot about."
  - When to use: as the starting point before any campaign, content, pitch, or pricing decision.
  - How: deliberately choose context rather than accepting the default.
- **Signs of weak positioning** (the trail it leaves): 
  1. Current customers love you, but new prospects can't figure out what you're selling.
  2. Long sales cycles and low close rates; you lose at the last minute.
  3. High churn shortly after purchase (customers bought for the wrong reasons).
  4. You're under price pressure (you look like everything else).

## Key Concepts
- **Context-setting**: positioning works like the opening scene of a movie — it answers where/what/why/who fast.
- **Positioning baggage**: history baked into how you describe the product.
- **Input vs. foundation**: positioning is both — none of the "new cool stuff" (ABM, growth hacking, content) works without it.

## Mental Models
- Think of weak positioning as **a wind in your face** — perfect technique, but nobody wants the omelet made from rotten eggs.
- Adding marketing budget to confusing positioning is like **speaking your language slowly and loudly** to someone who doesn't speak it. Volume doesn't fix comprehension.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating a comprehension problem as a sales/marketing problem**: if more marketing isn't producing more sales and reps work hard just to explain what you are, it's positioning.
- **Assuming positioning is "old stuff"**: the more crowded the market (2M+ apps, 580M+ products on Amazon), the more decisive it is.

## Key Takeaways
1. If you fail at positioning, you fail at marketing and sales — and the business fails.
2. Many people know *what* positioning is; almost no one knows *how* to do it. This book is a process for the *how*.
3. Customers must easily understand what your product is, why it's special, and why it matters to them.
4. Positioning is a deliberate business choice, not fate or luck.
