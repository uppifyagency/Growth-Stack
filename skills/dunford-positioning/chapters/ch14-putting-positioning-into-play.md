# Chapter 14: Putting Positioning Into Play — Sales Story, Messaging, Roadmap, Tracking

## Core Idea
"OK, now what?" After the 10 steps, make positioning real: build a sales story first, then messaging, then align roadmap/pricing — and re-check positioning over time.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Sales Story arc** (build this *before* messaging, especially for complex B2B sales):
  1. **Problem** — define the problem your solution solves (frames the discussion).
  2. **How customers solve it today + where that falls short.**
  3. **The "perfect world"** — features an ideal solution would have, given the problem and current shortfalls.
  4. **Introduce the product + position it in its market category.**
  5. **Value themes** — each, with proof, objection handling, case study/customer list.
  6. **Call to action** — what you want the prospect to do next.
- **Messaging document**: one master baseline so messaging (and positioning) doesn't "creep" as copy is built on modified copy in a chain. Don't craft messaging live with a group.

## Key Concepts
- **Roadmap follows positioning**: an "accounting software" roadmap differs from a "financial services" roadmap. Great positioning is reinforced over time with feature adjustments.
- **Pricing follows category**: align to the price expectations of the chosen category (raise prices for "CRM for investment banks"; a data warehouse commands more than a commodity database).
- **Tracking cadence**: re-check positioning every ~6 months, or after any major event affecting the competitive landscape or how customers evaluate solutions.

## Mental Models
- A shift in positioning feels less like a marketing change and more like a **business-strategy shift** — every department is eventually impacted.
- The sales-story arc gets the team to agree on the problem, current solutions, the gap, and the key purchase criteria — that alignment *is* the deliverable.

## Anti-patterns
- **Jumping to messaging before a sales story** for complex sales.
- **Letting messaging creep** without a master document.
- **Assuming a new competitor means you must reposition** — only reposition if customers' perceptions/criteria actually shift (start again at Step 4).

## Key Takeaways
1. Sales story before messaging; messaging from a single baseline document.
2. Forces that demand a re-check: a credible new competitor, regulation, economic shifts, new enabling technology, or shifting customer attitudes (even "cool purple lights" in a data center).
3. Positioning is never "done" — products and markets change; revisit deliberately.
