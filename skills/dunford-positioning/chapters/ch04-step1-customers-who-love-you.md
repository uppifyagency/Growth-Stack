# Chapter 4: Step 1 — Understand the Customers Who Love Your Product

## Core Idea
The components depend on each other in a seemingly circular way. Break the circle by starting with your happiest customers — they reveal your real value and best market.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Start with best-fit customers**: make a short list of customers who understood you quickly, bought fast, became raving fans, refer you, and act as references. Use this list as the reference point for the whole exercise.
- **Survey the ecstatic, not the average**: surveying *all* customers gives muddy results. Filter to only the ecstatic fans and a clear pattern of "why" emerges.

## Key Concepts
- **Best-fit customer**: bought quickly, loves (not just likes) the product, would fight to keep it, may say you should charge *more*.
- **Customer-facing vs. investor-facing positioning**: customers buy a solution to a problem they have *now*; investors buy your 5–10 year vision. Your website, sales materials, pricing, and near-term roadmap reflect *customer* positioning, not investor positioning.
- **Product vs. company positioning**: single-product companies should position product = company (fewer things to remember). Multi-product companies need both: product positioning (why buy this) + company positioning (why buy multiple).

## Mental Models
- **The fishing-net model**: if you haven't fished yet, cast a broad "fish net" to see what you catch. If you keep pulling tuna, move to the tuna spot. Positioning a tuna net as a "grouper net" too early hides your best market.
- "Find out who you are and do it on purpose" (Dolly Parton).

## Anti-patterns
- **Starting from the product and its special features**: you'll unconsciously compare to competitors *you* worry about, not the alternatives customers actually weigh.
- **Tightening positioning before you have enough happy customers**: keep positioning loose until patterns emerge.
- **Positioning for investors on customer-facing surfaces**: "disruption" excites investors but can terrify customers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Your best-fit customers hold the key to what your product *is*.
2. Concentrate growth on prospects who look like your ecstatic fans — they're easiest to sell and retain.
3. For a multi-product company, position the first/most-revenue product first unless products are mainly sold as a bundle.
