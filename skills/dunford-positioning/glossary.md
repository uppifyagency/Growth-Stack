# Glossary — Obviously Awesome (April Dunford)

**Abductive reasoning** — Choosing a market category by asking "what products typically have these features / deliver this value?" ("looks like a duck…") (Ch 11).

**Best-fit customer / prospect** — A customer who understands you fast, buys quickly, rarely discounts, doesn't churn, refers you, may say you should charge more. The seed of your target segment (Ch 4, Ch 10).

**Big Fish, Small Pond** — Positioning style: dominate a subsegment of an existing market with distinct unmet needs the leader ignores (Ch 11).

**Competitive alternatives** — What target customers would use or do if your product didn't exist; often a spreadsheet, an intern, or "do nothing." Component #1 (Ch 3, Ch 7).

**Consideration attributes** — Features customers weigh when deciding to buy (vs. retention attributes). Focus positioning here (Ch 8).

**Context-setting** — What positioning *is*: the clues that tell customers what you are, who it's for, and why it matters (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Create a New Game** — Positioning style: create a new market category; sell the problem first. Hardest and most expensive (Ch 11).

**Default positioning** — The unconscious frame adopted the moment you name the product; silently sets buyers, competitors, pricing, roadmap (Ch 2).

**Feature → Benefit → Value** — Feature (what you have) → benefit (what it enables) → value (how it maps to a customer goal). Always reach value (Ch 9).

**Five (Plus One) Components** — Competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value (+ proof), target market characteristics, market category, (+ relevant trends). Worked in dependency order (Ch 3).

**Frame of reference** — The market context through which customers perceive your product; products are exceptional only in their best frame (Ch 2).

**Head to Head** — Positioning style: win an existing market by beating the leader at their own game (Ch 11).

**Market category** — The frame you declare yourself part of; triggers assumptions about competitors, features, pricing. Component #5 (Ch 3, Ch 11).

**Messaging document** — A master baseline that prevents messaging (and positioning) creep (Ch 14).

**Positioning** — "The act of deliberately defining how you are the best at something that a defined market cares a lot about" (Ch 1).

**Positioning baggage** — History baked into how you describe the product; customers carry none of it (Ch 6).

**Positioning canvas** — One-page artifact: name + description, market category, and four columns (alternatives, attributes, value, who cares) (Ch 13).

**Positioning statement** — Traditional FOR/IS A/WHICH PROVIDES/UNLIKE template. Dunford says never use it for *doing* positioning (Ch 3).

**Positioning style** — How you intend to win a market: Head to Head, Big Fish Small Pond, or Create a New Game (Ch 11).

**Proof** — Provable value via data, third-party reviewers/analysts, or approved customer quotes; your opinion isn't proof (Ch 3, Ch 8, Ch 9).

**Relevant trend** — A new, noteworthy characteristic with a clear link to your product; makes you matter *now*. Bonus component (Ch 3, Ch 12).

**Retention attributes** — Features that matter at renewal (support quality, ease of doing business) (Ch 8).

**Sales story** — Problem → today's shortfall → perfect world → product + category → value themes → CTA. Built before messaging (Ch 14).

**Segmentation (actionable)** — Identifiable characteristics that make buyers care a lot; goes beyond demographics/firmographics (Ch 10).

**Subsegmenting** — Splitting an existing market (industry, geography, size, ecosystem) to find a winnable piece (Ch 11).

**Target market characteristics** — Identifiable traits of buyers who care a lot about your value. Component #4 (Ch 3, Ch 10).

**Trends Venn** — Intersection of Your Solution × Market Context × Trends; aim for the center (Ch 12).

**Two Traps** — Trap 1: stuck on what you intended to build. Trap 2: the market changed around you (Ch 2).

**Unique attributes** — Features/capabilities you have that the alternatives lack ("secret sauce"). Component #2 (Ch 3, Ch 8).

**Value (and proof)** — The benefit attributes enable, mapped to a customer goal, made provable. Component #3 (Ch 3, Ch 9).
