# Patterns & Techniques — Obviously Awesome

## The 10-Step Positioning Process (the master method)
**When to use**: any time a product is hard for customers to "get," sales cycles drag, churn is high, or you're under price pressure.
**How**:
1. **Understand the customers who love your product** — list ecstatic fans; survey only them.
2. **Form a positioning team** — cross-functional, business-leader-led, outside facilitator.
3. **Align vocabulary & let go of positioning baggage** — agree on terms; suspend old assumptions.
4. **List true competitive alternatives** — "what would best customers do if you didn't exist?"; rank + group (2–5 groups).
5. **Isolate unique attributes** — list everything alternatives lack; stay at feature level; consideration over retention.
6. **Map attributes to value themes** — Feature→Benefit→Value; cluster to 1–4 themes.
7. **Determine who cares a lot** — actionable segment; target as narrowly as needed to hit near-term goals.
8. **Find a market frame of reference + pick a style** — make value obvious; choose Head to Head / Big Fish / New Game.
9. **Layer on a trend (optional)** — only if it links to product *and* market.
10. **Capture positioning so it can be shared** — positioning document + one-page canvas.
**Trade-offs**: thorough and team-intensive (½–2 days); the payoff is company-wide alignment.

## The Positioning Canvas
**When to use**: to capture and share the final positioning on one page.
**How**: Product name + one-liner → Market category (+ subcategory) → four columns: Competitive alternatives | Unique attributes | Value | Who cares a lot.
**Trade-offs**: concise but loses nuance — pair with a fuller positioning document.

## The Feature → Benefit → Value translation
**When to use**: Step 6, and whenever a claim sounds like "so what?"
**How**: state the feature → name the benefit it enables → tie the benefit to a goal the customer is trying to achieve. Make value provable.
**Trade-offs**: forces discipline; technical teams resist because they auto-translate in their heads.

## Choosing a Positioning Style (Step 8)
**When to use**: after you've picked a candidate market frame.
**How**:
- **Head to Head** → you're the leader, or category exists with no clear leader; you can win on current criteria.
- **Big Fish, Small Pond** → small/new company; find a subsegment (identifiable, big-enough, real unmet need) the leader underserves.
- **Create a New Game** → no existing category fits; strong "why now?"; resources to teach the market.
**Trade-offs**: Head to Head needs scale/funding; New Game is slow and risks losing leadership to a fast follower; Big Fish is the safest entry and can expand later.

## The Sales Story arc
**When to use**: operationalizing positioning for complex B2B sales, *before* messaging.
**How**: Problem → how solved today + shortfall → "perfect world" features → introduce product + category → value themes (with proof, objections, case study) → CTA.
**Trade-offs**: aligns the team on problem/gap/criteria; not final copy — it's the skeleton for the deck.

## "Survey the ecstatic, not the average" (Step 1)
**When to use**: when overall customer surveys produce muddy "why they bought" patterns.
**How**: filter to only your happiest customers; a clear pattern of value + best-fit traits emerges.
**Trade-offs**: needs enough happy customers to see a pattern; until then keep positioning loose ("fishing-net" approach).

## Reframe to escape a trap (Steps 3 & 8)
**When to use**: product became something else (Trap 1) or the market moved (Trap 2).
**How**: name your positioning baggage → list real alternatives → find a frame that centers your strengths → adjust category (and roadmap/pricing to match). Examples: database→data warehouse; robot→self-driving industrial vehicle; baking soda→fridge deodorizer.
**Trade-offs**: repositioning can feel risky internally; expect investor/team pushback when narrowing.

## Layering a trend safely (Step 9)
**When to use**: to make a well-positioned product feel urgent/relevant now.
**How**: declare market category first → add a trend only at the intersection of Solution × Market × Trend (e.g. "database DevOps").
**Trade-offs**: misused trends → "cool but confusing," "hype lies," or hype-without-substance (Long Blockchain). Boring-but-clear beats baffling.

## Tracking positioning over time
**When to use**: ongoing — every ~6 months or after a major market event.
**How**: re-check whether competitive alternatives shifted in best-fit customers' minds (restart at Step 4). Triggers: credible new competitor, regulation, economic shift, new enabling tech, shifting buyer attitudes.
**Trade-offs**: a new competitor doesn't always require repositioning — only if customer perceptions/criteria actually change.
