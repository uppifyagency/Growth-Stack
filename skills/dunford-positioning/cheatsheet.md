# Cheatsheet — Obviously Awesome (April Dunford)

## Positioning in one line
> The act of deliberately defining **how you are the best at something that a defined market cares a lot about.**

## The 5 (+1) Components — in order
1. Competitive alternatives → what they'd use if you didn't exist
2. Unique attributes → what you have that alternatives lack
3. Value (+ proof) → benefit mapped to a customer goal
4. Target market characteristics → who cares a lot
5. Market category → the frame that makes value obvious
6. (+) Relevant trends → why you matter *now* (optional)

## The 10 Steps (quick)
1 Customers who love you · 2 Positioning team · 3 Vocabulary + drop baggage · 4 Competitive alternatives · 5 Unique attributes · 6 Map to value themes · 7 Who cares a lot · 8 Market frame + style · 9 Layer a trend · 10 Capture & share

## The 3 Positioning Styles — decision guide
| Style | Use when | Goal | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Head to Head** | You're the leader, OR category exists with no clear leader | Beat the leader on current criteria | Suicidal for small/new co. ("out-cola Coke") |
| **Big Fish, Small Pond** | Small/new co.; leader underserves a subsegment | Dominate a niche, then expand | Leader copies "good enough"; segment too small |
| **Create a New Game** | No existing category fits; strong "why now?"; deep pockets | Define + lead a new category | Slow; fast-follower steals leadership |

## Subsegment must be…
✅ Identifiable (you can build a prospect list) ✅ Big enough to hit sales goals ✅ Has a specific, important **unmet need**

## Feature → Benefit → Value
"15MP camera" → "sharp images" → "zoom/print large and stay sharp." If reaction is *"so what?"*, you stopped at the feature.

## The Sales Story arc
Problem → how solved today + shortfall → "perfect world" → product + category → value themes (proof) → CTA. **Build before messaging.**

## Signs of weak positioning
- New prospects can't tell what you sell (current customers love you)
- Long sales cycles, low close rates, last-minute losses
- High early churn (bought for wrong reasons)
- Price pressure (you look like everything else)

## The Two Traps
- **Trap 1**: stuck on what you *intended* to build (product became something else)
- **Trap 2**: market moved (your "diet muffin" is now a "gluten-free paleo snack")

## Hard rules
- ❌ Never write a positioning statement to *do* positioning
- ✅ Start from best-fit customers, not the product
- ✅ Value needs **proof** (data / analysts / customer quotes) — your opinion ≠ proof
- ✅ Target as narrowly as you can to hit near-term goals; broaden later
- ✅ Business-leader-led + cross-functional; marketing can't "own" it
- ✅ Trend only if linked to product *and* market — **boring beats baffling**
- 🔁 Re-check positioning every ~6 months or after a major market event

## Capture artifacts
**Positioning canvas** (1 page): name+one-liner · market category · [alternatives | attributes | value | who cares]. Plus a fuller **positioning document** for nuance.

## 5 takeaways
1 Any product can be positioned in multiple markets · 2 Great positioning rarely comes by default · 3 Best-fit customers' real alternatives reveal your differentiators · 4 Pick the market that makes your strengths obvious · 5 Use trends cautiously — successful-and-boring > fashionable-and-bewildering
