## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The thesis.** The internet *passively documents* what billions of people actually want, do, and buy — so you don't have to guess. ~99% of people don't know this demand data exists or how to read it; learning to is a durable GTM edge. Adopt **"Internet Glasses"**: see every page as human-intent signal, not just content. The goal is a *rod, not a fish* — the method, not a list of ideas.

**The Station model = a GTM funnel.** Run ideas through this pipeline (enter/skip/revisit any stop):
1. **Preparation** — instrument your browser (bookmarks, extensions, shortcuts) so you notice opportunity *passively*. The "automatic win."
2. **Generation** ("the Generator") — produce *many* ideas via 12 methods. Rule: **no bad ideas yet** — collect, don't judge.
3. **Validation** — filter against the biggest websites; **validate or invalidate** demand.
4. **Evaluation** — magnifying glass on existing competitors.
5. **Differentiation** — find your wedge via deep audience understanding.
   (+ **Visualization** and **Recommendation** as amplifiers.)

**Hierarchy of Information** (the decision spine): trust **Sales > Search > Survey**. Behavior beats stated preference. "What people *do* > what they *say*."

**The 12 Generation methods** (frames for spotting ideas): Exploration (tech progress / Gartner hype cycle / Sci-Fi Idea Bank), Affirmation (already-upvoted, pre-vetted), Location (geo-arbitrage of trends), Variation (seasonality / aspirational self), Authorization (patents/IP/open APIs — "API = opportunity"), Regulation (new laws reshape markets), + 6 written-only (Monetization, Association, Innovation, Valuation, Compensation, Reflection).

**Signature workflow — tool-chaining.** From any starting point: size & growth (Subreddit Stats / Gummy Search) → problems & language (Gummy Search / GigaBrain) → adjacent audiences (anvaka "say it") → real questions (Answer the Public) → demand & datasets (Keywords Everywhere, Google Trends, Google Dataset Search) → tell the story (Flourish).

**Competitor reverse-engineering — the 8 questions**: how it's built (BuiltWith, Import Yeti) · what they prioritize (above-fold, nav, pricing) · traffic (SimilarWeb, FB Ad Library — long-running ad = winner) · direct+indirect competitors (Answer the Public) · metrics/revenue (Jungle Scout, AppMagic, Koala) · messaging (Owletter, SparkToro) · reputation via the **2–3 star review rule** (5★=fans, 1★=angry, **2–3★=actionable gaps**).

**Differentiation.** Every great company differentiates on one clear axis — Apple=design, Amazon=speed, Costco=cost, RockAuto=variety — *matched to what the audience values*. Start from audience empathy and their exact lingo (creator ≠ influencer ≠ celebrity).

**Operating maxims**: Follow the surprise (surprise = research prompt). Niche/silly ≠ small (r/TVTooHigh = 180K). Cost is the productivity trigger for re-emerging tech. Curate your content diet (purge before adding).

**Execution discipline (when *applying*, not just referencing).** The method fails not from missing knowledge but from shortcuts narrated as coverage. Non-negotiables: Generation runs *before* Validation; seeds must be **data-derived** (your own hunches are Survey-tier — tag them, keep them a minority, validate them, never trust them); attempt the highest tier (**Sales**) early and headline the tier you *actually* reached; confirm every trend on **≥2 independent sources** and disclose data granularity (Google's ~+22% buckets, noisy single-month YoY); evaluate **≥3 competitors** across the 8 questions; **localize** community mining (Reddit is thin outside the US); and keep a **coverage ledger** — never say "complete / E2E / all stations" unless every station is `EXECUTED` *with an artifact*. Mapping a station after the fact ≠ executing it. **Full protocol with guardrails G0–G6 → [runbook.md](runbook.md).**
