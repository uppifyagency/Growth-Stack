# Chapter 2: Let's Start with a Silly Question

## Core Idea
A deliberately silly example (r/TVTooHigh, 180K members) proves two things: the internet is far bigger than you'd expect, and "silly/niche" communities can still teach you practical, monetizable lessons if you follow the rabbit hole.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Follow the surprise**: when something seems surprisingly big or surprisingly niche, that reaction is a signal — go deeper instead of dismissing it.
  - When to use: any time curiosity or surprise spikes while browsing.
  - How: take the niche term → explore related keywords, tools, and adjacent communities → look for real demand/traffic behind it.
- **Niche → demand chain**: a silly subreddit → the keywords its members search → real products serving that intent (e.g. "TV too high" → DisplayWars.com, a 2007-era screen-size tool getting ~1M views/quarter).

## Key Concepts
- **Top X% ranking**: on Reddit you can click a subreddit's rank to see same-size communities — a discovery surface for adjacent niches.
- **Audience overlap / connected subreddits**: tools reveal "if someone posts here, where else do they post?" (Apple TV → cord cutters → YouTube TV timeline).
- **Longevity signal**: r/cordcutters had tens of thousands of members a decade ago — community age/persistence is itself signal.

## Mental Models
- In the analog world, "a TV mounted too high" is an anecdote (n=1). On the internet, cumulative interaction turns it into a measurable "problem" with 180K people.
- Niche communities are systematically *overlooked because they look silly* — that's exactly why they're underexploited.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring something because it's "too niche" or "too silly"**: that dismissal is why the opportunity is still open.
- **Stopping at subreddit size**: size shows attention; pair it with tools (subreddit stats, connection maps) to learn growth, posts, and problems.

## Key Takeaways
1. The internet's scale makes even absurd niches large and legible.
2. Surprise = a research prompt, not a punchline.
3. Practical opportunities (DisplayWars, screen-size.info ~100K views/mo) sit behind seemingly trivial intent.
