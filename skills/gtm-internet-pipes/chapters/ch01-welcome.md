# Chapter 1: Welcome to Internet Pipes

## Core Idea
The internet passively documents what billions of people actually want, do, and buy — so you no longer have to guess. Most people have no idea this data exists or how to use it. Learning to read it gives you a permanent edge for finding and improving business ideas.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Internet Glasses**: a learned way of seeing the web as a continuous stream of human intent signals (searches, posts, upvotes, purchases) rather than just content to consume.
  - When to use: always — it's the mental posture behind every Station.
  - How: when you encounter anything online, ask "what does this reveal about what people want, and at what scale?"
- **Give a rod, not a fish**: the course teaches *how to surface ideas yourself*, not a static list of ideas. Internalize the methods, not the examples.

## Key Concepts
- **Passive documentation**: the web records desires that used to live only inside individual brains or behind expensive focus groups.
- **SEO reframed**: not link-building / black-hat tactics, but "billions of people sharing their thoughts, desires, and intent" — a window into demand.
- **Scale**: per minute (Dec 2023) ≈ 6M Google searches, 300K tweets, 4M FB likes, $450K Amazon spend, 48K Twitch hours streamed.

## Mental Models
- Think of the pre-internet world as "limited by what you could see, smell, hear, taste, touch" — and the internet as removing those limits on scale, tracking, and bias.
- Successful people tend to be optimists *because* they've seen firsthand how much opportunity exists; exposure builds the belief.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming you need to be a born hustler**: Steph grew up "opportunistically poor," not an entrepreneur — the edge came from learning to read internet data, not innate talent.
- **Treating data as fodder only for new businesses**: it also improves existing businesses, upgrades skills, beats competitors, and improves your own content/life.

## Key Takeaways
1. The core thesis: pay attention to what people *do* online; it's demand data hiding in plain sight.
2. ~99% of people don't know this data exists or how to use it — that asymmetry is the opportunity.
3. The goal of the whole course is a reusable *skill of noticing*, not a list of ideas.
