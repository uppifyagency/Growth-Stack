# Chapter 12: Evaluation Station (Analyze the Competitors)

## Core Idea
Take a magnifying glass to the companies already in a validated space. There's almost always someone participating (if not, double-check demand actually exists). Reverse-engineer them through freely available data.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 8 competitor questions** — answer these about each player:
  1. How does the product operate / how is it built? → **BuiltWith**, Ghostery, Wappalyzer, **Import Yeti** (suppliers/shipments, free).
  2. What products/ideas are they prioritizing? → above-the-fold, nav bar, comparison pages, loss leaders, CTA (demo vs. list vs. direct sale).
  3. Is pricing public? → "no company unintentionally forgets pricing"; hidden price often = enterprise. Coupon extensions (Honey/Capital One Shopping) hint at acquisition strategy.
  4. How are they driving traffic? → **SimilarWeb** (volume + source mix), Facebook Ad Library (long-running ad = winning ad), Adbeat/Moat, Ahrefs (PPC).
  5. Who are their competitors (direct *and* indirect)? → SimilarWeb (≤5), **Answer the Public** (X-vs-Y queries surface indirect rivals, e.g. mushroom drink vs. coffee alt vs. AG1).
  6. How are they doing / key metrics? → revenue via **Jungle Scout** (Amazon), **Koala Inspector** (Shopify), **AppMagic**/Sensor Tower (apps); PitchBook/Crunchbase/CB Insights/Owler (funded).
  7. What are they saying to customers? → **Owletter** (their email campaigns), Google Alerts/F5Bot/Sifton, **SparkToro** (audience makeup, e.g. Hatch → mommy-blogger / giveaway community).
  8. What are people saying about them? → **2- and 3-star reviews** (the nuanced gold), Twitter/X dynamic search.

## Key Concepts
- **The 2–3 star review rule**: 5★ = happy/friends (no signal); 1★ = chronically angry (little signal); **2–3★ = "I wanted to love it, but…"** — the precise, actionable gap to exploit.
- **Long-running ads = proven performers** (competitors copy them; Steph saw this at The Hustle).

## Mental Models
- Reverse-engineering is cheap and legal — most of a competitor's strategy is observable from outside.
- Use your *own* reactions as data: if the exercise puts you off ("I don't want to run on Facebook ads"), that's a real signal about fit.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build a competitor table: product, competitors, traffic + sources (SimilarWeb), revenue (Jungle Scout/AppMagic), positioning.
2. 2–3 star reviews map directly into Differentiation (the next Station).
3. Notice when search volume exists but sales don't — a validation caveat worth heeding.
