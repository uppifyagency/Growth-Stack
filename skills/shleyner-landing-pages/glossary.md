# Glossary — Transformational Landing Pages (Eddie Shleyner)

**Art (Hero)** — the Hero's visual; helps The Visitor *visualize* what they get and reinforces the incentive (Ch 2).

**Big difference / Big test** — a contender page that looks/reads nothing like the control; the path to 2–3× lifts (Eugene Schwartz) (Ch 1, Ch 7).

**Body copy (Hero)** — the Hero element that answers "how?" the promise is delivered, in ~20–30 words (Ch 2).

**CDCA** — the four jobs of every landing page: achieve **Clarity**, intensify **Desire**, earn **Credibility**, compel **Action** (Ch 1).

**Clickbait (vs. fascination)** — clickbait fails to deliver on its promise; a fascination delivers — relevance to the right reader is the dividing line (Ch 3).

**Confidence signals** — logos, guarantees, statistics; quick credibility cues, each "saying" something to The Visitor (Ch 5).

**Congruence (Transitional)** — the Hero's copy/art/layout must match the ad that delivered the visitor; mismatch causes bounces (Ch 2).

**Contender** — the challenger page tested against the control; if it wins, it becomes the new control (Ch 7).

**Control** — the current/best-performing page you're trying to beat (Ch 1, Ch 7).

**Copyworking** — rewriting great copy by hand to internalize cadence and word choice (Gary Halbert) (Ch 3).

**Creative ad** — a "general" ad built to be remembered (delayed action), e.g. a Super Bowl spot; the opposite of direct-response (Ch 1).

**Curse of Knowledge** — being too close to your work to see how confusing it is to a newcomer; root cause of incongruence (Ch 2).

**Direct-response ad** — a "conversion" ad designed to compel immediate action; a landing page is one (Ch 1).

**Downsell offer** — a secondary low-commitment CTA (e.g. "read chapter 1 free") that captures fence-sitters' emails; can cannibalize sales (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Fascination** — a 1–3 sentence statement that promises a benefit while withholding the value, creating curiosity (Ch 3).

**Filter** — a formula/angle for framing a fascination (18 of them); also used for headlines, subheads, subject lines (Ch 4).

**FOMO** — fear of missing out; manufactured via urgent economic incentives to answer "why now?" (Ch 4 filter #18, Ch 6).

**Frankenstein persona** — a fictional composite audience profile ("Copywriter Jane") that reads contrived (Sean D'Souza) (Ch 2).

**Future Pacing** — making the reader mentally experience the post-purchase future; trigger words "imagine / picture this / stop / start" (Ch 2).

**Guarantee** — a confidence signal that transfers risk from buyer to seller; more risk transferred = more confidence (Ch 5, Ch 6).

**Headline (Hero)** — one "big, beautiful promise"; clear, not clever (Ch 2).

**Hero divider** — the first/most important divider (above the fold); 6 elements: Primer, Headline, Subhead, Body, CTA, Art; must "earn the scroll" (Ch 2).

**Incentive (moral / social / economic)** — the three drivers of action (Freakonomics/Levitt); content marketers mostly use economic (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**LISH — Length Implies Strength Heuristic** — volume of copy/proof signals substance; powers fascination walls and school testimonials (Ch 3, Ch 5).

**Mechanism** — a named, quoted "technology/system/process" that makes value feel unique (e.g. "SmartClimate") (Ch 4 filter #1).

**Mirroring** — repeating a subject's last words back as a question during an interview to keep them talking (Ch 2).

**Nugget** — a content excerpt holding both valuable info and the benefit of knowing it; raw material for a fascination (Ch 4).

**Person (sans "a")** — one real interviewed prospect ("Andy") whose singular point of view represents the market; the basis for the page (Ch 2).

**Persona (with an "a")** — a made-up audience composite; discouraged as a campaign basis (Ch 2).

**Primer** — the Hero's opening clarity copy stating plainly what the page advertises; ends with a colon (Ch 2).

**Proof** — anything that makes a claim believable: specifics + credible sources (Ch 3 rule #3, Ch 5).

**School testimonial** — a wall of many ordinary customers' testimonials moving "as one"; triggers LISH (Ch 5).

**Slippery Slide** — Joe Sugarman's idea that every element compels reading the next, all the way down (Ch 2).

**Small test** — a single-variable test run after a big-test win (art, button color, downsell, incentive) (Ch 7).

**Statistical significance** — enough traffic for a test result to be trustworthy (Ch 7).

**Statistics (signal)** — substantial, market-relative numbers that say "trust the numbers" (Ch 5).

**Subheadline (Hero)** — unpacks the headline's promise via Future Pacing (Ch 2).

**Testimonial campaign** — a 3-step system to generate testimonials from scratch (tool + incentivized ask + baked-in collection) (Ch 5).

**The Visitor / The Reader** — Shleyner's name for the single prospect on the page (Ch 1).

**Transformational result** — a contender that at least doubles the control's conversion rate (Ch 1).

**Whale testimonial** — a standalone endorsement from one highly credible person whose reputation transfers to the product (Ch 5).

**Why now?** — the question Compelling Action answers via FOMO / easy / nothing-to-lose / because-you're-asking (Ch 6).
