# Patterns — Transformational Landing Pages

Concrete techniques, with when-to-use / how / trade-offs.

## Build a Transformational Page (CDCA)
**When**: creating or rebuilding any landing page.
**How**: make it achieve **Clarity** (Ch 2) → intensify **Desire** with fascinations (Ch 3–4) → earn **Credibility** (Ch 5) → compel **Action** (Ch 6). Build the page first, then derive the ads from the Hero.
**Trade-offs**: longer than a typical page, but each divider earns its place; design quality matters (50ms trust).

## The Personal Interview (audience research)
**When**: before writing a word — to find your "person."
**How**: ask **open-ended** questions → **mirror** the subject (repeat last words as a question) → **record** it. Mine the rawest, most honest line for your hook.
**Trade-offs**: needs one willing real prospect; a fixed question list is "not wrong" but yields stiffer answers than a fluid conversation.

## The 6-Element Hero
**When**: writing the above-the-fold divider.
**How**: **Primer** (plain what-it-is + colon) → **Headline** (one big, beautiful promise, clear not clever) → **Subhead** (Future Pace: imagine/stop/start) → **Body** (answer "how?", ~20–30 words) → **CTA** (incentive) → **Art** (visualize + reinforce incentive). Each element pulls the eye to the next (Slippery Slide).
**Trade-offs**: many pages omit Hero body copy to stay clean; Shleyner includes it to answer the "how?".

## Transitional Congruence (Order of Operations)
**When**: building the ad that drives traffic.
**How**: build the **landing page first**, then let the Hero's copy/art/layout inform the ad — often literally screenshot the Hero into the social post. If space is tight, use the **headline + part of the art**.
**Trade-offs**: none meaningful; incongruence is a "campaign killer."

## The 3-Step Fascination Process
**When**: writing the desire-building "wall."
**How**: (1) **find the nugget** (value + benefit; transcribe audio/video; hunt benefits first); (2) **tease the value & state the benefit** (validate with If/Then; rewrite archaic source for clarity); (3) **apply a filter**. Write 7× more than you need and pare down.
**Trade-offs**: assembly isn't fully teachable by rule — internalize via copyworking.

## Choosing a Fascination Filter (rotate for variance)
**When**: you have a nugget and want an angle.
**How**: pick by intent — curiosity (**Why**, **What**, **Surprising**), fear (**What never**, **Caution**), self-ID (**If-then**, **Are you**/FOMO), authority/uniqueness (**Mechanisms**, **Best**, **Truth**), ease/value (**Quickest-easiest**, **Number**, **How**, **Big promise**), engagement (**Specific question**, **When**, **Statement+Interest+Benefit**).
**Trade-offs**: fear filters (#4, #14) lose credibility if overused.

## Confidence Signal Stack
**When**: claims need backing.
**How**: **logos** (transfer brand trust; place high — "show proof first") + **guarantee** (transfer risk; bigger guarantee = more confidence) + **statistics** (substantial, market-relative numbers, "trust the numbers").
**Trade-offs**: stats must be genuinely substantial relative to competitors or they read weak.

## Whale vs. School Testimonials
**When**: adding social proof.
**How**: **whale** = one highly credible name (Ogilvy/Abraham) → transfers reputation; **school** = a wall of many ordinary customers → triggers LISH. Prefer schools when you lack a credible whale (often higher impact).
**Trade-offs**: a whale from an unknown person does nothing.

## Testimonial Campaign (from zero)
**When**: you have no testimonials.
**How**: (1) collection/embed tool (e.g. Testimonial.to) for video + text; (2) a **clear, easy, incentivized** ask to your audience; (3) **bake collection into the content** (ask at every intermission) so they accrue.
**Trade-offs**: never rely on goodwill — always offer an incentive (gift card, backlink, free content).

## The "Why Now?" Action Stack
**When**: closing for immediacy.
**How**: **FOMO** (time/supply-limited economic incentive) + **easy** (fewest form fields) + **nothing to lose** (guarantee, optional downsell) + **because you're asking** (one CTA per divider, stand-out color).
**Trade-offs**: a downsell captures bounces but can cannibalize — start with one CTA, add it if bounce is high.

## Test Big, Then Small
**When**: validating and improving.
**How**: run the CDCA contender vs. the control to **statistical significance**. If it wins, promote it to control and run **one-variable** small tests (favorite: the incentive). If it loses, revisit the person/angle and rewrite the **Hero hook**, then retest.
**Trade-offs**: small tests before a big-difference win waste the chance at a transformational lift.
