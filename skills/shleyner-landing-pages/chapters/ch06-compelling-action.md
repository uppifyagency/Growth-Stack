# Chapter 6: Compelling Action — The "Why Now?" Engine

## Core Idea
After the page sells The Visitor, it must answer **"Why now?"** Immediacy is the core function of direct-response — and there are four reasons that manufacture it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 4 Reasons to Act Now** (Submodule M — each answers "Why now?"):
  1. **"Because of FOMO!"** — a manufactured **economic incentive** with urgent conditions (24-hour flash sale, "save 50%"). Vary the percentage, the time window, the supply cap, or first-N bonuses. FOMO → urgency → immediacy.
  2. **"Because it's easy!"** — strip the **conversion form** to the fewest lines possible (email / name / card). Long forms make people "come back later" (= gone). "Pity the visitor"; delight her with how easy it is.
  3. **"Because I've nothing to lose!"** — a **guarantee** de-risks the decision; most people won't abuse it and the gain outweighs the loss. The **free downsell** retains fence-sitters by capturing emails into a nurture funnel.
  4. **"Because you're asking!"** — **one CTA per divider**. Many pages forget to actually *ask* repeatedly; every divider should remind The Visitor to take the next step.

## Key Concepts
- **Immediacy**: the specific goal of this module — move The Visitor to act *right away*, not "later."
- **Economic incentive**: the content-marketer's default lever (save money / exclusive access), structured around time or supply.
- **Conversion form friction**: each extra field costs conversions; minimize ruthlessly (VeryGoodCopy asks only for email).
- **Downsell / cannibalization**: a second CTA captures bounces but can pull would-be buyers to the lower-commitment path — start with one CTA, add a downsell if bounce is high.
- **CTA anchoring**: every CTA jumps to the divider holding the form, so many CTAs ≠ many forms.

## Mental Models
- **"As soon as people leave, they're gone"** — assume no second chance; manufacture a reason to act on this visit.
- **Make the button impossible to miss** — a contrasting color the page uses nowhere else.
- **One CTA per divider** — repetition of the ask is free conversion lift.

## Anti-patterns
- **No urgency** — without FOMO/scarcity there's no answer to "why not later?"
- **Long, effortful forms** — they bleed conversions even when the offer is strong.
- **A single CTA on a long page** — most dividers silently fail to ask.
- **Leading with a downsell** — risks cannibalizing primary sales before you've tested.

## Key Takeaways
1. The page must answer "Why now?" — immediacy is the whole point.
2. Manufacture FOMO with a time- or supply-limited economic incentive.
3. Make converting effortless: minimize form fields.
4. De-risk with a guarantee; capture fence-sitters with a downsell.
5. One CTA per divider, in a stand-out color.
