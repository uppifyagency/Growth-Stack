# Chapter 7: Testing — Big Tests, Small Tests & Iteration

## Core Idea
Testing is mostly a *mindset*, not tooling: first win with a **big test** (a radically different page), then refine forever with **small tests** (one variable at a time). The day you stop testing is the day you stop improving.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Big test = "big difference"** (Eugene Schwartz): pit two **drastically different** pages — your CDCA-complete contender vs. the bare control. Big differences give the best shot at a *transformational* conversion rate. Run it to **statistical significance** (enough traffic to be viable).
  - When to use: first, before any small optimization.
- **The recovery loop when a big test loses**: don't quit (failure is part of the process). Return to the **personal interview / your "person" (Andy)** and ask: *Right angle? Right problem? Right person?* Then **rewrite the Hero's headline, subhead, body** — the **hook**, not the whole page (content is fixed, so fascinations/testimonials stay). Rewrite → retest → keep going.
- **Small test**: once the contender wins, **discard the old control; the winner becomes the new control.** Then test a **clone with one variable changed**:
  - artwork · button color · the downsell · **the incentive** (Shleyner's favorite — incentives are finite and must be refreshed anyway).

## Key Concepts
- **Control vs. contender**: control = current best; contender = challenger. Winner is promoted to control.
- **Statistical significance**: enough visitors for the result to be trustworthy.
- **Baseline-dependent ceilings**: lift depends on where you start; most pages convert <5%, so there's huge room — but no page hits 100%.
- **The hook is what changes**: between big-test rewrites, the angle/claims/promises change; the underlying content doesn't.

## Mental Models
- **"Failing often, and testing big differences, shows you are doing enough as a copywriter"** — failure is evidence of ambition, not defeat.
- **Persistence + tenacity** are part of direct-response; rewrite/retest is the job.
- **Always be running a small test** — continuous one-variable iteration compounds.

## Anti-patterns
- **Starting with small tests** — button-color tweaks before a big-difference breakthrough waste the opportunity.
- **Quitting after a losing big test** — instead, re-interrogate angle/problem/person and rewrite the hook.
- **Calling a test before significance** — too little traffic = noise, not a result.
- **Stopping once you're happy** — improvement halts the moment testing does.

## Key Takeaways
1. Win first with a big test (a page that looks nothing like the control).
2. Run to statistical significance before judging.
3. If it loses, revisit your person and rewrite the Hero's hook — then retest.
4. After a win, promote the contender and run one-variable small tests.
5. Changing the incentive is the highest-value recurring small test.

## Connects To
- **Ch 1**: "testing big differences" is the engine behind transformational results.
- **Ch 2**: losing tests are fixed at the Hero (headline/subhead/body) and the personal interview.
- **Ch 6**: the incentive is the favorite small-test variable.
