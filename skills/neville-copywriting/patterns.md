# Patterns & Playbooks — The Copywriting Course

## AIDA Template-First Drafting
**When to use**: facing a blank page for any email, sales page, ad, or script (kills writer's block).
**How**: open a doc; write 4–5 subject lines + a one-line "purpose of email"; bold Attention/Interest/Desire/Action and write one sentence under each — Attention (headline/question/name), Interest (3–7 facts/benefits, cap ~7), Desire (logical ROI + emotional status/relief/fear), Action (one instruction); then flesh out and delete the labels — what remains is the letter.
**Trade-offs**: reliable and block-proof, but feels formulaic unless each section is genuinely re-thought for the reader; a single Action only — multiple options dilute response.

## Slippery Slide
**When**: writing any email or page meant to make someone act.
**How**: structure so each sentence's only job is to get the next one read, fueled by a story or genuine education; slide Attention→Interest→Desire to exactly one CTA at the bottom. Ruthlessly cut any boring sentence or the slope breaks.
**Trade-offs**: same structure works short or long; misleading hooks break the slide and burn trust.

## Give-First / 70-30 Outreach
**When**: cold email, welcome emails, recurring newsletters, deal emails — any first contact where instinct says ask.
**How**: lead with real value the reader can use even if they never buy (free report, three vetted vendors, a cost-saving idea); remove every ask from first contact; keep ~70% content to 30% pitch.
**Trade-offs**: front-loads research/effort and counterintuitive for salespeople, but flips reply rates from near-zero to >50%.

## Reader-First Rewrite (No One Cares About You)
**When**: any headline/opening that starts with the founder story or company.
**How**: delete the "we scour the globe / founded by…" intro; rewrite the same copy as the reader's story and problem (Dave's shirt bunches on his commute). Credentials wait until the reader is interested.
**Trade-offs**: your proof gets demoted, but it only matters once interest exists.

## Define Your Bob
**When**: before writing any product description or email broadcast.
**How**: profile the real buyer from your own calls/emails/data (age, routine, fears, discretionary income), ask "what can I help them with most?", write to that one person, and re-segment toward whoever actually pays.
**Trade-offs**: narrowing feels like excluding buyers, but "sell to everyone and you've sold to no one."

## CBI Lenses + Swipe-File Headlines
**When**: stuck on a headline or subject line.
**How**: rewrite one decent headline through Competitive (beat rivals), Benefit-driven (stat/testimonial), and Inspirational (you can too) lenses; or mine top/upvoted titles from Reddit/Quora/Digg/Twitter + Google "searches related to" into a swipe file, then test.
**Trade-offs**: fast idea generation across buyer types; stay relevant (no bait-and-switch), and never ship one winning formula forever — it fades with repetition.

## Match Copy Length to Price & Complexity
**When**: deciding short vs long copy.
**How**: email signup / cheap known item = short (1–2 benefits); ~$100 or needs install = medium; expensive/complex ($1k+) = long (Sugarman's rule). Answer every objection but cut any boring line.
**Trade-offs**: misjudging the buyer's question-load under-sells or bloats the page.

## Format-for-Comprehension Pass
**When**: any near-final copy, even if well-written.
**How**: apply bullets, bold, color coding (red=bad/green=good), a narrow ~500px column, small italic captions under images, shrunk images, and a BFB for the CTA.
**Trade-offs**: big comprehension lift with no new copy; over-formatting or oversized images distract.

## Cold Email Teardown (4 Steps)
**When**: a long, all-about-me cold email isn't converting.
**How**: run the 4 steps — add the real name (+50–100% readership); delete self-history, replace with one quantified benefit line ("$24k/year saved"), ideally backed by a custom proof artifact built from public info; restructure on the AIDA cold-email outline; collapse all CTAs into one bolded ask. Cut 60–80% of text. If you have a value-bomb, give it; if not, write 1–2 sentences with the direct ask. When you're the product, lead with "what I'll do for you" and demote credentials to bullets.
**Trade-offs**: a few minutes' research per recipient vs bulk sending, but tripling reply rate (1-in-10 → 3-in-10) outweighs lost volume; fabricated numbers collapse credibility.

## Modified AIDA (AIDA + T) Sales Page
**When**: selling a considered/unfamiliar product on a single page.
**How**: headline(s) + sub-headline + image → Interest (it's easier than they think) → Desire (the new world it opens) → Testimonial → product shot → comparison → exact how-to-buy → Action with light P.S. scarcity. Build long pages by stacking short per-block sections written separately.
**Trade-offs**: overkill for known/branded products that need only a buy button; for strong brands selling is done beforehand (fix the email sequence first).

## Steal the Amazon Field Set
**When**: building or auditing any eCommerce product listing.
**How**: mirror name/price/reviews/stock/summary → full description → the boring mandatory block (dimensions, weight, power, what's in the box, shipping, compatible products). Treat the store as you replicated on a showroom floor; each product page IS a landing page.
**Trade-offs**: skip fields you genuinely can't fill; the boring block is what most omit and what kills ~80% of support contacts.

## Work-Backwards Page Review
**When**: critiquing a sales/product/home/landing page.
**How**: identify the single goal (call/apply/buy); verify the CTA repeats per section and closes at the bottom; then strip buzzwords, clever labels, sliders/carousels, and mid-page checkouts; show don't tell (varied case studies big/medium/small, collective testimonials, a believable reason-why) instead of "we're different."
**Trade-offs**: resist redesigning layout first — content and the close matter more than visuals.

## The 3 AdWords Rules (Super Ad)
**When**: writing or auditing a text-only Google search ad.
**How**: read competitor ads ranking for your keyword, extract the priorities they all promise, fuse them into one concise super ad; keep it to the point (don't be clever); word it for buyers only since every click costs money. Match the landing page (ad scent).
**Trade-offs**: copying competitor priorities converges on sameness — differentiate on offer (price, guarantee, service area).

## Bleary-Eyes Single-Goal Page + Heat Map
**When**: reviewing a homepage/landing page or diagnosing an underperformer with live traffic.
**How**: read it carelessly in ~5 seconds — if "what is this / what do I do" isn't obvious, fix that first; give the page ONE goal (landing page = one CTA top and bottom). Use a heat map (Crazy Egg) to find the deep-blue zone where attention dies and rework that section; leave warm pages alone. Explain confusing offers with a literal 1-2-3 how-it-works image block.
**Trade-offs**: a homepage may need more breadth than a single-CTA landing page; heat maps need live traffic.

## A-Pile Give-First Autoresponder
**When**: warming strangers into buyers over time on any list.
**How**: send plain-text or minimal centered emails (no graphic-designer polish) so sender reputation earns the open regardless of subject; drip free value (case studies work in ANY industry) on intervals (0, +1, +2, +3 days), use the email AS the content, and drop hints/links to your offer only a few times deep in the sequence. End each email on a cliffhanger; plan the whole arc on one page first.
**Trade-offs**: slower than a hard pitch and you give a lot away free; pays off in trust, opens, and buyers who purchase immediately when you do sell.

## Constant Launch
**When**: you've run a launch with a great email sequence and don't want it to die on launch day.
**How**: take all launch broadcast emails, strip the dates (or use merge fields), and load them into a follow-up sequence so every new signup relives the launch.
**Trade-offs**: upfront sequence-building work; converts a one-time launch into evergreen passive income.

## Feedback-Routing PS
**When**: you want demand validation or feedback from a list without inbox flood.
**How**: add a PS asking readers to reply ("reply: I want it" for demand; "reply with your problems" to whitelist + collect ideas); at scale route overflow to a Google Form or blog-comment thread.
**Trade-offs**: direct replies flood the inbox at scale; the form route is cleaner but loses some whitelisting benefit.

## The 3-Email Black Friday Sequence
**When**: running any time-bound promotion — product, service, cold list, or no audience.
**How**: E1 (~2 days before deadline): offer + picture + few features + discount + deadline + one CTA. E2: proof/cool angle (clip EXISTING testimonials, numbers, story). E3: one-to-two-sentence scarcity reminder. Build each email from existing assets, reuse the copy on the sales page/blog, pre-load into an auto-responder, and next year just change the dates (~5 min). Too cheap to discount? Run a Reverse Black Friday (price going up). No list? Sell to friends/family first.
**Trade-offs**: cold lists convert worse (add a polite one-liner); resist making E1/E3 long — short wins.

## The 3-Step Blog Post Process (Research → Write → Promote)
**When**: any time you sit down to publish and feel overwhelmed.
**How**: research a topic list first (most of the time goes here, fed by the Idea File's 4 buckets); write by filling the Skeleton Outline (title + subheads), making each subhead specific, ending each section with an action step, then bundling all steps into a cheat sheet; chop the finished post into social posts to promote. No credibility? Write "how I did it" (specific), not "how to" (broad). First-draft bans: no SEO, no selling, no perfectionism; write intro/conclusion last; ship over polish.
**Trade-offs**: value is front-loaded into research, which most people skip; if the outline reads weird, toss the idea early.

## Timeless Content from Modules
**When**: salvaging a dead post or building content that should earn traffic for years.
**How**: create an angle on the under-served long tail; sex up the headline (4 variants, keep the slug); make an on-angle grabbing image; then add Blog Content Modules — TOC, custom score, map, calculator, generator, original-data tables, shareable templates, gated PDF, CTA. To improve any weak post, "just add modules." Pair each feature with a benefit for first-time buyers.
**Trade-offs**: the module step is the real work (and where people quit), but each post compounds and resists copycats instead of dipping.

## Own the Channel + Validate Before Producing
**When**: choosing where to invest content time, or launching a product/audience.
**How**: center owned, durable channels — email (controllable, act anytime) + YouTube (free discovery) — and route social back to them; ride boosted formats early but never depend on rented platforms (FB organic reach went 90%→~4%). Pick FFR content (Fast, Fun, Repeatable) that's already proven popular, in the Sex-Cash Venn overlap. Build the audience first, then find what to sell; sell/prepay before building; source ideas from signals (reviews, Glassdoor, Crunchbase/CB Insights, SimilarWeb direct traffic).
**Trade-offs**: owned channels compound slowly vs viral reach; "do what you love" can ignore demand — pair with proven-topic checks; selling before building risks a delivery scramble but proves willingness to pay.

## The $100 First-Gig Sprint (Getting Clients)
**When**: a beginner is frozen by fear of selling or overthinking a "real business."
**How**: build the lazy Google Doc portfolio (technical + real-world + previously-paid skills), write one concrete service offer, post it to personal Facebook/LinkedIn first (trust already exists), give it 24–48h, collect $100; broaden to Craigslist/Gumtree/Thumbtack if needed. Restrict scope ("for $100 you get this, this, this") and tier the price (~$500 mid, open-ended top); when leads flood in, don't delete the post — double the price, double again. Send ultra-short (1–2 sentence) cold emails then CALL directly. Document before/after screenshots of every job. Go broad first; let the market niche you down.
**Trade-offs**: the money is psychological de-risking, not a career; negotiate tiers by phone, not email; niching prematurely can shrink the market below viability.

## Test and De-Buzzword the Message
**When**: reviewing a sales/landing page before shipping or scaling spend.
**How**: run copytesting on the real target audience (which arguments they care about, remaining doubts), self-review with the caveman skim test, cut buzzwords (B2B is human-to-human), make every line earn its pixels, and synthesize data with experienced gut rather than obeying HIPPO or raw data.
**Trade-offs**: qualitative feedback is noisy and needs a filter; over-trusting it (or pure data) leads to bad calls either way.
