# Glossary — The Copywriting Course (Neville Medhora)

**A-Pile / B-Pile** — Gary Halbert's split of incoming mail into the trusted personal pile opened on the sender's name (A) vs the junk swiped to trash (B) (Ch 7).
**Action Step** — a section's closing instruction so explicit the reader has no guesswork on what to do next (Ch 11).
**Ad Scent** — the match between an ad's promise/headline and its landing page; a mismatch tanks conversion even at high click-through (Ch 8).
**AIDA** — Attention · Interest · Desire · Action; the four-part sales skeleton used as a fill-in template for any email, page, ad, or pitch (Ch 1, 2, 3, 5, 12).
**AlsoAsked** — tool that branches Google's "people also ask" questions into demand-proven post titles (Ch 11).
**Authentic Headline** — the only headline type that survives repeated exposure: relevant, casual, lowercase, not over-promising, reads as personally sent (Ch 3).
**BFB (Big F***ing Button)** — making the one action so obvious a viewer 10 steps back knows what to do in 1 second (Ch 2, 3).
**Bleary-Eyes Read** — reviewing a page carelessly the way real visitors do (5-sec test) to check the purpose and action are instantly clear (Ch 8).
**Blog Content Modules** — movable, reusable blocks (TOC, custom score, map, calculator, generator, data tables, templates, PDF, CTA) snapped onto a post to improve it without rewriting (Ch 6, 12).
**Bob (Composite-Average Customer)** — one fictional buyer = the statistical average of your real customers, addressed as a single person (Ch 1, 3).
**Broad vs Specific Article** — a definitive how-to guide (needs credibility) vs a "here's how I did it" case study (works without authority) (Ch 11).
**Broadcast** — a one-time autoresponder email sent to the whole list on a date and never re-sent (Ch 7).
**Buyer-Only Targeting** — wording an ad to qualify in purchase-intent visitors and repel browsers, since every paid click costs money (Ch 8).
**Buzzword Test** — if a word ("efficient", "productive") can be broken down further, it's a buzzword; replace with concrete before/after + ROI (Ch 9, 13).
**Casual Copy** — writing as if explaining to one friend at lunch — professional and real ("suit jacket on"), never goofy (Ch 1, 2).
**Caveman Voice** — self-review by reading a page as a bored skimmer ("boring, stupid, don't care"); whatever still stands out works (Ch 13).
**CBI Method** — Competitive · Benefit-driven · Inspirational; three headline lenses for three buyer psychologies (Ch 2, 3).
**Cheat Sheet** — the packaged sum of all action steps; doubles as TOC, content upgrade, and promo asset (Ch 11).
**Cliffhanger** — ending each sequence email by teasing what tomorrow's email reveals (Ch 7).
**Cold Email Outline** — AIDA re-skinned as four blocks: intro / what it does for them / what happens if they get it / when can we talk (Ch 4).
**Constant Launch / Perpetual Launch** — recycling launch broadcast emails (dates stripped) into a follow-up sequence so every new signup relives the launch (Ch 7).
**Content Moat** — making content so hard to produce (e.g. 40-hr teardowns) that the difficulty itself becomes a defensible advantage (Ch 13).
**Cool Angle (Email 2)** — the proof email in the 3-email sequence — the only one with creative latitude — built from testimonials, numbers, or backstory (Ch 10).
**Copywriting** — writing words (or images/video on the same mechanism) on a page that convert better — the difference between rejection and a sale (Ch 1).
**Custom Proof Artifact** — a small personalized chart/list built from a prospect's public info to prove you did the homework (Ch 4).
**Custom Score** — a made-up proprietary metric (Doug-score style) that differentiates content competitors can't copy (Ch 6).
**Deep-Blue-of-Death** — a heat-map zone (Crazy Egg) that turns deep blue partway down a page, signaling readers quit there (Ch 8).
**The Dip** — the inevitable traffic decay of one-off, event-driven content with no evergreen backbone (Ch 6, 12).
**Digital Product Leverage** — a course built once has near-zero marginal cost and sells indefinitely ("like making Terminator 2 once") (Ch 10).
**Does It Add to the Message?** — the one-question editing test: keep a wacky line only if it serves the message, else cut (Ch 1).
**Double the Price** — when an offer floods with leads, raise the price instead of removing the post; repeat (Ch 9).
**Dropping Hints** — selling without selling: linking your offer only a few times across a value-heavy sequence so scarcity of mention piques curiosity (Ch 7).
**Earn Its Pixels** — every line of copy must justify its space because readers skim and glaze over anything they don't care about (Ch 13).
**Ethical Bribe** — a free gift (ebook, case studies, hidden-post password) offered for an email signup; best for boring/unsexy businesses (Ch 7).
**Features vs Benefits** — sell what the product does for the reader, not what it is; pair every spec with its relatable outcome (Ch 2, 6).
**FFR (Fast, Fun, Repeatable)** — Noah Kagan's filter for choosing which already-proven-popular content to actually produce (Ch 13).
**First-Send Pass** — a generic email gets ~20% open the first time then declines sharply; don't mistake the first pass for working copy (Ch 7).
**Follow-up** — the evergreen, interval-timed autoresponder sequence sent automatically to every new subscriber; the money-maker (Ch 7).
**Give-Give-Give (not Ask-Ask-Ask)** — leading outreach with usable value (asking nothing) instead of pitching for the sale (Ch 1, 2).
**Giving Gary vs Leeching Larry** — body-copy test: Gary leads with the reader's quantified benefit; Larry drones on about his own company (Ch 4).
**Headline Math** — ~8/10 read the headline but only ~2/10 read on, so the headline decides whether anything else is read (Ch 3).
**HIPPO** — Highest Paid Person's Opinion; when rank overrides data — mature practice is data + experienced gut (Ch 13).
**Idea File** — a private, ever-growing topic reservoir in 4 buckets (Topics I enjoy · Things people ask · Personal stories · Possible articles) (Ch 11).
**Interval** — days between sequence emails (0 = immediate, then +1/+2/+3-day gaps) (Ch 7).
**Lazy Google Doc Portfolio** — a shareable Google Doc (not a website) listing technical, real-world, and previously-paid skills (Ch 9).
**Length is Earned** — long-form copy is justified only when every section does a job; most long pages are bloated imitations (Ch 8, 3).
**Logical vs Emotional Desire** — AIDA's two Desire levers: justify cost with ROI math AND trigger feeling (status/relief/fear) (Ch 1).
**Long Tail of SEO** — many low-volume, human-varied search phrases that sum to real traffic the dominant sites ignore (Ch 6).
**Make Content, Get Links** — Ross Hudgens's reduction of SEO to two moves: produce link-worthy content, then outreach to earn links (Ch 12).
**Match Length to Buyer Knowledge** — a branded/Googled product needs only a buy button; an unfamiliar product needs the full sequence (Ch 3, 5).
**Modified AIDA (AIDA + T)** — AIDA with Testimonials inserted (plus an optional benefits comparison) — the default sales-page skeleton (Ch 5).
**No One Cares About You** — open with the reader's problem, not the founder story; they care about themselves (Ch 1, 3).
**One Call To Action** — funnel everyone to a single unmistakable next step; readers execute one action well, menus split attention (Ch 12).
**One-Two-Three How-It-Works Block** — explaining a subscription/box/service in three literally numbered, image-backed steps (Ch 8).
**One View Concept** — a single viewer can change your life years later, so publish even at low view counts (Ch 13).
**Owned vs Rented Reach** — email/your list are assets you control and compound; social reach is borrowed and decays as platforms monetize (Ch 12, 13).
**Personalization Lift** — replacing "to whom it may concern" with the recipient's real name gives a 50–100% jump in readership (Ch 4).
**Plain-English TLDR** — for technical pages, list the impressive "sciencey" detail for credibility, then restate it for the layman (Ch 6).
**Playground Brand** — a personal brand under your own name (OKDork, NevBlog) used as a no-downside lab; winners get copied into the company brand (Ch 13).
**PNG Transparency** — using transparent .png graphics so images sit on any background and survive site migrations; JPEG only for colored squares (Ch 8).
**Quality Score** — the deliverability signal that drops (pushing you to Promotions/Spam) when you mass-blast irrelevant email (Ch 7).
**Restrict the Scope** — spell out exactly what $100 buys so clients don't expect more; charge extra for anything beyond it (Ch 9).
**Results Over Completion Rate** — judge a course by whether buyers got the result they wanted, not by the % who finish (Ch 10).
**Reusability** — writing copy once from existing assets and redeploying across emails, pages, blogs, and future campaigns (Ch 10).
**Reverse Black Friday** — when a product is too cheap to discount, announce the price is going UP so the current price becomes the deal (Ch 10).
**Sea of Sameness** — Peep Laja's term for content that copies high-volume keywords incumbents already own and so drowns; escape via format (Ch 13).
**Sell While You Sleep** — pre-scheduling the email sequence in an auto-responder weeks ahead so the sale runs automatically (Ch 10).
**Sex Cash Theory** — balancing projects that make money ("cash") against those that earn credibility ("sex"); aim for the Venn overlap (Ch 13).
**Sitting-down-at-the-computer-itis** — the reflex to turn stiff and formal the moment you touch a keyboard, left over from English class (Ch 1, 2).
**Skeleton Outline** — the title plus a few subheadings that fully plan a post before writing; that IS the article (Ch 11).
**Slippery Slide / Slope** — every line's only job is to get the next line read (via story or education), sliding to one action (Ch 2, 3, 12).
**Socially-Proven Headlines** — subject-line ideas mined from upvoted titles on Reddit/Quora/Digg/Twitter, then rewritten for your audience (Ch 2, 3, 12).
**Super Ad** — one ad that fuses every winning angle the top competitors are already advertising into a single message (Ch 8).
**The $100 Goal** — targeting a first $100 (not a business) so fear, not money, is the obstacle removed; getting paid rewires a beginner (Ch 9).
**The 3-Email Black Friday Sequence** — Introduce (E1) → Cool Angle/proof (E2) → close-the-door scarcity (E3); the universal promotion formula (Ch 10).
**The 4 Steps of a Great Cold Sales Email** — personalize, give something or keep it short, use a simple outline, end with one request (Ch 4).
**The 70/30 Rule** — make recurring/audience copy 70% useful content and 30% sales; help first, sell last (Ch 2, 6).
**Three-Pronged Pricing** — Basic ($100, scope-restricted) / Advanced (~$500) / Expensive ($2,000+); pitch the floor, upsell up, no ceiling (Ch 9).
**Timeless (Evergreen) Content** — a post/asset engineered to keep earning search traffic indefinitely instead of spiking then fading (Ch 6, 12).
**Ultra-Short Cold Copy** — one-to-two-sentence emails that land in the primary inbox and convert best; over-written copy performs worse (Ch 9).
**Whitelisting PS Trick** — a PS asking readers to reply with their problems; the reply auto-adds you to contacts so future emails hit the inbox (Ch 7).
**Work Backwards** — design content/a review from the desired end action (signup/sale), then check the CTA repeats and closes (Ch 5, 6).
**Writing Modules** — movable reusable content blocks (lists, images/GIFs, scored rankings, quotes) added to make any post better and more defensible (Ch 6, 12).
