# Chapter 10: Building & Selling Products, Courses & Promotions

## Core Idea
Build a digital product (a course) once, then sell it over and over forever — "it's like James Cameron making Terminator 2 once, then selling it again and again." The same simple, low-pressure copy formula sells every product, service, or course: introduce the offer, prove it, then close the door with scarcity. Write that copy once and reuse it everywhere — emails, sales page, auto-responder — so the sale runs while you sleep.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 3-Email Black Friday Sequence** — the underlying formula for ANY promotion (product, service, cold list, or no-list). **Email 1 = Introduce the offer**: name the product, show a picture, list a few features, state the discount + deadline, single clear CTA. **Email 2 = Add a "cool angle"** (Neville/Dan's term for the proof email): testimonials, screenshots of reviews, big numbers, recent results, or the product's backstory. **Email 3 = Close the door**: ultra-short reminder ("it's almost up, deadline tonight"), repeat offer + deadline + CTA. Use when running any time-bound sale. How: time Email 1 ~2 days before the deadline; keep Email 3 to one or two sentences.
- **The "Cool Angle" (Email 2)** — the only email with creative latitude; 1 and 3 are mechanical. When you have proof: pull existing testimonials, Mashable/Forbes mentions, before/after numbers, or a "discount-on-a-discount" price-tier table (their price / your price / your sale price). How: don't rewrite from scratch — clip existing material that's already raving about you.
- **Reusability** — "the whole point of the internet is to leverage work you've done before, over and over." Never write copy that dies after one send. How: build emails from existing sales-page text, Amazon reviews, and Instagram posts; then redeploy the same email on the sales page, blog, auto-responder, and next year's sale (change the dates, ~5 minutes).
- **Sell While You Sleep (pre-scheduled auto-responder)** — load the 3-email sequence into an auto-responder weeks ahead so it fires on the right dates automatically. How: pre-build, schedule, walk away; "I started getting all these dings on my phone — because I did the work a few weeks before."
- **Reverse Black Friday** — when a product is already cheap and can't be discounted, announce the price is going UP soon; the current low price becomes the deal (used to push a $4.99 book to $14.99).
- **The Facebook Script / Selling Without a List** — for people with no audience: post the offer to your immediate network (friends/family already "like and trust you"), using the same 3-post structure as the 3 emails, with "DM me" or "comment" as the CTA instead of a button.

## Key Concepts
- **Digital product leverage** — make a course in 3–4 weeks, then it "sells every single day for doing absolutely nothing."
- **Results over completion rate** — course completion rates (1–2%) are a vanity metric; judge by whether students got the result they came for, not attendance.
- **Platform doesn't matter to buyers** — customers don't care about your slick LMS; a WordPress page + protection plugin, Notion, Dropbox Paper, or a Gumroad/PayPal button works fine.
- **Pick the platform that fits YOUR primary action** — Teachable/Kajabi/Thinkific are for "host a video → PDF → click Next → track progress"; if that's not your core use (e.g. you need rich text/copy formatting), don't use them.
- **Community is coming** — "within a year or two every course will have a community component"; but software slickness matters less than an active first set of users.
- **Cool angle = proof** — the middle email's job is credibility (testimonials, numbers, story).
- **Scarcity closes** — the final reminder converts fence-sitters; short emails get the best open rates because they read like "a tap on the shoulder," not a sales pitch.
- **Naming a course/product** — test names; emotional/specific names ("Sumo Business Blueprint") can beat boring descriptive ones; a quirky spelling ("Kopywriting" with two K's) signals "different."
- **Raise prices** — under-pricing forces high volume to make any money; higher prices make people take it more seriously AND fund a better product.

## Mental Models
- **Think of a course like Terminator 2** — high one-time build cost, near-zero marginal cost, infinite resale.
- **Build a community like a neighborhood, not a master-planned city** — start with one tiny forum (e.g. a "31-Day Challenge"), let demand pull the next section into existence; pre-planning every category gives you a deserted "giant Chinese ghost city."
- **Use "our only sale of the year" when you rarely discount** — it makes a single discount email a genuinely strong subject line and reactivates people who balked at full price.
- **Treat shipping a discount like a notification, not a negotiation** — "every time we're scared to send a discount, people end up buying."

## Anti-patterns
- Building a course you're bored by or that won't give buyers a real ROI (Neville's "Social Crash Course" and "Sumo Diet" flops — no clear 10x return = weak sales).
- Over-engineering the platform / "over-designing" before validating — get sucked into Kajabi/Notion quirks instead of shipping; pre-sell a beta first.
- Rewriting every sales asset from scratch each campaign instead of reusing existing material.
- Pricing a digital product too low ($4.99) so it isn't worth focusing on.
- Reviewing/selling copy inside a Facebook group when your product needs long-form formatting — wrong tool for the job.
- Trying to copy a big competitor's entire forum structure on day one (empty sections look like "someone tried to start a forum and failed").
- Long, heavy "sales-y" final reminders — the short one-liner outperforms.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every promotion is the same 3 emails: **introduce → prove (cool angle) → close with scarcity.** It "almost seems stupidly simple, but this is all it is."
2. Write copy ONCE, reuse it across emails, sales page, blog, and auto-responder — and next year by just changing the dates.
3. Pre-schedule the sequence so it sells while you sleep.
4. Ship the product/course on the simplest platform that fits your primary action; don't let tooling become procrastination.
5. Charge enough — raising prices fixes economics, signals value, and funds a better product.
6. No list? Sell to your immediate network first using the same structure; they already trust you.
7. Use the "cool angle" email for proof; clip existing testimonials and reviews rather than inventing new ones.
