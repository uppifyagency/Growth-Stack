# Chapter 7: Email Writing & Autoresponders

## Core Idea
The whole game of email is to land in the **A-pile** (the personal, want-it stuff), not the **B-pile** (junk people thumb-delete). You earn that by being the opposite of the **Annoying Friend** — give a lot of value *before* you ask for anything. An autoresponder is a "magical slave machine" that warms strangers into buyers automatically: do the work once, and every new subscriber relives your best content (and your best launch) forever.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The A-Pile / B-Pile** (Gary Halbert) — When sorting mail or thumbing through a phone, people split everything into the A-pile (real, personal, "belongs to me") and the B-pile (junk, swiped to delete). Use as the lens for *every* email. **How**: make emails look personal, not "newslettery" — plain text or minimal centered template, no graphic-designer polish — and be genuinely useful, so your *name* alone earns the open regardless of subject line.
- **The Annoying Friend** — The mental model for why most marketing feels spammy: the friend who only calls when he wants something (take, take, take). Use when deciding email cadence/intent. **How**: invert it — give free info, case studies, help, ask how they are, expect nothing; then "by the way, we have this if you want it."
- **Give-First (Gary Halbert)** — "The #1 problem with people trying to make money is they try to get a lot without giving a lot of value first." **How**: front-load the relationship with free value; money flows back after trust is built.
- **The Ethical Bribe** — A free gift (ebook, white paper, case studies, password to a hidden post) offered in exchange for an email signup. **When**: use it for "boring"/unsexy businesses (analytics, B2B) where the content alone won't pull signups; skip it for an already-interesting personal blog (a plain "sign up" form converts, and you avoid grab-and-unsubscribe). Demonstrated with the dog ("a treat"): the bribe makes them *eager* to sign up.
- **Subject + Life** — How to write endless emails on any niche: take your **Subject** and cross it with **Life** (relationships, problems, impressing people, work, sleep, family). **How**: "wine" + Valentine's date = "ways to impress your date"; "wine" + status = the Robert Parker story. A good *story* attached to a product sells like crazy (the Prey laptop-theft story).
- **The Constant Launch / Perpetual Launch** — Don't let a great launch sequence die after launch day. **How**: take all the broadcast emails you sent during a launch, strip the dates, drop them into a follow-up autoresponder. Every new signup relives the launch "as if it just launched" — turns a one-time product into an evergreen one (passive income = do the work once).
- **Broadcasts vs. Follow-ups** — The two core mechanics. **Broadcast** = a one-time email sent to the whole list on a date, never sent again (use for a real-time sale, a timely blog post). **Follow-up** = the actual autoresponder sequence; sent on intervals (0 days, then +1, +2, +3...) to everyone forever, the moment they sign up. "Follow-ups are the secret sauce."
- **The Whitelisting PS Trick** — In one of the first 3 emails, add a PS: "reply and tell me what your problems are / what you need help with." When they hit reply, you're auto-added to their contacts → future emails skip Promotions/Spam and hit the inbox. Bonus: free feedback for years + it feels personal.
- **The "I want it, I want it" PS** — End a warm-up email with "reply and say I want it, I want it, I want it" to gauge demand and pre-sell. Floods the inbox with qualified buyers begging for the product before it's even released (validation + commitment in one line).
- **The Cliffhanger** — End every sequence email teasing the next: "Tomorrow I'm going to show you step-by-step how to validate ideas with zero dollars." Makes people *look forward* to the next email daily. **How**: plan the whole sequence in a one-page Google/Word doc first, so you always know what tomorrow's email is and can write the cliffhanger backwards.
- **Dropping Hints** — Sell without selling: across a value-heavy sequence, link your *other* product only a few times (e.g. email 1–3 no link, email 4 one link, email 6 again). Scarcity of mention piques curiosity; doubled/tripled sales of the *old* product while promoting the *new* one.

## Key Concepts
- **A-pile** — The trusted, personal pile that gets opened on the name alone; your goal for every send.
- **Newslettery** — The B-pile look (pretty, graphic-designed, image-heavy) that gets swiped to trash; avoid it.
- **Follow-up sequence** — The autoresponder proper: interval-timed emails sent automatically to every subscriber.
- **Broadcast** — A single one-time send to the whole list; never recycled into the sequence.
- **Interval** — Days between sequence emails (0 = immediately, then 1/2/3-day gaps).
- **Use the email AS the content** — Stop using email as a mere notification ("check out my blog post → click away"); put the value *inside* the email itself.
- **Case studies** — The universal autoresponder fuel ("here's how Dan saved $6k/day with our steel tubing"); works in *any* industry, especially boring ones.
- **Cross-industry arbitrage** — A tactic common in one industry (autoresponders online) is unknown in another (steel tubing, faxes); importing it is an edge.
- **TLDR / linear layout** — In teardowns: kill side-buttons and multi-column layouts; go linear, top-to-bottom, add a "too long; didn't read" summary.
- **Quality score** — Mass-blasting irrelevant email tanks deliverability; ISPs push you to Promotions/Spam. Err on caution.
- **First-send pass** — A generic email gets ~20% open the *first* time ("industry standard"), then declines hard on repeats — don't mistake the first pass for a working email.

## Mental Models
- **Think of the autoresponder as a friendship, not a megaphone** — give-and-take like a spouse or parent, so they open you on sight.
- **Work backwards from the one action** — before writing, ask "if they could do *one* thing, what is it?" Write the Action/CTA section *first*, then build interest/desire up to it.
- **Use a story to make people *need* the product** — felt narrative (the laptop theft → "I sincerely wish I had Prey") outsells feature lists.
- **Let the universe niche you down** — throw spaghetti (emails, cold calling) at the wall; double down on whatever people clamor and pay for, instead of boxing yourself in upfront.
- **If it's not working, you have nothing to lose** — when a layout/template gets zero response, you have full permission to change everything ("What are we losing? Zero?"). Test the new version on 10–20% of the list.

## Anti-patterns
- **Designer "newsletter" emails** — pretty, image-heavy, multi-column layouts read as junk and lose to a plain-text email; it stings that the *text*, not the graphics, did the work.
- **Being the Annoying Friend** — only emailing to sell ("buy, buy, buy") = spammy; kills trust and opens.
- **Email-as-notification** — "check out my blog post" with the value living off-platform; people now expect the value *in* the email. (Nobody clicks "read more" then explores your blog — "go check your stats.")
- **Mass-blasting irrelevant content** — generic one-size-fits-all sends to a 200–300-segment list crater click rate (~1%) and wreck quality score/deliverability after the first-pass open.
- **Over-customizing without the data** — chasing per-user personalization you can't actually power yet; ship a single genuinely-useful email instead.
- **Hard-selling early** — pitching before warming up; the Sumo sequence dropped the *first* buy link only at email ~10.
- **Mailto-only / unclear CTA** — a bare `mailto:` link or "call this random number" with no instruction; tell them exactly what to do next.
- **Letting a launch die** — sending great launch emails once and never recycling them into an evergreen sequence.
- **Niching down first as a freelancer** — boxes you in before the market tells you what it wants.

## Key Takeaways
1. Engineer every email into the **A-pile**: plain/minimal look, useful content, recognizable sender name — the subject line matters far less than who it's from and whether past emails were good ("I don't spend much time on subject lines").
2. **Give first, sell last.** Warm strangers with free value (case studies work in *any* industry); only drop hints/links to your offer a few times across the sequence.
3. Know the two tools cold: **follow-ups** = the evergreen sequence (the money-maker), **broadcasts** = one-time sends. Build the launch once, strip dates, run it forever (**Constant Launch**).
4. Use **email as the content**, not a notification — the value lives inside.
5. Bolt on the high-leverage PS tricks: **whitelist** ("reply with your problems"), **demand-test** ("reply: I want it"), and route overflow to a **Google Form** or a **blog-comment thread** so feedback is streamlined (and socially-proven) instead of flooding your inbox.
6. Every sequence email ends on a **cliffhanger**; plan the full arc on one page first and write cliffhangers backwards.
7. When email copy fails, **work backwards from the single action, go linear, cut the graphics**, and test the rewrite on 10–20% of the list — zero current response means zero risk.
