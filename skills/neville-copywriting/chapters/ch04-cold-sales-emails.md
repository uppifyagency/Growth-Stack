# Chapter 4: Cold Sales Emails

## Core Idea
A cold email exists to get *one* small action, not to tell your life story. Make it personal, give them something useful (or stay short), and end with a single bolded request. Copywriting "replicates you" — fix a salesperson's email and the same effort that landed 1 sale in 10 now lands 3 in 10, tripling revenue with zero extra outreach.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 4 Steps Of A Great Cold Sales Email** — the master checklist for any cold outreach. (1) **Make it personal** — research who you're emailing, never "to whom it may concern." (2) **Give them something OR keep it short** — be a "Giving Gary," not a "Leeching Larry." (3) **Use a simple email outline** (the AIDA formula in disguise). (4) **End with a single request** — one bolded ask. Use for any unsolicited sales, pitch, or job-outreach email.
- **The Cold Email Outline** (a re-skin of **AIDA / A-I-D-A**) — four blocks: **Intro** ("Hey, I'm Jason") → **What this product will do for them** → **What will happen if they get this** → **When can we talk**. Use it as the skeleton so you stop writing rambling paragraphs. Maps Attention/Interest/Desire/Action onto plain, send-able sections.
- **Giving Gary vs. Leeching Larry** — the personality test for body copy. *Giving Gary:* "You sell concrete? We know a method that saves 40% on production — let me show you." *Leeching Larry:* "We are sellers of concrete. We've been around since 1979, started in Cleveland…" Use Gary's framing whenever you catch yourself describing yourself instead of their benefit.

## Key Concepts
- **Personalization lift** — swapping "to whom it may concern" for the person's real name gives a 50–100% jump in opens; it "gets your foot in the door."
- **Research plugins** — tools like Rapportive-style Gmail plugins surface a stranger's company, title, and LinkedIn before you write, so you can personalize fast.
- **"Give them something"** — lead with a concrete, quantified benefit to *them* (e.g. "$24,000/year in pure profit savings"), not your history.
- **Custom proof artifact** — a tiny personalized chart/list of *their* equipment, costs, or rooms, built from publicly available info, that proves you did the homework.
- **The single bolded ask** — pick one action (call / click / reply with a number / book a time) and highlight it so there is zero ambiguity about the next step.
- **Resume-as-bullets** — when credibility *is* the offer (job/consulting pitch), strip the brag-paragraph into a short bullet list and put the "what I'll do for you" line first.
- **The follow-up deliverable** — offer to send a PDF/plan ("reply and I'll send you a doc on how to improve X") to lower the friction of the first reply.

## Mental Models
- **Think of a cold email as a single transaction, not a brochure** — you are *asking for something*, so don't waste their time; their interest is in themselves, not your company.
- **Use "give or keep it short" as a binary gate** — if you have a real value-bomb, give it; if you don't, the email should be one or two sentences ("I'm a hotel consultant — any hiring going on?") which beats a long all-about-me message.
- **Simpler = more money for less work** — the IPVM and account-manager rewrites won by becoming shorter, more casual, more personal, with one clear ask; "you do less work and make more money."
- **Run the conversion math before judging effort** — 200 sends/month at 1-in-10 vs 3-in-10 is the difference between modest and tripled sales, so a few minutes of personalization is the highest-leverage work you can do.

## Anti-patterns
- **Shotgun spam** — blasting an un-personalized template to everyone "hoping it lands." More total work than targeted sending, and most recipients won't even read it.
- **Big blocks of text** — long emails to "just get a meeting" bury the ask and get deleted; nearly every example email was cut by 60–80%.
- **All-about-me copy** — listing founding year, city, and history ("I am this, I am this…") instead of what you can do for the reader.
- **Multiple competing asks** — "30 different things going on" with several links confuses the reader; never give more than one option.
- **Vague or buried CTA** — "please let me know if there's a time that works" with no direct instruction. Replace with a blunt, specific ask.
- **Redundant sentences** — saying the same benefit three ways; collapse it into one interesting line.

## Key Takeaways
1. Always personalize: real name minimum, ideally a researched detail — it can double readership and is the cheapest win available.
2. Lead with *their* benefit, quantified ("$24k/year saved"); if you have nothing concrete to give, make the email two sentences.
3. Structure every cold email on the Intro → What it does for them → What happens if they get it → When can we talk outline (AIDA in plain clothes).
4. End with exactly one bolded action and make it effortless to take.
5. When you're the product (job/consulting), put the "here's what I'll do for you" line first and demote your credentials to a tight bullet list.
6. Cut hard — delete every sentence that doesn't advance the ask; concise reads as confident and converts better.
7. Treat tripling reply rate as a copy problem, not a volume problem — the same sends with better copy multiply revenue.
