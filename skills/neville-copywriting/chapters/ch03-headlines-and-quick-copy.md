# Chapter 3: Headlines & Quick Copy

## Core Idea
Headlines exist to make the reader read the *next* line — but they are a "trick" with a short shelf-life: once an audience sees you repeatedly, **authenticity and relevance beat bombast**, and the real engine of conversion is the body — a single block of personal, educational copy that pulls one reader down a **slippery slope** to **one** action. Stop selling the product; sell what it does for *them*.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The CBI Method (Competitive / Benefit-driven / Inspirational)** — three "lenses" for generating headline variants, each aimed at a different buyer psychology. **Competitive** = beat-the-competition, aggressive, type-A ("86% faster than regular WordPress"). **Benefit-driven** = analytical/rational, often a testimonial or a stat ("increased our conversions by 328%"). **Inspirational** = "you can do it too" ("even non-nerds can build a WordPress site"). Use when you have writer's block on a headline: run one decent headline through all three lenses, then test. (Neville notes he invented the acronym on the spot — it's "headline juice," not a rigid formula.)
- **Socially-Proven Headlines** — when you can't think of anything, mine social sites (Digg, Reddit, Twitter) by searching your product keyword and sorting by top/most-upvoted; the crowd already validated which angles grab attention ("How to get rid of that pocket bulge" for a wallet store). Adapt — keep relevant, don't blindly mislead. Use when starting from blank.
- **AIDA (Attention · Interest · Desire · Action)** — "tattoo this on your head." The 1800s sales spine for any email/page. **Attention**: an odd opener ("Hey, I screwed up"). **Interest**: interesting facts / a story (finger lights aren't just for ravers — nightlights, Halloween, stop-motion). **Desire**: pile on why it's cool/useful. **Action**: ONE explicit, obvious instruction. Use it as a writing scaffold — literally type A/I/D/A as section headers and fill them in.
- **The Slippery Slope (a.k.a. slippery slide)** — structure copy so each sentence makes the next irresistible; "the point of the first line is to make them read the second" (Ogilvy). Fuel it with *education or a story*. Use for any length, especially long copy.
- **Make Your Own "Bob"** — invent a named, fully-attributed avatar of your single ideal reader (age, gender, income, beliefs, what they want to learn) and write to that one person. Use before writing a word.
- **The Wizard's "Overheard on the Street" Trick** — list things that, overheard on a sidewalk, would make you go "WHAT?" ("My left testicle makes more money than my right one"), then fuse one with your product to forge an attention-grabbing headline. Use to generate hooks from scratch.
- **Joe Sugarman's Length Rule** — cheap/easy item → short copy; expensive item needing convincing → long copy. Paired maxim: "Copy is like a skirt — long enough to cover the essentials, short enough to keep it interesting."

## Key Concepts
- **Headline math** — ~8 of 10 read the headline, only ~2 of 10 read on; the headline decides whether the article gets read at all.
- **New / novel / unique / helpful** — the four psychological triggers people pay attention to; aim subject lines at these.
- **Authentic headline** — the only "type" that survives repeated exposure: relevant, lowercase-casual, not over-promising; reads as if Neville personally hit send.
- **Mobile truncation** — phones cut off long subject lines, so keep them short and front-load the meaning ("pricing calculator," not a clever tease).
- **One call to action / one link** — people execute one action well; two actions muddy and lose them. Bury one link at the bottom of the slope.
- **BFB ("Big Fucking Button")** — Google's term; make the action so obvious that someone 10 steps back can tell what to do in one second.
- **Educate or entertain (ideally both)** — give away so much usable value (D'Angelo's "why a wuss can't attract women," Ramit's free how-to) that readers save your emails and come to you first when ready to buy.
- **Get inside the prospect's head** — know what they want to *learn*; everyone reads your stuff to learn something.
- **The P.S.** — almost everyone reads it; bold it and put a payoff/claim there to pull skimmers back up.

## Mental Models
- **Think of a headline as a trick, not a strategy** — split-tests show "tiny change → 900% lift," but the lift fades with repetition; even Ogilvy (who coined headline importance) ran headline-less product/image ads. Long-term trust > any single clever line.
- **Write like you talk, to one person** — throw away academic/corporate voice; use lots of "I" and "you," bold the words you'd emphasize out loud ("oh my GOD you got married"). Record yourself and transcribe if needed.
- **Be ruthless: if you're bored reading it, cut it** — boredom anywhere breaks the slippery slope; hitting delete costs nothing (undo exists). A good email is one you re-read and enjoy.
- **Show the product, sometimes** — courage to drop the headline and let an image or plain body copy carry the sell (Ogilvy's cookware/McGraw-Hill ads).

## Anti-patterns
- **Clickbait / trick subjects** ("Re:", "hey…", "you won't believe what happens next") — works once, erodes trust, gets you ignored or banned. Platforms killed these in 2013-14.
- **Talking about your product in the headline** — "no one cares about your product; they care what it can do for them."
- **"Welcome to [App]" / coupon-in-subject** — announces "I'm selling you" and screams you're not special; instant delete.
- **Reusing one winning headline forever** — every formula fades with the same audience; switch it up while staying relevant.
- **Multiple links / clown-page formatting** — pretty image-heavy newsletters can win clicks yet make zero money (House of Rave: $40 revenue vs $80 cost); the ugly text block did 12× sales.
- **Worrying about the 1% who'll bristle at casual/fun copy** — the bigger risk is the 99% who won't read a stodgy email at all.
- **Boilerplate corporate jargon** — kills the personal connection that makes email work.

## Key Takeaways
1. Generate headlines via **CBI lenses** + **socially-proven** mining + the **overheard-on-the-street** trick; then test and never lean on one formula.
2. For *email subject lines specifically*, don't be clever — be short, casual, lowercase, mobile-safe, and relevant to what's inside.
3. Build every piece on **AIDA** running down a **slippery slope** fueled by a story or genuine education.
4. Define **Bob** and write to him alone; use "I"/"you," bold for vocal emphasis, keep it fun and personal.
5. **One** clear action / one **BFB** link at the bottom; add a bolded **P.S.** for skimmers.
6. Match length to price (Sugarman); cut anything that bores you; the ugly text block usually out-earns the beautiful one.
7. Sell the benefit to *them*, never the product to you.
