# Chapter 2: Core Writing Techniques

## Core Idea
Copywriting is "the act of writing words on a page that convert better" — and the craft is mostly **giving, formatting, and following a structure**, not clever wordsmithing. Stop asking; start giving. Write like you talk to a friend, format ruthlessly for comprehension, and run everything through one repeatable four-step skeleton (AIDA). "No one cares about your product. They care what it can do for them."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **AIDA** (Attention · Interest · Desire · Action) — the bar-none number-one formula; a four-part sales process that works for emails, sales pages, ads, video scripts, phone scripts, even getting a kid to clean a room. **When:** every single thing you write. **How:** open a doc, type the four words bolded, write one sentence under each (Attention = catchy + relevant hook/question; Interest = interesting facts/uses/stats/quotes; Desire = make them internally *want* it by showing a better life / projecting results; Action = one specific CTA — click, reply, PayPal, enter email). Then flesh each section out. Kills writer's block: you fill a template instead of facing a blank page.
- **Give-Give-Give, not Ask-Ask-Ask** — the core mentality shift. **When:** any outreach/sales email. **How:** lead by sending genuinely useful stuff (a report, vendor recs, an insight) with *nothing* being asked for; the response rate flips from mostly-rejections to mostly-replies.
- **The 70/30 Rule** — 70% interesting content, 30% sales. **When:** newsletters, deal emails, any recurring audience comms. **How:** teach/entertain first (use the product yourself, then explain how it helps), then "oh by the way, we have a deal." Never sell-sell-sell.
- **The CBI Method** (Competitive · Benefit-driven · Inspirational) — three "lenses" to generate headline variants, each appealing to a different buyer. **When:** stuck on a headline/subject line. **How:** rewrite the same headline through each lens — Competitive (beat-the-competition, aggressive, "A" personalities, often a stat: "86% faster…"); Benefit-driven (analytical/rational, works great with a testimonial or stat); Inspirational ("even non-nerds can… / you can do it too"). Don't ship one formula repeatedly — generate "headline juice."
- **Socially-Proven Headlines** — mine already-upvoted content for subject lines. **When:** no headline ideas at all. **How:** search your topic on Reddit, Quora, Dig, Twitter; copy the highest-upvoted titles into a swipe file; rewrite them for your audience.
- **Write Subject 1–4 First** — always draft 4 (up to 10) subject lines before writing the body, then pick or split-test the best. **How:** even a deliberately bad first one primes the brain; one good line out of four lets you craft the email around it.
- **Short vs Long Copy rule** — "Long copy is usually better, but it depends." **When:** deciding length. **How:** match copy length to price + complexity + how many questions/objections must be answered. Email-signup = short (1–2 benefits); cheap/known item (yo-yo) = short; ~$100 / must install = medium; expensive/complex ($1k+ software) = long. Cover the essentials, keep interest. Length never matters if the reader is genuinely interested.
- **Features vs Benefits** — don't sell what it *is*, sell what it *does for them*. ("No one cares about your product.")

## Key Concepts
- **Slippery slide / slippery slope** — the job of every line is to get them to read the next line; build momentum so they slide down the page.
- **Formatting and styling** — most of a copywriter's actual job; raising comprehension without writing new copy (bold, bullets, color, spacing, captions, tables, ~500–550px text width).
- **The caption trick** — small italic, often grey, text under an image; "drastically increases comprehension" and signals it's a caption, not body text.
- **Color coding** — red = bad/loss, green = good (plus emoji ☺/☹) to make a before/after instantly legible.
- **BFB — "Big F***ing Button"** — make the one desired action unmistakable; Google's "10-steps-back, one-second" test: if you can't tell what to do in one second, the page failed.
- **The one-secret / Purpose-of-email line** — write the single goal of the message at the top of your doc so every line serves it.
- **Casual copy** — "write like you talk, not like your English-class teacher"; communication is just moving info brain-to-brain — fun + casual wins.
- **"Sitting-down-at-the-computer-itis"** — the reflex to turn formal and boring the moment you touch a keyboard.
- **The PS / PPS** — people often jump straight to it; ideal spot for the guarantee, urgency, or a final desire-booster.
- **Build an asset** — a thorough, search-informed article ranks and compounds; a random little post doesn't.

## Mental Models
- **Pretend you're talking to one friend at lunch** — if you wouldn't say "turnkey integrated convergent platform" to a friend, don't write it. Keep "your suit jacket on": casual but still professional, not goofy.
- **Use AIDA as a fill-in-the-blank template** — think of a blank page as four labeled boxes to fill, not prose to invent.
- **Read it from 10 steps back** — if the action isn't obvious in one second, redesign (BFB principle).
- **70/30 is the CarMax test** — the salesman who teaches first and pitches last gets the sale; the one who opens with "buy buy buy" gets pushed away.

## Anti-patterns
- **Asking before giving** — "I really want you to buy this" / "welcome to [App], thanks for your interest" — boilerplate no one reads; pure asking gets deleted.
- **Talking about yourself / your product** ("me, me, me") in cold outreach — to a reporter/prospect who doesn't know you, this goes straight to trash.
- **Tricking the open** — fake "RE:" subjects or "hey man…" baiting works once, then "eventually you will piss people off."
- **Putting the coupon/price in the subject line** — screams "I'm selling you," everyone gets it, reader feels un-special.
- **Burying the call to action** mid-email or having none — readers don't know what to do.
- **Oversized images** — huge images distract and confuse; shrink them when they don't need to be big.
- **Reusing one rigid headline formula** — stops keeping people "on the edge of their seats."
- **Formal/jargon "English-class" copy** — boring = unread = no conversions.
- **Over-worrying the 1%** — fearing the rare "this wasn't professional enough" complaint and writing stiff copy for everyone as a result.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run *everything* through AIDA — it removes writer's block and reliably lifts sales.
2. Give first, ask last; aim for ~70% value / 30% pitch.
3. Lead with what it does for *them*; "no one cares about your product."
4. Formatting alone (bullets, bold, color, captions, narrow column) raises comprehension before you write a word.
5. Draft 4+ subject lines first; use CBI lenses or socially-proven titles when stuck.
6. Match copy length to price/complexity/objections — not to a fixed rule.
7. Write casual, like one friend talking to another, and make the action a BFB.
