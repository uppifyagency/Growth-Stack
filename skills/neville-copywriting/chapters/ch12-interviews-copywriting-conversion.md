# Chapter 12: Interviews I — Copywriting, Conversion & SEO

## Core Idea
Persuasion on the page is a *system*, not inspiration: get the reader on a **slippery slide** (attention → interest → desire), write the way you talk, strip jargon, and funnel everyone to **one** action. The same logic powers SEO and audience-building — make genuinely useful, *timeless* content, then go get links and own the audience you build, because traffic you rent (social) disappears and traffic you own (blog + email list) compounds.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) — Neville's spine for any email/page. *When:* every piece meant to make someone act. *How:* grab **A**ttention (hook/headline), build **I**nterest with genuinely interesting facts, stoke **D**esire by going on about why it's cool / who else uses it ("plumbers buy these from me, of all people"), then end on a single explicit **A**ction. He literally types "Attention / Interest / Desire / Action" down the page and fills each in. Also written **ADA** in shorthand.
- **The Slippery Slide** (a.k.a. "slippery slope") — *When:* you need the reader to keep reading instead of bouncing. *How:* the first sentences exist only to get the next sentence read; either **educate** (give damn-good information) or **tell a story** so they "just can't stop," sliding to one CTA at the bottom. Misleading hooks ("naked girls over here → buy email software") break the slide.
- **One Call To Action** — *When:* always, vs. the instinct to offer many options. *How:* people execute *one* action well; multiple buttons/links split attention and convert worse. Give the customer one simple, unmistakable next step (Neville's first AppSumo rewrite cut "lots of images, lots of buttons" down to one).
- **The Headline Formula (combine + borrow socially-proven headlines)** — *When:* staring at a blank subject line. *How:* (1) keep a running list of attention-grabbing phrases you hear "on the street"; (2) pull headlines that already went viral on **Digg/Reddit** (socially pre-proven), and (3) **combine** them with your product angle (e.g. "custom baseball gloves" × a viral headline). Make it relevant, not misleading.
- **Writing Modules (for Timeless Content)** — *When:* you want one post to keep working for years instead of dying after a traffic bump. *How:* treat a post as movable **modules** (custom images/GIFs, screenshots, big lists/tables, your own scored ranking of options, quotes, embedded tools); to improve any post, "just add some modules." Original modules (hand-built lists, your own "Doug score") are defensible — competitors can't take them without directly copying.
- **Make Content, Get Links** — *When:* SEO from scratch. *How:* the whole game reduces to two moves — produce content good enough to deserve links, then do **outreach** to earn them. Ignore the "3,000 ranking factors" rabbit hole; people who chase it "have no chance of ranking."
- **AIDA Cold-Email Outreach (for backlinks)** — *When:* pitching a site for a link. *How:* hyper-**personalized**, custom one-paragraph email — subject line uses their name + what they get; lead with the value; one clear link/CTA. Prospect on the fly: if they shared a third-party copywriting checklist, pitch *them* a copywriting checklist/template. Spammy, un-personalized outreach gets you "outed."

## Key Concepts
- **Timeless (evergreen) content** — a post engineered to keep earning search traffic and sign-ups indefinitely, vs. the "dip" where each new post spikes then fades.
- **The dip** — the inevitable traffic decay of one-off, event-driven content (stunts, news) with nothing to sustain it.
- **Copywriting vs. content writing** — copy is *denser and more valuable per word*, lives on bottom-funnel/landing pages, aims at a specific action (often a purchase); content marketing is longer, top-funnel, ~"70% content / 30% sales."
- **Own the customer** — Shopify = you own the buyer's data/relationship; Amazon = you don't (Amazon owns the customer), so split effort but never depend on rented platforms.
- **A blog is an asset; social media is fleeting** — social retains attention, the blog/email list is where people go for information and where you can direct traffic later; skipping the blog is a "disservice."
- **Validation before production** — crowdfunding (Kickstarter) is for proving people will pay *before* you manufacture, not a perpetual store.
- **Verified-review reading** — judge a product (and copy proof) by *verified* reviews; tools like FakeSpot flag reviews that all arrived one day or from one improbable country.
- **Suffer-then-snowball** — early content gets no views; you create "for your own joy" long enough to gather data, then content compounds.
- **Don't fake-freshen dates** — re-stamping "updated 2020" without real updates no longer works; update only when the topic genuinely changed.

## Mental Models
- **Think of a post as Lego modules, not prose.** "Is this post any good?" becomes "which modules can I add?" — turns vague editing into a checklist and builds defensibility.
- **Write like you'd explain it to a friend, not a boardroom.** Jargon and "buttoned-down business" tone are *why* no one reads marketing emails; conversational beats corporate.
- **Use Kickstarter as a paid focus group.** People "plunk down real money," so a funded campaign = real validation; ~30–38% of backers come organically from Kickstarter's own audience. Don't relaunch the same product there.
- **Treat the subject line as the first A in AIDA** — but never announce the sale in it ("welcome," "I'm trying to sell you" = instant delete).
- **SEO is a 10-year compounding game** — brand, design, copy, and outreach "tie together"; with no topical authority you're "winging it" and any ranking is "pure luck."

## Anti-patterns
- **Multiple CTAs / button-soup pages** — splits attention; people convert on one clear action, not a menu.
- **Jargon and corporate tone** — kills the slippery slide; readers bail in the first sentences.
- **Misleading hooks** — clickbait that doesn't pay off ("naked girls" → email tool) burns trust and breaks the slide.
- **Event-stunt content with no evergreen backbone** — produces "the dip"; effort evaporates with the traffic spike.
- **Selling the offer in the subject line** — flags "I'm being sold," triggers the delete reflex.
- **Spammy, templated link outreach** — gets you publicly outed; only personalized, context-aware pitches earn links.
- **Building only on rented platforms** — pure Instagram/Amazon presence with no owned blog/email list = no asset and no way to redirect traffic.
- **Chasing "3,000 ranking factors"** instead of the two that matter (good content + links).
- **Re-dating old posts to fake freshness** — Google sees through it; update only on real change.
- **Relaunching the same product on Kickstarter every year** — just open a store; Kickstarter is for *new*/uncertain products needing validation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run every email/page through **AIDA on a slippery slide** ending in exactly **one** action.
2. Write conversationally and cut jargon — that alone is why most marketing copy is unread.
3. Steal-and-combine **socially-proven headlines** (Digg/Reddit) with your angle; keep a swipe list of street-level phrases.
4. Build **timeless content from modules** — hand-made lists, images, your own scored rankings — so posts compound and stay defensible.
5. SEO = **make link-worthy content + personalized outreach**; ignore the ranking-factor noise and expect a multi-year horizon.
6. **Own your audience** (blog + email list); social is rented and fleeting, Amazon owns your customer.
7. **Validate before you manufacture** (Kickstarter/pre-sell) and trust only verified reviews as proof.
