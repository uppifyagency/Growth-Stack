# Chapter 6: Content Marketing & Content Writing

## Core Idea
Educate first, sell second — and write content as a **timeless asset that gets better over time**, not as disposable posts you race to replace. The volume-and-velocity treadmill ("post more to avoid the dip") keeps you flat forever; thoughtful, module-rich, angle-driven posts compound into traffic, signups, and sales for years. The single biggest content failure is not bad copy — it's a post that asks the reader to do nothing next.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 70/30 Rule** — 70% genuinely interesting/useful content, 30% sales. *When:* every email, newsletter, blog post, or page where you eventually want to sell. *How:* research (or personally use) the product, teach how it makes the reader's life better, *then* slide in "oh, by the way, we have a deal." Counterintuitive: don't "sell sell sell then mention the use" — help first, sell last. This is the formula Neville used to scale AppSumo emails from 50k to 750k+ readers.
- **Features vs Benefits** — *When:* any time you describe a product to a non-expert end user (experts already infer benefits; first-time buyers don't). *How:* pair each feature with the relatable outcome it produces. "8GB RAM" → "opening files/browsers is super fast, zero lag." "50,000 miles" → "two free round-trip tickets anywhere in the US." Use both together — keep the spec for credibility, add the benefit for meaning.
- **The 4-Step Content Revamp** — the repeatable process to salvage a non-performing/page-8 post *without writing new copy*. **(1) Create an angle** (find an under-served sub-niche / long-tail the big players ignore — e.g. "romantic things to do in Rio" vs the dominated "things to do in Rio"). **(2) Sex up the headline** (Neville always writes **four** headline variants; bake the angle's keywords in; keep the URL slug). **(3) Make a grabbing image** that fits the new angle. **(4) Add helpful modules** (the real lift — see below). Most people stop at step 2 and stay invisible.
- **Blog Content Modules** — treat content like a sales page: movable building blocks you snap onto any post to make it better instead of "rewriting." The menu: **cheat sheet / summary / table of contents** (one move = three modules + a skeleton outline); a **custom score** (Doug-score style — make up a proprietary metric, e.g. "Rio Romance score"); a **custom map** with your own photos; an **embedded picture gallery**; a **cost breakdown table** (WordPress *TablePress*, sortable); a **calculator** ($100 Upwork build); a **generator** (borderline software — does the task instead of teaching it); **original research / big lists & tables** (scrape & synthesize data nobody else has — copywriter salaries, time-to-fame for YouTubers); **shareable templates** (a Google Doc/Sheet — "official"-feeling, link-laced, droppable in Facebook groups as a free gift); a **PDF download** of the whole post (just a shareable Google Doc, gated by email); and always a **call-to-action / next action**.
- **Tale of Two Blogs** — the cautionary frame. *NevBlog:* 1,000+ posts, no planning, post-more-to-fight-the-dip → flat/declining for 10 years, $0. *CopywritingCourse:* ~141 posts, 1–3/month, research + best-in-world intent → surpassed NevBlog's lifetime traffic within months and monetizes. *When:* deciding cadence vs depth — choose depth.

## Key Concepts
- **The dip** — the inevitable traffic decline after a post's spike; the trap that drives mindless posting. Timeless content beats the treadmill.
- **Long tail of SEO** — many low-volume (0–20 searches) but human-varied phrases that sum to real traffic the big players miss; the home of the "angle."
- **Timeless / evergreen asset** — a post you can forever update in place; think of each post as a product/asset that *appreciates*, not a one-off that decays.
- **Featured snippet / zero-click result** — Google answers in-page (e.g. "how old is Trump"); rankings can "slip" while real traffic holds. Still worth targeting — Google *pulls* that answer from a source page.
- **Plain-English TLDR** — for technical/scientific pages, list the impressive "sciencey" detail for credibility, then restate it for the layman ("what that means is…") in a separate TLDR table.
- **Work backwards** — start from the desired end action (signup, sale, exposure) and design the content to drive it; never start from "we need a post."
- **Credibility module** — testimonials + results + an "about the teacher / braggadocious" block; a page of pure claims with no proof fails.
- **Big-content-first** — write one large asset, then break it into many social posts that link *back* to it.
- **Platform risk** — Delicious, Digg, MySpace, Periscope, Google+, Facebook Pages all peaked and died; building solely on a platform is "a countdown until you're fucked." Own your website.
- **Best-resource-in-the-world test** — Google's job is to point to the best resource for a query; write to *be* that resource.

## Mental Models
- **Think of content as modules, not prose.** When asked "is this good?", don't debate subjective copy — just snap on more modules (TOC, score, map, calculator) until it's undeniably better.
- **Think of each post as a product/asset that gets better over time** — fluid and editable, unlike a printed book; you can improve a live post in one click.
- **Use the car-salesman test for the 70/30 Rule** — be Jason who helps for a long day then offers the deal, not the guy who shouts "buy a car?" at the door.
- **Use social platforms as temporary free traffic to your owned site** — borrow the audience, don't build the house on rented land.

## Anti-patterns
- **Sell, sell, sell up front** — opening with the pitch repels readers; violates 70/30.
- **Listing features with no benefits** — confuses first-time buyers who can't translate specs into outcomes.
- **Fighting the dip with volume** — more unplanned posts = a flat treadmill and no compounding.
- **Stopping at "make it longer / add images / rewrite the headline"** — small SEO bump at best (page 8 → page 4 = still zero clicks); skip the modules and you've done nothing.
- **No call-to-action at the bottom** — the cardinal sin; the reader bounces back to Google and the interaction is dead.
- **Copying a competitor move-for-move** — kills differentiation and "makes you unhappy"; they may not even be doing anything good.
- **Wall-of-text technical copy with no plain-English translation** — impressive but unreadable to the actual buyer.
- **Building your audience solely on one social platform** — guaranteed saturation/decline outside your control.

## Key Takeaways
1. Lead with help (70%), close with the offer (30%) — in every email, page, and post.
2. Always pair every feature with its benefit; write for the first-time buyer, not yourself.
3. To rank a dead post, run the 4-step revamp — angle → headline (write 4) → image → modules — usually with zero new copywriting.
4. Differentiate with proprietary modules competitors can't lift: a made-up score, original scraped data, a calculator, a sortable table, a shareable Google-Doc template.
5. End every piece with a single clear next action (signup, download, follow, buy).
6. Win the long tail by choosing an angle the dominant sites ignore — don't fight TripAdvisor head-on.
7. Build the big asset first, then atomize it into social posts that link back; own the destination.
