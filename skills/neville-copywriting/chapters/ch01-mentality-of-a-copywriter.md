# Chapter 1: Mentality of a Copywriter

## Core Idea
Copywriting is "the act of writing words on a page that convert better" — and the entire game is a mindset flip from **ask, ask, ask** to **give, give, give**. Same envelope, same audience; only the words change, and the words decide whether you get rejected or rewarded. Master the mentality first; the formulas come second.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action) — the core four-step sales process behind every page, email, ad, or pitch. **When to use:** any time you must sell a product *or* an idea and don't know where to start (kills writer's block). **How:** open a doc, write 4–5 candidate **subject lines** + a one-line **"Purpose of email"** (e.g. "get Jason to call us"), then draft a table with one sentence under each of A/I/D/A — *Attention* (headline/question/their name), *Interest* (3–7 interesting facts/benefits, max ~7 bullets before readers trail off), *Desire* (make them *really* want it — logical "$200/hr × 300 hrs = $60k lost" or emotional "look cooler"), *Action* (one single instruction — call, reply, click). Then delete the A/I/D/A labels and what remains is a finished sales letter.
- **Give-Give-Give (vs. Ask-Ask-Ask)** — the Bobby parable. **When to use:** all outreach/cold email. **How:** lead with value the reader can use even if they never buy (a free report, three vetted vendors, a cost-saving idea). Remove every "buy from me / our company is dedicated" line. Giving is counterintuitive for salespeople but flips reply rates from near-zero to >50%.
- **No One Cares About You. They Care About Themselves** — the prime directive. **When to use:** every headline and opening line. **How:** delete the founder story / "we scour the globe" intro; rewrite the same copy as the *reader's* story and problem ("Dave's shirt bunches up on his 30-min commute…"). They'll look you up later *if* they get interested.
- **Make Your Customer 'Bob'** — write to one composite-average person, not "everyone." **When to use:** before writing any product description or email. **How:** profile the real buyer via your own calls/emails/data (age, gender, daily routine, kids?, deepest fears, discretionary income), then ask "**what can I help them with the most?**" Re-segment toward whoever actually pays (House of Rave: pivot from broke 16-yr-old ravers to 35-yr-old wedding-planner moms).
- **The Envelope Exercise** — two identical envelopes to "mom"; only the words differ, producing love or fury. A mental proof that copy is the single highest-impact lever you can pull.

## Key Concepts
- **Copywriting** — writing (or images/video built on the same underlying mechanism) that *converts better*; the difference between rejection and a sale.
- **Communication** — "simply getting information from one brain into another"; fun/interesting transfer beats boring/formal transfer.
- **Sitting-down-at-the-computer-itis** — the reflex (a leftover from English class) to switch into stiff, formal, "boring mode" the moment you touch a keyboard.
- **Casual copy** — write as if explaining to one friend over lunch, "suit jacket on": professional and real, not goofy.
- **Polite/relevant tangent rule** (Sugarman) — "the point of the headline is to get them to read the second line; the point of the second line is to get them to read the third…" — a slippery momentum from one line to the next.
- **Composite-average customer (Bob)** — a single fictional buyer that is the statistical average of your real customers; the one person you address.
- **Logical vs. emotional desire** — two levers in AIDA's Desire step: justify the cost (math/ROI) *and/or* trigger feeling (status, relief, fear).
- **"Does it add to the message?"** — the one-question editing test for every wacky/funny sentence; yes → keep, no → cut, "it's just words."

## Mental Models
- **Use give-give-give when** you'd instinctively ask — flip it; the absence of any ask is what makes Mr. Moneybags reply.
- **Think of your reader as Bob**, not a crowd: "if you sell to everyone, you've sold to no one." Write one email to one person.
- **Use humor only when it serves the message** — not every story is funny ("my grandma just died" isn't a punchline). Be informative first; entertaining is a side benefit.
- **Think of each sentence as a hook to the next** — convoluted, multi-branch tangents lose the reader, and a lost reader is lost revenue.

## Anti-patterns
- **Leading with yourself / your company** ("Hi, we are XYZ, founded by Josh who grew up in Portland…") — reviewers throw it out instantly; it does nothing for the buyer.
- **Corporate word-salad** (Witchbox: "turnkey integrated convergent technology platform…") — nobody knows what you do; you'd never say it to a friend.
- **Too wacky** — dressing/talking like a "silly jackass" or piling tangent on tangent (typewriter → time machine → Gloria Swanson) confuses and kills conversions.
- **Formal "boring mode"** — grammatically perfect, monotone copy that puts readers to sleep like the dull professor.
- **Asking too early / asking at all in outreach** — the default sales email is all ask and offers nothing in return.
- **Writing to "everyone"** — generic descriptions convert no one; failing to re-target toward who actually has money to spend.

## Key Takeaways
1. Flip the mindset to **give-give-give**; remove every ask from first contact and lead with usable value.
2. Open with the reader, never yourself — **no one cares about you, they care about themselves**.
3. Run everything through **AIDA**; one sentence per step first, then flesh out — you'll rarely face writer's block again.
4. In Desire, hit both **logical** (ROI math) and **emotional** (status/relief/fear) buttons; give one clear **Action**.
5. Write **casual**, "suit jacket on" — to one friend, not a crowd — but **not too wacky**; keep a sentence only if it adds to the message.
6. Define **Bob** from real customer data and write every word to that single composite buyer; re-segment toward whoever actually pays.
