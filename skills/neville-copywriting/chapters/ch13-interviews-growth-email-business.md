# Chapter 13: Interviews II — Growth, Email & Building Businesses

## Core Idea
Build on channels you *own*, not platforms you rent. Across Noah Kagan, Sam Parr and Peep Laja, the recurring lesson is the same: **email + your own audience are the durable assets** — everything else (Facebook, Instagram, SEO rankings, a domain name) is borrowed reach that the landlord can revoke. Find the thing you'd do for free, build an audience *first*, then figure out what to sell them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Email + YouTube — "the only two channels that matter"** (Noah Kagan) — Treat email as the controllable, scalable line to your audience ("I can get on my phone and send an email and say go buy this"); treat YouTube as the largest *untapped* discovery engine that "brings you the audience for free." Use when choosing where to invest finite time. How: every other channel (FB "is fucked," Twitter engagement "marginal," TikTok hard to convert, Patreon "stupid") is at the mercy of an algorithm — pick the two you can own and play.
- **The Pirate-Ship / Landlord model of channels** (Sam Parr) — Email is a ship you own: "every email is a little wind in our sails," you take aboard whoever you want and go wherever you want. Social is "a store in a building where the landlord jacks up the rent every 18 months." Use when deciding what to build a media business *on*. How: pick the medium that has changed least in 20 years (email) so your asset compounds instead of decaying (FB organic reach went 90% → 50% → ~4%).
- **"Find the thing you'd do for free, then monetize it"** (Noah Kagan) — The people who last did it before there was money in it (early bloggers, Mr. Beast making videos his mom told him to quit, Vsauce). Use when picking what to create. How: notice what you naturally do in the morning for free; make *that* the job. Bloggers who entered "just to make money" rarely lasted.
- **FFR — Fast, Fun, Repeatable** (Noah Kagan) — A content-selection filter applied *after* confirming a topic is already proven popular (mined from top Reddit posts + trending YouTube). Run candidate topics through three gates: **Fast** to produce (low production lift), **Fun** to make, **Repeatable** (a format you can run weekly forever, like Shark Tank). Use to decide *which* videos to actually make.
- **Sex Cash Theory** (Noah Kagan) — From the film world: some projects make money ("cash," Titanic), some earn credibility/creative satisfaction ("sex," indie roles). Use to consciously balance a content slate. The goal is the **Venn diagram** of "what people want" ∩ "what I want to make" — make the same video that reaches 1,000,000 instead of 1,000.
- **Chasing Analytics** (Noah Kagan, via Eric of Beardbrand) — The failure mode of over-optimizing for views: a brand that chases analytics becomes "barbershop videos over and over" — huge views, but those viewers don't buy. Use as a warning. How: weigh *what you want to make × who your customer is × what gets views* — and watch the analytic that actually maps to revenue, not raw views.
- **The "One View" concept** (Noah Kagan) — A single viewer can change your life years later (the CEO who followed his blog; James Altucher replying off a mailing-list email). Use to justify making content even when view counts look "low." How: just put yourself out there — putting it out (blog/audio/video) is what creates the chance encounters.
- **The 10-Year Rule** (Noah Kagan) — On average it takes ~10 years to make a million dollars; flukes exist but plan for the long arc. Use to set expectations and to force you to "start right now."
- **Bottom-Fourth-of-the-Résumé / "interesting person = interesting writer"** (Sam Parr) — To spot good writers, ask about the *bottom* of the résumé (the philosophy class, the ultramarathon, the motorcycle racing) and see if they can tell a great story about it. Use when hiring writers. How: a great writer is a curious, interesting *person* first; check their Twitter to see if they can educate + entertain inside a 140-char constraint.
- **Validation by Signals (six signal sources)** (Sam Parr's Ideation Bootcamp) — Find a business by hunting commonalities the way a hit song reuses chord progressions. Signals: (1) **user reviews** — app reviews, Glassdoor reviews, subreddits ("management's too old-school" = an opening); (2) **databases** — Crunchbase, CB Insights, the Inc. 5000; (3) **direct traffic on SimilarWeb** (people typing the URL = real demand); plus untapped customer demand, transplantable business models, and crushing-but-unfocused incumbents. Use to source and pressure-test ideas.
- **Sell-then-build / prepay validation** (Sam Parr; Peep Laja) — Launch the landing page and sell *before* the product exists; build it as you go. Sam: ~$250K in 20 days, then built the class. Peep: crowdfunded ADEFT into his *own email list* to gauge demand and create "cheerleaders." Use to validate willingness-to-pay with real money.
- **Community = "part party, part cult"** (Sam Parr, on Trends) — A great paid community needs: an **us-vs-the-world** mentality (a cult), a **clear, strict-but-fair leader**, and **FOMO** (publicly calling out fascinating members). Use when building a subscription/community product. How: enforce rules ("be respectful," "don't overly self-promote"), kick rule-breakers, refund instantly.
- **Conference vs Trade Show** (Sam Parr) — A conference can make ~$300K/yr but won't scale; the real money is a **trade show = a marketplace of buyers + sellers**, where the talks merely attract the right people (like music at a concert attracts the right dates). Use when monetizing an event audience.
- **Copytesting / Audience Research** (Peep Laja) — Not usability testing — it's "how your messaging *lands* on the very people you're trying to sell to." Use *before* shipping a sales page. How: show the page to your target segment, ask which arguments they care about (5-point scale), what doubts remain, why they'd choose you or not.
- **The Caveman Voice (skim test)** (Peep Laja) — Read a page like the Geico caveman: "Boring. Stupid. Don't care." Whatever still stands out is what's working. Use as a fast self-review proxy for how real (skimming) readers process a page.
- **HIPPO — Highest Paid Person's Opinion** (Peep Laja) — When opinion overrides data because of rank, not evidence. Use as a label for the bias; but mature practice is **data + gut synthesis**, not pure data — an experienced gut filters noisy qualitative feedback.
- **Sea of Sameness + the Content Moat** (Peep Laja) — Most content drowns because it copies what keyword tools say to write ("how to write a good headline" — written to death, can't outrank incumbents). Use when planning content. How: innovate on *format* (e.g. growth.design's 40-hour interactive teardowns) so the difficulty itself becomes your moat and earns the backlinks — *then* you can rank for the obvious terms.

## Key Concepts
- **Owned vs rented reach** — Email/your list is owned and stable; social reach decays as platforms monetize and demand ads.
- **Platform monetization cycle** — New platforms boost whatever they need (FB photos, then groups, then Reels) until everyone does it, then they push the next thing; ride the boosted format early, never depend on it.
- **The playground brand** — A personal brand under your own name (OKDork, NevBlog) is a no-downside lab to test ideas; winners get copied into the company brand. Lowers CPA (people connect with a person, not an entity).
- **Social media is done through people, not logos** — Brand handles = press releases; *personalities* (Noah > AppSumo channel, Tim Soulo > Ahrefs) carry 10× the following and lift the brand.
- **Cold email as a career engine** (Sam) — Almost every investor, friend, and conference guest came from cold email; replying to good marketing emails ("why did you make this button red?") opens doors because almost no one does it.
- **The fib that becomes true** (Sam) — Telling cold prospects "all these other people already accepted" works because most then *do* accept, so it isn't a lie.
- **B2B is still human-to-human** (Peep) — "B2B Neville still eats ice cream"; buzzwords ("where magic happens") = zero clarity = the #1 sales-page failure.
- **Content has to earn its pixels** (Peep) — Readers skim; every argument is rated for whether they care; the rest gets glazed over.
- **Top-of-funnel ≠ buyers** (Peep) — Beginner "how-to" content draws traffic but not budget-holders; a $220K agency commitment never comes from "how to write a headline." Advanced content has less competition and reaches decision-makers.
- **Name that means nothing = best brand name** (Peep) — amazon.com, CXL: a literal name boxes you in when you expand beyond the niche.

## Mental Models
- **Think of email as a ship you own and social as a rented storefront** — build the asset that doesn't decay and that you can act on whenever you want.
- **Use the Venn-diagram test (Sex Cash) when planning content** — sit in the overlap of audience demand and personal interest; outside it you make 1,000-view videos that bore you or you, viral videos that don't sell.
- **Read your own copy in the caveman voice** when self-reviewing — assume the reader is bored and skimming; only what survives that filter is doing work.
- **Treat building like writing a hit song** (Sam) — proven structure + reused progressions + your own style; steal the riff (model/format), don't reinvent the genre.
- **Build the audience first, then find what to sell** (Peep) — his prior startup failed for lack of audience; CXL started as just a blog, and the business came from the audience.

## Anti-patterns
- **Building your business on rented land** — relying on FB/IG/Twitter reach you don't control; one algorithm change or ban wipes it out.
- **Naming your channel after the company or the niche when you might expand** — ConversionXL got "painted into the corner" by the word *conversion*; pick your own name or a meaningless brandable one.
- **Chasing analytics into irrelevance** — optimizing purely for views until the channel attracts an audience that never buys (Beardbrand's "barbershop videos").
- **Entering a medium only for the money** — most who started blogging/YouTubing for cash quit; the ones who'd do it free lasted.
- **Hiring writers off polished long-form samples alone** — a boring expert beats a fluent fluff-writer; check whether they're an interesting person who can tell a story.
- **Buzzword B2B copy** — "when sales and marketing meet, magic happens" reads clearly only to the person who wrote it; everyone else feels nothing.
- **Writing into the Sea of Sameness** — chasing high-volume keywords already owned by incumbents; you'll never outrank them and "nobody gives a shit."
- **Optimizing every part of your life / over-perfecting design** — going 80%→90% on design costs as much as 0%→80% for little ROI (Amazon "has no design"); and removing all friction from life makes you less happy and less human.
- **Mistaking a conference for the prize** — a conference caps out; the marketplace (trade show) is where the money is.

## Key Takeaways
1. **Own your audience.** Prioritize email (controllable, scalable, stable) and one discovery channel (YouTube); ride boosted social formats early but never depend on them.
2. **Find what you'd do for free, then monetize it** — and expect the 10-Year Rule; the durable creators were intrinsically motivated.
3. **Build the audience before the product**; validate by selling first (prepay/landing page) and by reading signals (reviews, Glassdoor, CB Insights, direct traffic on SimilarWeb).
4. **Cold email relentlessly** — to win guests, investors, mentors, and jobs (the fake-Hustle-email application that beat Vox/Vice editors). Replying to great copy you receive is a free door-opener.
5. **Run content through FFR and the Sex-Cash Venn** — proven topic, then Fast/Fun/Repeatable, sitting in the audience-meets-you overlap.
6. **Test messaging on real targets** (Copytesting/audience research) and self-review with the caveman skim test; cut buzzwords — B2B is human-to-human, and every line must earn its pixels.
7. **Personal brands beat logos**; build a playground under your own name and let employees/founders carry the social presence.
