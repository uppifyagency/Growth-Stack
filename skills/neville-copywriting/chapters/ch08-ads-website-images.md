# Chapter 8: Ads, Website Tuneups & Images

## Core Idea
On every paid ad, homepage, and sales page the only job is to move a *qualified* visitor one step closer to one action — so match the message to the searcher's intent, give the page a single clear goal, and remove everything (length, options, white space, clever copy) that creates confusion. Visual mockups (PNG, transparent, mobile-first) carry copy that words alone can't.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 3 Google AdWords Rules** — for text-only search ads (~270 chars, usually no image). (1) **Know the intent** — read the competitor ads ranking for your keyword; whatever they all promise (e.g. "dismissal," "30 years experience") *is* the buyer's priority. Combine every winning angle into one **"super ad."** (2) **Keep it to the point, don't be clever** — the searcher already typed an exact buying keyword; you only need to move them Google→site, not impress them. (3) **Keep it for buyers only** — every click costs money, so word the ad to repel tire-kickers and attract qualified buyers (price, "enroll today," "same-day," service area). Use when writing/auditing any search ad.
- **The Bleary-Eyes Read (5-second test)** — read a homepage "with bleary eyes, like I don't care," because that's how real visitors read. If you can't tell *what this is and what to do* in seconds, the page fails. Use as the first pass on any homepage review.
- **The "One-Two-Three" How-It-Works Block** — any subscription/box/service page must show the whole business in 3 dead-simple steps with a clear image ("choose your meat & eggs → it's delivered → send the box back"). Literally number it. Use whenever the offer is even slightly confusing.
- **The Deep-Blue-of-Death Heat Map** (Crazy Egg) — a scroll/heat map where the page turns deep blue halfway down means readers quit there; a page that stays warm to the bottom is reading well. Use heat maps to *diagnose where attention dies*, then fix that section — don't change a page that's already reading.
- **Make the CTA the Only Thing (landing) vs. Breadth (homepage)** — on a single-goal landing page, "get rid of everything and just say download your comic"; put the one CTA at top and bottom and call it a day. A homepage may show more breadth, but a confused brain ("I'm seeing this, I'm seeing pictures…") converts worse. Decide page type *first*.

## Key Concepts
- **Ad scent** — the ad's promise/headline must match the page it lands on; a mismatch tanks conversion even when click-through is great (high CTR + low orders = broken scent or weak product headline).
- **Super ad** — one ad that fuses every priority the top competitors advertise into a single message.
- **Buyer-only targeting** — qualify in the ad copy so paid clicks come from intent-to-purchase, not browsers.
- **Build the list before showing products** — even an e-commerce/art store should capture email up front; an audience lets you re-sell every new piece to past buyers.
- **PNG with transparency** — `.png` removes the white box around a graphic so it sits on any background; survives blog/platform migrations and color changes. JPEG only when the image is a fully-colored square (saves space).
- **Watermark** — brand graphics so content-scrapers can't pass your images off as theirs.
- **Mobile-first mockup** — vertical images beat horizontal for product diagrams; if it's "hard to read on mobile," remake it vertical (also lets you reuse it on Instagram).
- **Spec/diagram mockup** — an annotated product image (arrows to specs) conveys more than paragraphs and works as ad, page, and social asset at once.
- **Length is earned, not copied** — long-form is fine *only* if every section does a job; most long pages are long because the author imitated another long page.

## Mental Models
- **Think of a paid click as cash leaving your wallet** — so design the ad to filter out non-buyers before they cost you.
- **Use the bleary-eyes read when reviewing your own site** — your brain stops seeing the familiar ("if you see a billboard for 500 days you don't notice it"), so judge it as a cold, careless stranger would.
- **Read the competition to find the buyer's priorities** — the angles everyone is already paying to advertise reveal what the market values; copy the *priority*, not the wording.
- **Think "shut up and take my money"** — if the visitor already wants a cheap product (the ad sold them), don't make them read a long page; surface the Add-to-Cart at the top and let them buy.

## Anti-patterns
- Being clever/creative in a search ad — wastes the limited chars; the searcher only needs the path from Google to your site.
- Burying the CTA / add-to-cart at the bottom, or stacking multiple competing CTAs on a single-goal landing page — splits intent and kills conversion.
- Massive white space and sparse bullets that "convey very little" and look "janky" — density and clear structure read as credible; emptiness reads as slapped-together.
- Copying another page's length or layout without its logic — "that's what most people do"; you inherit bloat with no flow.
- Using JPEGs for overlay graphics — the white background box appears the moment you change the site's background; build for future migrations with transparent PNGs.
- Ignoring mobile — long pages get complex and diagrams get unreadable on phones, where most traffic is.
- Artists/makers selling one-off pieces with no email list — every new release reaches no one.

## Key Takeaways
1. Match ad copy to search intent, fuse competitors' winning angles into a super ad, and qualify for buyers — every click is paid.
2. Give every page ONE goal; on a landing page strip everything but the single CTA (top + bottom).
3. Run the bleary-eyes 5-second test; if "what is this / what do I do" isn't instantly clear, fix that before anything else.
4. Explain any subscription/service business as a literal 1-2-3 how-it-works block with an image.
5. Diagnose with heat maps — find the "deep blue of death," fix where attention dies, and leave well-reading pages alone.
6. High CTR + low orders = broken ad scent, weak page headline, or a buried buy button — fix the page, not the ad spend.
7. Build images as transparent, watermarked, mobile-first PNGs; a single annotated spec mockup can carry an ad, a page, and a social post.

## Connects To
- **Headlines & the slippery slide / readability** — ad scent and the bleary-eyes read extend the headline-first, easy-to-read principles.
- **Sales pages & long-form copy** — "length is earned" and the single-CTA rule build on sales-page structure.
- **AIDA / the 4 U's** — search-ad intent and "buyers only" map to Attention + Desire qualified to the right reader.
- **Email & audience building** — the capture-email-before-selling rule ties to list-building and repeat-buyer monetization.
- **Formatting & visuals** — PNG/watermark/mockup craft supports the "make it skimmable and visual" copy principles.
