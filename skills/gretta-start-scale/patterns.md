# Patterns & Playbooks — Start & Scale 3.0

## P.S.S.P. Ideation
**When to use**: turning a vague problem into a concrete, sellable product.
**How**: list 5 broad problems you've experienced → for each, write **P**roblem, a **S**tat that makes it real, the **S**olution (often the polar opposite), and a **P**roduct. Then screen each on market/demand/shippability and cut to 3.
**Trade-offs**: stats must be credible; not every product is e-commerce-viable (shipping is the gate).

## Differentiate by Changing One Dimension
**When**: you have a known product and need a UVP without reinventing the wheel.
**How**: change one of **Design** (personalisation > customisation, sustainable materials), **Function** (solve a pain point from 2–3★ reviews; product hybridization), **Price** (accessible DTC vs luxury), or **Time** (limited edition / drops → scarcity).
**Trade-offs**: customisation is tech-heavy; faked scarcity decays; keep hybrids relevant.

## Build the Market Before the Product (Market-Product Fit)
**When**: pre-launch, to avoid "launching to crickets."
**How**: grow an engaged audience around a niche/hashtag → study who engages → build personas → reverse-engineer a product they'll love → convert followers → email waitlist → customers.
**Trade-offs**: audiences take time/energy; start as early as possible.

## The 3 C's Content Engine
**When**: growing any social channel.
**How**: post **Content** that adds value / builds trust / inspires action (relevant CTAs); **Collaborate** (shout-out-for-shout-out, paid niche-account shout-outs, influencer giveaways); post with **Consistency** (daily). Pick 3–5 **content territories**; find **Top Performing Content** and weight toward it; mix **BGC/UGC/IGC**.
**Trade-offs**: don't push constant sales content (resentment/unfollows); curate legally with credit.

## The Content Funnel
**When**: visitors aren't converting ("200 visits, 0 sales").
**How**: map content to **Awareness** (problem/brand: posts, blogs, giveaways, ebooks) → **Evaluation** (social proof + product content: reviews, demos, FAQs) → **Purchase** (psychology + an offer).
**Trade-offs**: each stage needs the right content; skipping evaluation loses warm leads.

## Tag-to-Win Giveaway
**When**: fastest pre-launch social growth.
**How**: ask followers to follow you + tag a friend per comment; give away **two** of a single attainable product (candid photo); time-limit it; co-host with a higher-reach niche account/influencer/non-competing brand; run a second-chance draw + consolation discount.
**Trade-offs**: don't overuse (devalues brand); easy entry beats "tag 5 friends."

## Marketing Psychology Triggers
**When**: getting people to act (signup, share, buy).
**How**: **Reciprocity** (give free content/gift first — incentivize signups), **Social Proof** (popularity, public praise, expert endorsements, proof of demand), **Scarcity** (limited time/stock; show waitlist vs units).
**Trade-offs**: never fake scarcity — the brain stops responding without a real reward.

## Post-Purchase Micro-Moments
**When**: turning buyers into repeat customers/advocates.
**How**: incentivize sharing at 4 moments — right after purchase (CheckoutShare), unboxing (great packaging + insert/email CTA), product in action (branded hashtag), transformation/results (before/afters, video testimonials).
**Trade-offs**: results content suits health/beauty most; always get consent to reshare.

## Waitlist Landing Page (7 components)
**When**: pre-launch demand capture and validation.
**How**: headline → subhead → hero image (prototype/render in use) → benefits copy (from brand pyramid) → social proof → clickable signup CTA → scarcity. Build with Lander/Unbounce; aim ≥100 signups; data to a Google Sheet.
**Trade-offs**: clarity over cleverness — actually ask for the signup.

## Influencer Campaign (goal-first)
**When**: scaling reach/sales/content via creators.
**How**: pick a goal (4 C's) → select on the 3 R's + audience analysis (audience ≠ influencer) → mix macro/micro by goal → find via Suggestions-for-you, clusters, customer-follows, platform search → personalized outreach (give a why, transparent comp, creative freedom) → negotiate with a brief → leverage assets (repost, paid ads, retargeting, re-collaborate) → measure ROI against the goal.
**Trade-offs**: chasing follower count over fit is the #1 mistake; results compound over months — don't quit after 10 sends.

## Source via Alibaba (Start Local, Scale Global)
**When**: scaling manufacturing after validating a prototype.
**How**: prototype locally first; on Alibaba search **Manufacturers** (not Products); shortlist 5 on Verified + Trade Assurance + ISO/BSCI + 5yrs/revenue + relevance + transparency; open with a **leverage-building message** (cite their specifics, long-term partnership, factory visit) + a one-page spec sheet for apples-to-apples quotes.
**Trade-offs**: never order off-platform without Trade Assurance; you pay for samples/molds.

## Protect Margin (it's your mistakes margin)
**When**: pricing and when a rival undercuts you.
**How**: keep gross margin 30–60% at ≥2.5× cost; if undercut, (1) cut production costs (don't cheap out on materials), (2) bundle low- with high-margin items, (3) raise the price (lower later if needed).
**Trade-offs**: discounting from launch trains customers to expect low prices.

## Launch in 3 Phases
**When**: bringing a product to market.
**How**: **Plan** (Monday date, pre-mortem, "why buy now" offer, marketing) → **Pre-launch** (~8 weeks hype + waitlist; email weekly then 2–3× final week) → **Launch** (test everything, email + post with one CTA, keep momentum + closing-soon scarcity). Then **reiterate** from real customer feedback (surveys).
**Trade-offs**: never promise a date you can't hit; "just launch — no launch is perfect."

## Scale People & Process (SOPs + Scorecard)
**When**: the founder becomes the bottleneck.
**How**: write **SOPs** for recurring tasks; use the **Entrepreneurship Scorecard** ($1,000/$100/$5 per hour) to delegate down by task value; founder runs support until ~6 figures, then "hire when it hurts."
**Trade-offs**: outsourcing support too early loses the product-market intersection.
