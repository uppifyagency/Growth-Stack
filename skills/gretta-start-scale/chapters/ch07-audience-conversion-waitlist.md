# Chapter 7: Building an Audience II — Conversion & Waitlist

## Core Idea
Turn the audience you built into subscribers, customers, and advocates. The post-purchase stage is the most-forgotten and one of the most valuable (retention beats acquisition); giveaways are still the fastest way to grow social; marketing psychology gets people over the line; and the email **waitlist** is the single most powerful pre-launch asset — "you basically cannot launch without a waitlist."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Post-Purchase Stage (Loyalty + Advocacy)** — the relationship doesn't end at purchase. Goal: drive **repeat purchases, reviews, and referrals**. It's far cheaper to retain than acquire, which raises Customer Lifetime Value (SkinnyMe: ~30–40% repurchase rate; AOV ~$55 → CLV ~$70+), letting you outspend rivals on acquisition.
- **Customer Experience Micro-Moments** (4) — little moments to celebrate and incentivize sharing, building virality into an e-commerce brand: (1) **Right after purchase** (dopamine/instant gratification; share "look what I just bought" to fight buyer's remorse — Shopify app *CheckoutShare*; "share in front of the sold sign"), (2) **Product arrival & unboxing** (the unboxing phenomenon — Trunk Club's trunk box, Purple's 15-sec unrolling at 72M views; incentivize via insert cards/packaging/email), (3) **Product in action** (Frank Body's #thefrankeffect ~50K posts; UberEats-on-bag CTA; AliaSkin #aliaskinbabe monthly winner), (4) **Transformation & results** (before/afters — @skinnymetea_results; the most powerful UGC, plus video testimonials).
- **Marketing Psychology** (from Cialdini's *Influence* — 7 principles: reciprocity, commitment/consistency, liking, authority, social proof, scarcity, unity). Gretta's three most powerful:
  - **Reciprocity** — give first to get. Incentivize signups with free content (SkinnyMe's "14-day free booty challenge" → 8,000 email signups in a day → automated email series; app *Just Do Know*) or a **free gift with purchase** (app *Ultimate Special Offers* — gift shown at $0; lifted store conversion 2%+).
  - **Social Proof** — fastest way to scale trust (Nielsen: 83% trust friend/family recs vs ~30% for branded content). Four categories (Shopify): **popularity** (likes/views/subscribers), **public praise** (reviews/ratings/UGC — apps Yotpo/Loox), **expert endorsements** (dermatologist/doctor; influencers; PR), **proof of demand** (units sold, recent purchases, live viewers, waitlist size — e.g. "11M cups of tea sold").
  - **Scarcity** (FOMO) — limited time or stock ("only 10 left," flash sale countdown, limited edition). The Fifth's whole model was scarcity (sell on the 5th for 5 days → exclusive by time, not price). **Don't fake scarcity** — the brain stops reacting if the stimulus has no reward. Tactic: show waitlist count vs units in stock (Taylor: 16,000 waitlist / 1,000 units, fully transparent → hype).
- **The Waitlist Landing Page — 7 components**: (1) **Headline** (grab attention, build FOMO/anticipation), (2) **Subhead** (one-liner: what it is + who it's for), (3) **Hero image** (prototype/3D render, show context of use), (4) **clear benefits-driven copy** (from the brand pyramid's rational benefits), (5) **social proof** (even a pre-launch friend/influencer testimonial), (6) **clickable CTA** wired to waitlist signup, (7) **scarcity** (countdown timer / "coming soon, be the first"). Build with Lander/Unbounce/Leadpages/Launchrock; data can flow to a Google Sheet (no CRM needed yet).

## Key Concepts
- **Email beats social for conversion** — OptinMonster: ~6.05% email conversion vs ~1.9% social (>3×); ~$44 returned per $1 (4,400% ROI). Funnel social followers → landing page → email list → customers.
- **Giveaways = fastest social growth** — they stack reciprocity (giving a prize), social proof (giveaways out-engage everything), and scarcity (time-limited). Best leverage: co-host with a higher-reach niche/vertical account, influencer, or non-competing brand (loop giveaways).
- **The Tag-to-Win Giveaway** — ask followers to follow you + tag a friend per comment. The Fifth's caption grew Instagram +20,000 followers in 24h (80,000+ comments; most-engaged brand globally that day) giving away just *two* watches. Rules that make/break it: strong clear CTA; make entry dead-easy (tag *one* friend, more in extra comments — beats "tag 5"); attainable prize (a watch, not a car; candid un-photoshopped shot); **give away two** so tagging a friend is relevant; time-limit it; boost with paid shout-outs/influencers; run a **second-chance draw** + a consolation discount ("everyone's a winner, 15% off"). (Bambi Boutique: 0→150K followers in a week via 3 macro-influencer giveaways.)
- **Goal: ≥100 waitlist subscribers before launch** (The Fifth had 8,000 → sold 1,000+ watches / $100K day one). A waitlist is also a cheap **demand test** — many landing pages get built for products never made; crickets = move on having spent little.

## Mental Models
- **"You can take the person out of the Stone Age, but not the Stone Age out of the person"** — humans are hardwired; psychological triggers reliably move behavior.
- **"Sell the sizzle, not the sausage"** — sell benefits, not features; The Fifth's *model* was the UVP.
- **Retention > acquisition** — repeat buyers raise CLV and let you outspend competitors.
- **Clarity over cleverness** — on a landing page, actually ask for the action you want.

## Anti-patterns
- Forgetting the post-purchase stage (lost revenue, exposure, and repeat-buyer rate).
- Faking scarcity repeatedly until customers stop responding.
- Giveaways with hard entry, unattainable prizes, or only one prize (no reason to tag a friend).
- Launching with no waitlist ("launching to crickets").
- A clever landing page that never clearly asks for the signup.

## Key Takeaways
1. Work the post-purchase micro-moments to drive repeat purchases, reviews, referrals.
2. Use reciprocity, social proof, and scarcity deliberately — and never fake scarcity.
3. Grow fast with tag-to-win giveaways co-hosted for reach; give away two; second-chance draw.
4. Build a 7-component waitlist landing page; aim for ≥100 subscribers before launch.
