# Chapter 4: Developing & Planning Your Product

## Core Idea
Turn a validated idea into a physical, shippable product the cheap, low-risk way. The governing motto: **Start Local, Scale Global** — prototype and iterate with a hands-on local manufacturer, then move to a global manufacturer (Alibaba) only after you've validated demand and proven a prototype. Get a designer to make a render/prototype, treat packaging as a brand touchpoint, and craft a leverage-building first message to suppliers.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Start Local, Scale Global** — local manufacturers let you be involved, innovate, engineer from scratch, avoid high MOQs and cultural/communication barriers, and inspect in person. Global manufacturers (esp. China) are cheaper per unit and great at *reverse-engineering an existing product* but bad at building from scratch and demand high minimums. Sequence: prototype + first run locally → scale globally once selling. Caveat: "Made in [country]" can itself be a UVP (SkinnyMe stays made in Australia) — don't always go global. Keep it a **one-stop shop**; never source 20 parts from 20 suppliers.
- **Render vs Prototype** — a **3D render** is a digital visual (designer-made; ~$250–$500 on Upwork for an industrial/product designer); a **prototype** is a physical early model. Build a prototype if you can; otherwise a render is the second-best thing to send manufacturers for samples. Renders double as marketing assets (Facebook ads, web — Recess, Kin Euphorics use them everywhere).
- **Briefing a Designer — 3 keys**: (1) **Product features** (non-negotiables), (2) **Materials**, (3) the **Start & Scale product framework** — lightweight, high perceived value, easy to ship (always a balancing act: heavier can feel more premium but costs more to ship). Always get the **original working files** (you own the assets).
- **Alibaba Supplier Shortlist — 6 criteria** (build a list of 5): (1) **Verified**, (2) **Trade Assurance** (refund if you don't get what you ordered), (3) **Management certifications** — ISO (quality) + BSCI (social compliance), plus product-specific certs you Google, (4) **Years operating + annual revenue** (>5 years; large revenue = economies of scale passed to you), (5) **Relevance** (already makes products in your space), (6) **Transparency** (proud factory photos, lists equipment). **Always search "Manufacturers," not "Products."**
- **Leverage-Building First Message** (guest: Kian Golzari) — don't open with "best price? MOQ?" Instead: introduce yourself and your selling avenue (influencers/KOLs, passion, years of use even if no sales yet); cite *specific* facts from their page (years established, worker count, % exports to your market, trade shows attended); ask about quality-control/machinery to signal standards; state you want a **long-term partnership** and intend to **visit the factory** (makes them give honest info and treat you as serious); attach a **one-page specification sheet** so every quote compares "apples to apples."

## Key Concepts
- **Packaging = first physical brand touchpoint** and a shareable unboxing moment. Five design considerations: (1) unboxing experience, (2) shipping cost (SkinnyMe shipped tea in a ~$1.20 envelope → scaled fast), (3) product shape (not everything needs a box), (4) perceived value (The Fifth needed a premium experience), (5) functionality/fragility (Dropbottle = padding for glass).
- **Packaging sourcing rule of thumb**: 100–300 units → use a packaging company (Lumi, Ink); 500+ units → go direct to a manufacturer (Alibaba). Often the same designer who did your logo/product does packaging; the product manufacturer can package for you. Inspect packaging like product (typos, spec misses); order bulk but not huge batches until confident.
- **Breaking the mould costs money** — custom molds/samples are paid; The Fifth paid ~$2,000–$3,000 per new watch-face mold. You always pay for samples.
- **Beginner-questions trick** — keep a list of suppliers you *don't* want to work with and ask them your novice questions, so you approach your real targets looking professional.
- **Shipping & tracking** — ship in-house first (3PLs need ~300–500 units/mo in US/CA/UK/AU, ~100/mo in Asia). Use the major local post (USPS, AusPost); check it integrates with your platform (Shopify). Ship daily (know the cutoff) — faster shipping lifts conversion; slow delivery promises kill sales. Print labels (DyMo LabelWriter 4XL), flat-pack, buy materials in bulk (eBay). Offer **free standard shipping** (baked into price), customer pays for express, or free over a $ threshold.
- **AI tools** (change constantly): Midjourney (visual ideation for designers — public, so don't use for secret IP), Toggle3D (DIY 3D models), Brandwatch/Reviews (competitor packaging sentiment), Packwire/Canva+ChatGPT (packaging prototypes), Remove.bg + Booth.ai (virtual product photoshoots).

## Mental Models
- **Manufacturers think in customer lifetime value, not order value** — signaling long-term partnership + a factory visit unlocks better pricing even below MOQ.
- **Compare apples to apples** — a spec sheet is the only way to make multi-supplier quotes comparable.
- **The proof is in delivery** — don't commit until samples, responsiveness, and communication prove quality (the 10-5-3-1 funnel: many → 5 shortlisted → 3 sampled → 1 chosen; judge on sample quality, sample price, then communication).

## Anti-patterns
- Ordering from a global manufacturer before validating demand and proving a prototype (Gretta lost $1M on a single off-platform tea order with no Trade Assurance).
- Searching Alibaba for "Products" instead of "Manufacturers" (different algorithm, worse results).
- Opening a supplier message with price/MOQ demands — marks you as not serious.
- Treating a 3D render as a finished, send-to-factory design (it's the start of ideation).
- Going cheapest on packaging — gives a cheap first impression that damages the brand.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start Local, Scale Global: prototype locally, scale to Alibaba only post-validation.
2. Get a render/prototype from an industrial designer (~$250–$500); own the working files.
3. Shortlist 5 Alibaba suppliers on the 6 criteria; open with leverage + a spec sheet.
4. Design packaging as a brand touchpoint; ship daily and aim for free standard shipping.
