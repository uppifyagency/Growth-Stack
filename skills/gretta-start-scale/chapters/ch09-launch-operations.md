# Chapter 9: Launch & Operations

## Core Idea
"You only get one chance to launch." Run the launch as three phases (plan → pre-launch → launch), answer the customer's one question ("why buy this *now*?") with scarcity/newness, then operate the business: free shipping and buy-now-pay-later to lift conversion, tight stock management, founder-led customer support, and a never-ending reiterate/optimize loop driven by real customer feedback. The cardinal rule: **just launch — no launch is perfect; get the product out and start getting feedback.**

## Frameworks Introduced
- **3-Phase Product Launch**: (1) **Plan** — lock a launch **date** (start on a **Monday** — highest traffic & email opens), assign dates to everything, run a **pre-mortem** (brainstorm what could go wrong at each step and fix it proactively), decide the launch **offer** answering "why buy now?", and plan marketing (influencers, waitlist emails, social, paid). (2) **Pre-launch** (~2 weeks to a couple months; ideally ~8 weeks) — build hype, get people on the waitlist, line up influencer sneak-peeks/coming-soon posts, ensure stock + store are ready; **email the waitlist weekly from ~2–3 weeks out, then 2–3× in the final week**. (3) **Launch** — test everything (dummy purchases, order button, receipt, pop-ups, multiple browsers + mobile), email the waitlist + post on social at the exact time with **one clear CTA to shop now**, keep customer support open, maintain posting, and keep momentum with closing-soon scarcity for the first several days.
- **Launch offer angles** — **newness** ("be the first" — Gretta's recommended default; don't discount from the start as it devalues), or a **launch-only discount / BOGO / early-bird for the first X buyers**, or **scarcity** (numbered limited editions, limited stock).
- **Free Shipping (conversion lever)** — bake shipping into the product price and advertise "free shipping." Split tests consistently show **a higher price with free shipping beats a lower price + paid shipping** ($30 free-ship > $25 + $5). Standard shipping has no perceived value; only tracked/express shipping does. Offer: free domestic standard, a flat international fee, tracked express options. Faster delivery (1–5 days vs 10–20) drastically lifts conversion.
- **Buy-Now-Pay-Later + PayPal** — Afterpay/Zip (Australia/NZ; find a regional equivalent elsewhere) raise conversion via interest-free installments — use **only one** (the other penalizes you). Always offer **PayPal** alongside credit card (big conversion lift).
- **Stock Management** — know three numbers always: **stock on hand, average sales (daily/weekly/monthly), and lead time** (often 1–3 months). Use Shopify apps/forecasts + reorder triggers; do a stock take **weekly (Mondays)** early on, monthly later. Too much stock = expensive to hold; too little = lost sales + bad experience (SkinnyMe's #1 early problem). Manage from home/garage before scaling to 3PL.
- **Customer Support ("Customer Success")** — the founder should do it personally for the **first few months/year**: it's the direct intersection of product and market, sets the brand tone of voice, builds your FAQ list, and surfaces sales questions (answering them = more sales). Set up `support@yourcompany.com`, an auto-responder with a 24–48h expectation, never take feedback personally, copy-paste/keyboard-shortcut common answers, build an FAQ page. Scale to a help desk (Zendesk/HelpScout/Intercom) at ~$10K/mo; **hire when it hurts** — outsource or a vetted VA (test-project first) only at ~6 figures annual revenue. (Gretta did support 6 hrs/day for SkinnyMe's first 6 months, scaling 0→$600K/mo.)
- **Reiterate & Optimize** — never stop improving; but **don't iterate until you have real customer feedback** (focus on their pain points, not what you *think* they want). Track common complaints/returns/issues in a running spreadsheet; actively seek feedback via **satisfaction surveys** (Typeform/SurveyMonkey, short, multiple-choice, incentivized, sent within ~a month, automated) — ask NPS ("1–10, would you refer?") and "one thing you'd change?". Use feedback to fix the product (Dropbottle v1→v2: removed double-wall glass that broke, raised the fruit infuser for better photos, thickened outer glass) or spin out a variation (Frank Body's glitter scrub riding the Coachella trend).

## Key Concepts
- **Gretta's E-Commerce Stack** — deliberately kept as a maintained, evergreen list in the lesson notes (not named in-course) because tools date fast. Principle: tools are sidekicks that automate, analyze, and predict, but **can't replace the human touch** — over-automate and "everything looks like it was built by a robot." "These tools are only as good as the person using them."
- **AI customer service** — chatbots (Tars/Intercom/Zendesk) as 24/7 agents whose key value is **language/timezone localization**, escalating hard queries to humans; AI virtual assistants give personalized recommendations; AI also surfaces customer-response data to improve marketing/copy/product.

## Mental Models
- **"Just launch — no launch is perfect."** Getting feedback fast beats waiting for perfect.
- **Why buy this now?** — every launch must answer this with newness, an offer, or scarcity.
- **Higher price + free shipping > lower price + paid shipping** — proven in split tests.
- **Hire when it hurts** — bring on help only when you genuinely can't keep up at ~6 figures.
- **Tools are sidekicks** — automation enhances, never replaces, your creativity and connection.

## Anti-patterns
- Promising a launch date you can't hit / launching without enough stock.
- Discounting from day one (trains customers to expect low prices, devalues the brand).
- Charging visible standard shipping (kills conversion vs free shipping baked in).
- Running out of stock from not knowing your numbers/lead times.
- Outsourcing customer support too early — you lose the market intersection and tone of voice.
- Iterating the product on your assumptions instead of real customer feedback.
- Over-relying on automation until the brand feels robotic.

## Key Takeaways
1. Launch in three phases with a Monday date, a pre-mortem, and a "why buy now" offer; then just launch.
2. Bake shipping into price (free shipping) and add BNPL + PayPal to lift conversion.
3. Know stock-on-hand, average sales, and lead time; founder-run support until it hurts.
4. Continuously reiterate from real customer feedback (surveys/complaints), not assumptions.
