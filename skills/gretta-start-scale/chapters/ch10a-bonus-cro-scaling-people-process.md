# Chapter 10a: Bonus — CRO & Scaling People/Process

## Core Idea
The bonus module's "build & operate" half: optimize the store for conversions with an industry-standard landing-page formula, then scale the *people and process* behind the brand so the founder stops being the bottleneck — via SOPs, the Entrepreneurship Scorecard, and the right co-founder/GM decision. Cross-cutting threads: don't reinvent the wheel; turn accidents into UVPs; start scrappy and scale; lean on systems. (The dated *May 2023 Content Challenge* module is filler — skip it.)

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Landing Page Formula (CRO walkthrough)** — the industry-standard home-page order Gretta applies when she audits a store as a first-time user: **lifestyle/hero image + UVP** (subject line tells you instantly what the store is — e.g. "all things rosé") → **featured products** (feature your **top ~3** as a product block that clicks through to the collection — converts better than dumping the whole catalogue) → **Reasons To Believe (RTBs)** as **3 visual icons** (circles/images + title + one line of text — e.g. *quality, curation, convenience*; far better than bulk text) → **social proof / reviews** → **social feed / blog** (blogs work well for effects-based products — e.g. "5 foods that improve your hair"; source ideas on Pinterest). Move long brand-story copy off the home page into an **About tab** — most shoppers are there to shop, not read.
- **Product-page & cart optimization** — use **white/transparent backgrounds** on standalone product shots for a consistent, professional look; collapse long copy into **product info tabs** (a Shopify app, e.g. "Product Info Tabs" — splits the main pitch from ingredients so you don't get text next to white space); add **reviews/social proof** (Loox/Yotpo) and grow review count via post-purchase automated emails (~3-5 days after delivery), review giveaways, and thank-you-card asks; add **upsells/bundles** in the cart to lift AOV (upsell your most popular/most accessible product, or "people who bought this also bought" — but make the bundle discount meaningful: saving $4 on three $20 packs won't motivate; ~$50 might). Add a **shipping-rate calculator** and **multi-currency** switch. Note: **Shopify checkout can't be customized** — just ensure it meets best practice.
- **Pop-ups & FOMO** — fire the email-capture **pop-up fast** (within the ~8-second attention span — shoppers now *expect* a pop-up and the discount); give a real incentive (a discount code, not a vague "exclusive offer"); capture a segmenting field where useful (e.g. gender) for targeted email. Tools: **Justuno** (discount-unlock), **Wheelio** (gamified spin-to-win). Install a **FOMO** "others shopping now" app. Offer a **waitlist/notify-me** pop-up for shoppers not ready to buy.
- **Reduce analysis paralysis** — fewer choices convert better. One product with **selectable colors/variations** beats many near-identical SKUs scrolled down a page; streamlining the decision raises conversions.
- **SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures)** — a **step-by-step list of instructions for executing a routine procedure**. Rule: *if you perform a task more than once, make a SOP.* The biggest game-changer for people/process — it lets you hand work over in a logical, supported way, done the way you want, and delegate **down to cheaper, more junior staff**. Gretta's own example: an **Instagram-management SOP** (goal = a feed optimized for growth/conversions that acts as a landing page for influencer work + ads + organic sales) structured as **one-off items** (feed strategy → optimize bio → story highlights → plan first 9-12 posts via Planoly/Later or a saved IG collection → set up saved collections for curated content) then **ongoing items** (a posting schedule, e.g. once daily alternating 8am/8pm). She keeps a separate **influencer-management SOP** for micro/nano outreach at scale. (Process Street is her tool reference for SOPs.)
- **The Entrepreneurship Scorecard** — bucket tasks by leverage: **$1,000/hr** (evergreen, high-leverage — building SOPs, launching a new channel/market, creating a new product), **$100/hr** (e.g. filming content for that channel), **$5/hr** (answering comments, posting, basic customer service). The founder does the $1,000/hr tasks; mid-level the $100/hr; juniors the $5/hr — matched to role and wage.
- **Co-founder vs GM decision** — solo-found if the brand is your niche/audience and you can cover both marketing and ops; bring in a complementary skill set when yours is one-sided (pure product nerd vs pure marketer vs pure ops/finance person). Framing: *would you rather own 100% of a $5-10M business or 50% of a $100M one?* Over 50% of startups fail on co-founder disputes, so **don't co-found with romantic partners/close friends**; prefer someone you met through work/study who you've worked well with for a good while. Have hard conversations + a **shareholders agreement** up front (lawyers walk worst-case scenarios that *do* happen). Gretta's preferred structure: promote a trusted right-hand to **GM with vesting equity** (e.g. via an **ESOP**; "5% already vested + 5% over 5 years") rather than co-founding cold — gives an employee skin in the game with tax concessions.

## Key Concepts

- **Q&A (operating answers)**: undercut by a rival 80% cheaper? In e-commerce (unlike Amazon's price-sensitivity), a higher price can mean higher *perceived* value — do competitive analysis, communicate value, price accordingly. Selling to US/UK: start with a **multi-currency Shopify store** (currency adapts to location) before separate regional stores; market with local influencers + paid ads. Don't DM influencers who promoted a *direct* competitor within ~60 days (looks inauthentic; define "direct competitor" narrowly). **Pre-orders** validate demand and bring revenue — label "[PRE-ORDER]" loudly and pad the ship timeframe (under-promise, over-deliver). Use an ERP (Odoo) only past ~$1M/yr with many SKUs.
- **Insider advice**: don't reinvent the wheel — **convergence** (combine two ideas) + change one dimension for a fresh UVP. **Film validation reactions** — facial expressions/body language reveal the "wow" (or confusion/disappointment) more than written feedback. **Pinterest is the go-to branding tool** — its rabbit-hole of similar pins is an exponential aid; build mood boards (e.g. her "tennis athleisure" board mixing Slim Aarons photography with modern athletic wear) and share them with designers as a visual brief. Names can change/adapt early — **secure the domain** (a good proxy for trademark availability; a free 30-min trademark-lawyer consult helps) and remember a name owned in another category may be free for yours (the best names — Samsung, Nokia, Honda — mean nothing until branding gives them social meaning). **Packaging is the reveal moment** — unboxing is engineered, not accidental (Apple even tests sticker-peel time); Frank Body's shipping bag ("Help, I'm a coffee scrub trapped in a bag") signals tone of voice instantly. **Brand persona should be a subset of your own tone of voice** early on (Gretta = "a friend" for Skinny Me Tea); stray further only later with agency-level resources.
- **The $1.1M moldy-tea disaster (full version)**: tired of constantly running out of stock and hating forecasting, Gretta ordered a *year's* worth of tea (~$1.1M) to "get ahead." It sat in storage (~$10-20K), got stuck in customs 13 days (~$20-30K), and arrived **literally compost** — moldy, high E. coli, metal bolts/springs. Throwing it out cost ~$15K; she was 3 months behind with a 3-month lead time, back to hand-mixing the MVP. Lesson: **start as small as humanly possible and scale up**; the biggest loss is lost *market opportunity/time*, not the money — act fast and move on rather than litigate for years (think what 3 months of value-add is worth vs the lost million).

## Mental Models
- **Don't reinvent the wheel** — convergence + a single changed dimension.
- **Turn accidents into UVPs** — many central value propositions started as mistakes.
- **Start scrappy, scale up** — hand-mixing in a kitchen → $600K/mo; small early orders cap risk.
- **You can't clone yourself ×12** — be the best *generalist* as founder, not best at everything; people will beat you in their specialty — delegate via SOPs or you can't scale.
- **The market opportunity is the biggest loss** — protect time over recovering sunk money.

## Anti-patterns
- Competing purely on price in e-commerce instead of communicating value.
- Co-founding with a romantic partner/close friend or without a shareholders agreement (Gretta's two costly past examples — one ex-boyfriend sued claiming co-founder status, ~3 years and ~$300K in legal fees; another led her to exit The Fifth).
- The founder doing $5/hr tasks instead of $1,000/hr tasks; refusing to delegate.
- Over-ordering stock to "get ahead" (the $1.1M moldy-tea order); litigating instead of moving on.
- Slow or incentive-free pop-ups; too many product variations (analysis paralysis); long brand-story copy on the home page.

## Key Takeaways
1. Optimize stores with the landing-page formula (lifestyle+UVP → top-3 featured → RTB icons → social proof → feed), product info tabs, fast incentivized pop-ups, FOMO, and meaningful upsells.
2. If you do a task more than once, write a SOP — it's how you delegate down and stop being the bottleneck.
3. Allocate effort with the Entrepreneurship Scorecard ($1,000 / $100 / $5 per hour); match tasks to role and wage.
4. Prefer a vesting-equity GM over a cold co-founder; never co-found with partners/friends, and never without a shareholders agreement.
