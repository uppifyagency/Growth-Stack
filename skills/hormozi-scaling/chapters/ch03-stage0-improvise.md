# Chapter 3: Stage 0 — Improvise (headcount 0, role: Researcher)

## Core Idea
You have nothing and know nothing, so your job is to **research** by giving something away for free and learning from real feedback. The objective here is to **learn more than to earn** — most people fail by trying to sell before they have expertise or proof.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Give something free (product)**: pick a mechanism — software, media, service (knowledge- or time-based), or physical stuff. Structure the free thing to (a) **reveal a problem** (e.g. an assessment), (b) be a **free trial** (taste the solution), or (c) be the **free first step of a multi-step process**.
  - B2C rule of thumb: do **things everyone hates doing** (clean, cook, laundry, lawn care). B2B: do **things businesses aren't as good at as you** (act as a fractional expert / done-for-you across a function).
- **The Core Four (marketing)**: the only 4 ways to make people aware of your stuff — **warm outreach, cold outreach, post free content, run paid ads**. Start with **warm outreach** (cheapest, fastest): email, call, text, DM everyone who already knows you. "100 warm leads can jumpstart a business; 100 cold leads can't."
- **The 4 R's (customer service objective)**: get customers to **Review, Refer, Retain, Resell** (ascend). Service free customers as if they're paying — they *are* paying with time, feedback, and proof.
- **Recruit yourself**: build **work capacity**. "You can beat 99% of people by preparing the night before, showing up early, leaving when the job's done right, remembering names, following up, improving one thing at a time." "Get good enough at almost anything with 20 hours of focused effort — most people spend a decade delaying the first hour."

## Key Concepts
- **Learn > earn** (early objective); throw the kitchen sink at customers to see what they value.
- **Hidden costs** (inverse of the Value Equation): the non-monetary reasons people don't act — effort, sacrifice, risk, delay. Remove what sucks and you can charge almost anything.
- **Entity separation (HR)**: form an **LLC/S-corp** to wall off personal assets (LegalZoom / attorney, ~a few hundred $).
- **Finance**: open a **business bank account**, put money in, **write off startup expenses** (phone, computer, internet, home office) to save on taxes. Avg small business now costs ~$3,500 to start.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fear of judgment**: refusing warm outreach out of embarrassment — the #1 thing that stalls people here. "Be fearful of never having a business instead."
- **Chasing scale too early**: "remove the word *scale* from your mind" — find what works first.
- **Trying to sell before having proof or expertise.**

## Key Takeaways
1. Make one **free** offer; use it to **learn** and to gather **proof**.
2. Run the **Core Four** (start warm) to get people to try it.
3. Service free customers ruthlessly for the **4 R's**.
4. Legally separate yourself (LLC) and open a business bank account.
