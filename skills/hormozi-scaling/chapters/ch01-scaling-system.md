# Chapter 1: The Scaling System (Start Here)

## Core Idea
Two businesses with the same headcount (a $2M restaurant and a $100M software company both at 35 employees) organize almost identically — so the durable, transferable pattern of scaling is **functions by headcount**, not revenue. This training maps that pattern into 10 stages, each with one constraint to break and one bottom-line action to graduate.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 3 Elements of Scaling**: every business is (1) **Functions** — what a business does to make money; (2) **People** — the *who* that does those things; (3) **Operations** — the glue (meetings, communication, training) that connects people to functions. Have all three and you can go from $0→$100M and from no humans → many humans.
  - When to use: as the top-level mental model for what you're actually building.
- **The 8 Functions** (with Hormozi's exact definitions): **Product** (goods, services & results you deliver) · **Marketing** (how you tell people about your stuff) · **Sales** (how you get people to give you money) · **Customer Service** (how you increase the odds customers rate you highly, buy again, refer) · **IT** (how you gather, store, analyze & display information) · **Recruiting** (how you find and hire people) · **HR** (how you decrease the likelihood employees/regulators sue you) · **Finance** (how you report and manage money).
- **The Per-Stage Template**: every stage is described by → **headcount** · **your role** (how you translate priorities into functions) · **leadership structure** (layers of leaders) · **# of companies** at this scale (US Census / BLS) · the **constraint** per function · what's needed to **graduate** · a single **bottom-line objective**.

## Key Concepts
- **Constraint**: the one thing at a level most limiting growth; strategy = pointing resources at it.
- **Graduation action**: the single move that unlocks the next stage. "If you only had one thing to take away, take that."
- **Bottom line**: the stage's headline problem → headline fix.
- **Guidelines, not laws**: if you're ahead on a function but skipped a step, that skipped step becomes a **weak link** later.

## Mental Models
- **Companies die by indigestion, not starvation.** Most fail by doing *too much*, not too little. Focus beats breadth.
- **Strategy is prioritization under scarcity.** You have limited resources and unlimited options; prioritize the resources that get the highest return — i.e. the current constraint.
- **Only study your stage** (± one). Consuming all levels jumbles priorities from different stages and slows you down.

## Anti-patterns
- **Watching/implementing every stage at once**: creates indigestion and premature optimization.
- **Building something you'll have to reverse later** because you skipped the stage before.

## Key Takeaways
1. Scale is organized by **functions × headcount**, not by revenue.
2. Learn the **8 functions** and their one-line definitions cold — the whole roadmap hangs off them.
3. At every level: find **the constraint**, take **the one graduation action**, ignore everything else.
4. Focus is the meta-skill: die by indigestion, not starvation.
