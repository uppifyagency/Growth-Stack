# Chapter 3: Asking Important Questions

## Core Idea
Non-biasing questions can still be trivial. Spend your conversations on the scary, business-destroying questions — and start broad so you don't validate a problem nobody will pay to fix.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Important questions**: ones whose answer could completely change or disprove your business. Find them via thought experiments — "If this company failed, why?" and "What would have to be true for it to be a huge success?"
- **Look before you zoom** — avoid the **premature zoom**: don't drill into one problem until you have a strong signal it's a *must-solve*. Most people have problems they won't pay to fix.
- **Product risk vs. Market risk**:
  - **Product risk**: Can I build it? Can I grow it? Will they keep using it?
  - **Market risk**: Do they want it? Will they pay? Are there enough of them?
- **Prepare your list of 3**: pre-plan the 3 most important things to learn from each type of person.

## Key Concepts
- **Love bad news**: a clear negative ("meh") saves you time and money. "You're searching for the truth, not trying to be right."
- **Gaze upon the elephant**: businesses have multiple failure points; obsessing over the interesting one while ignoring the boring-but-fatal one (e.g. "can the poor school actually pay us?") is how you miss the elephant.
- **"If you could get me more gigs… I'd pay"** = shifting the burden onto your product. That's product risk, not validation (ad networks, affiliates, video games are near-pure product risk).

## Mental Models
- Spend $5k to learn you're on a dead end (awesome) vs. spend all $50k on the first idea and fail (bad). De-risk cheaply.
- "Talk to me about your blog" vs "about *marketing* your blog" — zooming on a non-problem produces legit-sounding but misleading answers.

## Anti-patterns
- **Comforting yourself with trivial questions** instead of the scary ones.
- **Upping your game** to convert a lukewarm signal into a compliment → false positives.
- **Pitching the idea immediately** at the start of a conversation (biases which answers they give).

## Key Takeaways
1. **You should be terrified of at least one of the questions you're asking in every conversation.**
2. **There's more reliable information in a "meh" than a "Wow!" You can't build a business on a lukewarm response.**
3. **Start broad and don't zoom in until you've found a strong signal** — for your whole business and every conversation.
4. **You always need a list of your 3 big questions.** And you're allowed to ask about money — you're a startup, it's okay.
