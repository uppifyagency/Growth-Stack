# Chapter 6: Finding Conversations

## Core Idea
Stop chasing cold leads and start making people come to you. When you do need to ask for a meeting, frame it so they know you respect their time and that they, specifically, can help.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Going to them**: cold calls/emails work but are a grind — "the goal of cold conversations is to stop having them." Seize serendipity, find a good excuse, immerse yourself where they are, use landing pages to collect leads for *conversations* (not metrics).
- **Bringing them to you** (better): organize **meetups** ("HR professionals happy hour" — instant credibility), **teach/speak**, **blog about the industry**, **get clever** (the "knowledge-exchange call" hack).
- **Creating warm intros**: "7 degrees of bacon" (just ask), industry advisors (equity for intros), universities/professors, investors, cashing in favours.
- **Framing the meeting** — **Vision / Framing / Weakness / Pedestal / Ask** (mnemonic: **"Very Few Wizards Properly Ask [for help]"**):
  1. **Vision** — half-sentence on the problem/world you're improving (don't mention your idea).
  2. **Framing** — your stage; if true, that you have nothing to sell.
  3. **Weakness** — the specific gap you need help on (shows you're not a time-waster).
  4. **Pedestal** — show how *they in particular* can help.
  5. **Ask** — ask for help.

## Key Concepts
- **The advisory flip**: go in search of *advisors*, not customers. Removes the needy vibe, gives you a helpful internal narrative, and counterintuitively puts you in control (you're now evaluating *them*).
- **To commute or to call**: prefer in-person early — calls lose body language and the casual power dynamic, and become scripted interviews. Nobody becomes friends (→ warm intros) over the phone.
- **How many meetings**: keep going until you stop hearing new info — often 3–5 with a focused segment. >10 with all-over-the-map results = your segment is too fuzzy.

## Mental Models
- "If it's not a formal meeting, you don't need to make excuses about why you're there. Just have a good conversation."
- People love helping entrepreneurs but hate wasting time — a good frame promises a real difference.
- Grab the reins once the meeting starts (repeat the email framing, then drop into your first good question), or it becomes them drilling you on your idea.

## Anti-patterns
- "Can I interview you?" / "Can I get your opinion on what we're doing?" / "Do you have time for a quick coffee?" — all set the wrong expectations.
- Letting the meeting become a sales meeting by default (you don't know why you're there → they close up on pricing, attention shifts to you).

## Key Takeaways
1. **If it's a topic you both care about, find an excuse to talk about it** — your idea needn't enter the equation.
2. Bringing people to you makes them take you more seriously and want to help.
3. Organizing a meetup is "the most unfair trick I know for rapid customer learning."
4. **Keep having conversations until you stop hearing new stuff.**
