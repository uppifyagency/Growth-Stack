# Chapter 8: Running the Process

## Core Idea
Even perfect conversations fail without process around them. Prep, take good notes, and review with the whole team — so learning spreads instead of bottlenecking in one head. Then get back to building.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Avoid the learning bottleneck**: when one person hoards the learning, they become a de-facto dictator ("the customer said so"). Disseminate learning to the whole founding team promptly and faithfully — notes + exact quotes.
- **Three parts**: **Prepping → Conversing → Reviewing.**
  - **Prep**: know your 3 big questions; the one essential prep question is **"What do we want to learn from these guys?"** Plus the risk-unearthing pair: "If this company failed, why?" and "What would have to be true for it to be a huge success?"
  - **Review**: with the team, go through notes/quotes, update beliefs + the next 3 questions, and debrief the *craft* (which questions worked).
- **Note-taking signal symbols** (capture + sort): :) excited · :( angry · :| embarrassed · ⚡ pain/problem · ⊓ goal/job-to-be-done · ☐ obstacle · ↝ workaround · ^ background/context · ☑ feature request/purchasing criteria · $ money/budget/process · ♀ specific person/company · ☆ follow-up task.

## Key Concepts
- **Who should show up**: anyone making big decisions (incl. tech) goes to *some* meetings. Two people is ideal (one talks, one takes notes and catches mistakes); three can overwhelm. **You can't outsource customer learning** — founders must be in the room.
- **Where to write it down**: notes must be sortable, combinable, permanent, and separate from other noise. Index cards (one quote/learning per card) are the author's favorite — random access + sorting; spreadsheets/Evernote also work.
- **Hack it** (Gordian knot): don't get stuck in process. The personal trainer just *called the police* and booked a trial in 20 minutes while everyone else theorized.

## Mental Models
- Telling the team "what I learned" = telling them "what you'll do" → owning the conversations creates a dictator. Share the raw learning, not just conclusions.
- **This stuff is fast**: prep an hour (not a week); do a week or two of conversations (not months), then give them something to commit to. Keeping it casual lets you keep learning forever without spending real time on it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Symptoms of going through the motions**: you talk more than they do; they compliment you; you don't know the next step; no notes; you didn't review with the team; an unexpected answer didn't change your idea; you weren't scared of any question; you're not sure which big question you're answering.
- Audio recordings/notebooks you never sort or revisit. **Notes are useless if you don't look at them.**

## Key Takeaways
1. **If you don't know what you're trying to learn, you shouldn't bother having the conversation.**
2. Prep with the whole team (business + product), or you'll miss their concerns.
3. **You'll always make mistakes** — review, don't beat yourself up, get better as a team. "It's going to be okay."
4. **Go build your dang company already.**
