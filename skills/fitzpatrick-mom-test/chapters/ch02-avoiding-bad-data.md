# Chapter 2: Avoiding Bad Data

## Core Idea
Even when you follow the Mom Test, conversations drift toward bad data that gives false negatives and — more dangerously — false positives. Learn to spot and neutralize it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 3 types of bad data** and their fixes:
  1. **Compliments** → **Deflect** them.
  2. **Fluff** → **Anchor** it to specifics in the past.
  3. **Ideas / feature requests** → **Dig beneath** to the motivation.
- **Fluff comes in 3 shapes**: generic claims ("I usually / always / never"), future-tense promises ("I would / will"), hypothetical maybes ("I might / could").
- **Stop seeking approval**: two sources of compliments — **fishing for compliments** (intentional) and **the Pathos Problem** (accidental: exposing your ego makes people protect your feelings).

## Key Concepts
- **The world's most deadly fluff**: "I would definitely buy that." Sounds concrete; people are wildly optimistic about their imagined future selves.
- **Symptoms of compliments**: "Thanks!", "I'm glad you like it", and back at the office: "That meeting went really well", "Everybody loves the idea." If you can't say *why* they liked it or *what* it'd save them, you have a compliment, not data.
- **Dig around feature requests AND emotions**: any strong emotion (angry, embarrassed, overjoyed) is worth exploring. ("It's the worst part of his day and you're not going to figure out why?!")

## Mental Models
- **Anchor the fluff**: "Do you ever X?" → "When's the last time?" → "Talk me through it." A fluffy answer has no value in itself but is an anchor to real facts.
- The MTV story: they asked for **analytics**; the real motive was **keeping their own clients happy** with a fancy emailed report. Build to the motivation, not the request.

## Anti-patterns
- **Taking compliments at face value** as positive data points.
- **Obeying feature requests** → feature creep. "Ideas and feature requests should be understood, but not obeyed."
- **Slipping into pitch mode** — "Anyone will say your idea is great if you're annoying enough about it." If you slip, apologize and recover.
- **Interrupting to 'fix' their understanding** — "The more you're talking, the worse you're doing."

## Key Takeaways
1. **Compliments are the fool's gold of customer learning: shiny, distracting, entirely worthless.**
2. **People will lie to you if they think it's what you want to hear** / if you've mentioned your idea, they'll protect your feelings.
3. **While using generics, people describe who they want to be, not who they are** — get specific to find the edge cases.
4. A "complainer" who won't act ≠ a customer: "If they haven't looked for ways of solving it already, they're not going to look for (or buy) yours."
