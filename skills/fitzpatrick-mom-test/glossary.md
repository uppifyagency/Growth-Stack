# Glossary — The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)

**Advancement** — The customer moving to the next step of your real-world acquisition funnel (Ch 5).

**Advisory flip** — Going into conversations looking for *advisors* rather than customers; removes neediness and puts you in control (Ch 6).

**Anchor (fluff)** — Pulling a generic/hypothetical answer back to a specific example in the past ("When's the last time? Talk me through it") (Ch 2).

**Bad data** — False negatives/positives from conversations; three types: compliments, fluff, ideas (Ch 2).

**Commitment** — The customer giving up something they value — time, reputation, or cash — to show they're serious (Ch 5).

**Compliment** — Worthless praise; "the fool's gold of customer learning." Deflect it (Ch 2).

**Currencies of conversation** — What a commitment is paid in: time, reputation risk, cash (Ch 5).

**Customer slicing** — Repeatedly narrowing a broad segment into finer who-where pairs until you know exactly who to talk to and where to find them (Ch 7).

**Deflect (compliments)** — Acknowledging and moving past praise to get back to facts ("how are you dealing with this at the moment?") (Ch 2).

**Dig (beneath ideas/emotions)** — Exploring the motivation behind a feature request or strong emotion instead of obeying/recording it (Ch 2).

**Earlyvangelist** — (Steve Blank) Your crazy first customer: has the problem, knows it, has budget, and has already built a makeshift solution (Ch 5).

**Fluff** — Generic claims ("usually/always/never"), future promises ("would/will"), and hypotheticals ("might/could") (Ch 2).

**Framing (a meeting)** — Vision / Framing / Weakness / Pedestal / Ask ("Very Few Wizards Properly Ask") (Ch 6).

**Gaze upon the elephant** — Facing the big, scary failure point instead of obsessing over an interesting-but-minor one (Ch 3).

**Hack it** — Skipping process to get the result directly (e.g. just call them) — the Gordian-knot move (Ch 8).

**Important question** — One whose answer could completely change or disprove your business; you should be terrified of at least one per conversation (Ch 3).

**Keeping it casual** — Replacing formal meetings with chats to learn faster and bias less (Ch 4).

**Learning bottleneck** — One person hoarding the customer learning, becoming a de-facto dictator (Ch 8).

**Love bad news** — Treating clear negatives/lukewarm signals as valuable truth (Ch 3).

**Meeting Anti-Pattern** — Relegating every conversation into a scheduled calendar block (Ch 4).

**Mom Test (the)** — Three rules for un-lie-able questions: talk about their life; ask specifics in the past; talk less, listen more (Ch 1).

**Pathos Problem** — Accidentally exposing your ego so people protect your feelings with compliments (Ch 2).

**Premature zoom** — Drilling into a problem before confirming it's a must-solve; a big source of false positives (Ch 3).

**Product risk** — Can I build it? grow it? will they keep using it? (validated by building, not talking) (Ch 3).

**Market risk** — Do they want it? will they pay? are there enough of them? (validated by conversations) (Ch 3).

**Rule of thumb** — The book's punchy one-line takeaways (e.g. "Opinions are worthless") (all chapters).

**Segment (who-where pair)** — A customer group specific enough that you know both who they are and where to find them (Ch 7).

**Talk less** — "The more you're talking, the worse you're doing" (Ch 2).

**Warm intro** — A credibility-establishing introduction through a mutual contact; the goal of all cold outreach (Ch 6).

**Zombie lead** — A friendly "customer" who never commits; a sign you didn't push for advancement (Ch 5).
