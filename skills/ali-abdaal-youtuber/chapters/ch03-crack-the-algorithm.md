# Chapter 3: Crack The Algorithm

## Core Idea
There is no algorithm to hack — there is only **The Million Dollar Formula**: (1) get people to **click** on your video, and (2) **keep them watching**. The algorithm follows the preferences of people; people don't follow the algorithm. Master those two things and you can go from zero to viral; everything else (tags, keywords, likes) is noise. This session is about earning the click (title + thumbnail) and the first move of retention (the hook).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Million Dollar Formula** — the entire job of a YouTuber compresses to two things: *earn the click* and *keep them watching*. Use it as the lens for every decision: if a change doesn't improve click-through or watch-time, it doesn't matter. There is "no more secret to it."
- **Earning The Click = Title + Thumbnail (only two levers)** — these are the *only* things you can play with to win the click. Titles are weightier: "I can predict a video's performance 70 to 80% based on its title," ~20% on the thumbnail. Use when planning any video — a pro won't film unless they already have a good title.
- **The 5 Rules Of A Good Title** — (1) **Sell the result, not the process** (go one level up: "how I take notes from books" → "how I remember everything I read"; the spotlight effect → "how to beat imposter syndrome"). (2) **Intrigue, not agreement** — put a *question* in the viewer's mind, don't state the answer ("I was wrong, the real secret to productivity" beats "why having fun makes you productive"). (3) **Don't describe** the content ("how to 100x your productivity" beats "10 tips for being productive"). (4) **Optimize for humans, not the algorithm** — no keyword stuffing; picture the specific viewer who'd enjoy it. (5) **A/B test your titles** (pro-only; needs volume).
- **Generate 20 Titles Per Video** — the highest-ROI habit in the whole course. Takes ~10 minutes, outperforms any amount of editing/research/storytelling. Brainstorm parties / crowdsourcing in the community is the scaled version.
- **Search vs Browse Spectrum** — decide where a video sits before titling it. **Browse** (~90% of YouTube traffic: homepage + suggested) wants clickbaity, emotional, attention-grabbing titles and is the *only* real path to viral/breakout growth. **Search** (~10%) wants niche, specific, question-answering titles; gives a long-tail trickle, almost never goes viral. Use search when your topic is inherently searched (e.g. "iPhone review", Salesforce tutorials); default to browse for growth.
- **The 3 Rules Of A Good Thumbnail** — (1) **Make it pop** — use a *real photo, not a screen-grab*, then over-edit it (Lightroom/Photoshop: ramp contrast, lift shadows, bump exposure/vibrance; edit at small size so it isn't overkill). (2) **Think about the vibe** — "your vibe attracts your tribe"; match the audience. (3) **Emotion is good if it fits the vibe.** A/B test thumbnails too (variations have beaten originals by 27–200% CTR).
- **The Hook (first 10–30 seconds)** — front-load intrigue to flatten the inevitable early retention drop-off. Hook recipes: **ask a question**, **share a surprising fact**, **offer a transformation** ("this horrible lighting → this, for under $50"), **provide a tip**, **show loads of B-roll** with upbeat music, or **flash-forward** to exciting bits later in the video (MrBeast). More B-roll in the first 30s generally correlates with better retention.

## Key Concepts
- **Click-through rate (CTR)** — clicks per impression; the metric the title/thumbnail move. A/B variations are judged on it.
- **Clickbait vs Intrigue** — scrub "clickbait" from your vocabulary; something is only clickbait if the **promise** (title + thumbnail) isn't **delivered** in the video. Intrigue = a clickbaity promise you actually keep.
- **The promise/delivery contract** — a title and thumbnail create an *expectation*; the video must satisfy it (else viewers feel "missold").
- **Title the result, not the process** — the process (taking notes, the spotlight effect, "just post") is the *means*; the result (remember everything, beat imposter syndrome, get over fear) is what the viewer actually wants.
- **Retention graph** — typical shape: steep early drop-off then a slow trickle up; the hook's job is to shrink the early drop (e.g. 66% vs 74% still watching at the 30s mark).
- **Impressions** — how many times YouTube *offered* your video (e.g. 19.2M impressions → 780k clicks). Proof "the algorithm isn't against you" — conversion to a click is your title/thumbnail's job.
- **how I vs how to** — "how I" reads as a guide (humble, "this worked for me"); "how to" reads as a guru / advice-giver; both work, test it.
- **The vibe / aesthetic axis** — "looks like a great Instagram feed" (Peter McKinnon, Matt D'Avella, Pickup Limes) vs "YouTube-y MrBeast face" (big cut-out face, wide eyes, shock expression).

## Mental Models
- **Think in titles, not videos** — pros don't film a video until they have a great title; the question "what's the title?" becomes a spinal reflex on hearing any idea. If there's no good title, don't make the video.
- Use the **Instagram-feed test** for thumbnails: ask "would this look classy on an Instagram feed?" rather than "would vidIQ say more people click on it?" — for sophisticated (non-kid) audiences, aesthetics beat the data-driven shock-face.
- **Balance the art and the science** of thumbnails: the *science* (big face, cut-out, whites of eyes, strong emotion) is real but skews to kid audiences; the *art* is a coherent, classy channel a viewer subconsciously trusts.
- Think of a viral video as **standard, standard, standard, VIRAL, standard** — you only need one or two viral videos a year; targeting **browse** is what makes that lottery winnable.
- **Stand out by zigging** — when everyone copies the MrBeast face, an aesthetic, restrained channel stands out *more*.

## Anti-patterns
- Watching all the course content "until life is sorted" and never filming — those people never succeed; consistency beats content consumption.
- **Keyword/tag stuffing** and chasing "the algorithm" — keywords don't matter anymore; let TubeBuddy/vidIQ auto-fill tags and forget them.
- **Descriptive titles** ("10 tips for being productive", "my system for taking notes") — fine once you have an audience, dead on arrival for beginners.
- **Titling the process instead of the result** (e.g. "just post", "the spotlight effect") — kills intrigue.
- Real clickbait — a promise the video doesn't deliver; viewers click off, watch-time tanks, the video dies.
- Using a **browse title for a search video** (or vice versa) — mismatched intent.
- **Screen-grab thumbnails** and dark/unedited images — they don't pop in the feed.
- Wearing the wrong **vibe** — MrBeast shock-thumbnails on a channel targeting 30-something entrepreneurs.
- Over-thinking the **meta-game** (too many "how I" titles, "samey" thumbnails) — 99.9% of your audience finds you via search/browse/suggested and neither knows nor cares about your channel's patterns.
- Beginners burning time on **A/B testing**, revamping old titles/thumbnails, or worrying about likes/dislikes — none move the needle (dislikes don't affect performance; bad old videos are usually just bad videos).

## Key Takeaways
1. Everything reduces to **earn the click + keep them watching**. Stop trying to game an algorithm that only ever rewards human clicks and watch-time.
2. **The title is ~70–80% of the game.** Don't film until you have a strong one; brainstorm **20 titles** (~10 min) for every video.
3. **Sell the result, create intrigue, don't describe, optimize for humans** — and pick **browse vs search** deliberately (browse = viral potential, default to it).
4. Thumbnails: **real photo + heavy edit to make it pop**, matched to your **vibe**; emotion only if it fits. Aesthetics often beat the data-driven shock-face for sophisticated audiences.
5. Reframe "clickbait" as **intrigue**: make a bold promise, then *deliver* it — that's how "How I ranked first at Cambridge" hit 5M views honestly.
6. Win the **first 10–30 seconds** with a hook (question / surprising fact / transformation / tip / B-roll / flash-forward); pros spend ~half their effort on the first minute.
7. A/B test titles *and* thumbnails once you have volume (TubeBuddy) — CTR lifts of 20–200% are real and free wins.
