# Chapter 7: Guest Workshops I — Growth, Niching & Repurposing

## Core Idea
Make one great long-form asset (the YouTube video) and let everything else grow as a byproduct: repurpose it everywhere, niche it around a *who* with a painful problem, monetise it through a small core of fans, and build it on your own unfair advantages. Distribution and depth of relationship beat raw output and reach.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Big Domino (Joe Gannon)** — pick the *one* thing that, when done, makes everything else easier or unnecessary. Your YouTube video is the big domino: it has visuals, high-quality audio, and a script/transcript, so it knocks over LinkedIn, Twitter, TikTok, Instagram, blog, and newsletter as byproducts. Use when you feel pulled to grow five platforms in isolation.
- **Pillar Content + Butchering + Fire Moments (Joe Gannon)** — *Pillar content* = the long-form video/podcast/blog. *Butchering* = chopping it into many formats (clips, threads, carousels, articles). *Fire moments* = the standout 30–60s sound bites worth clipping (they also promote the long-form video). Use after every upload: "what is the title that allows for the most future content?"
- **Cross-Platform Pollination / The Most Effective Repurposing Plan (Joe Gannon)** — make a titled, subtitled clip in square (≤60s → FB/Twitter/LinkedIn/IG) and portrait (Reels/TikTok/Shorts) once, drop into a reusable template, and cast the net as wide as possible. Use as the 80/20 first approach for beginners.
- **The Artery Method (Ali, via Joe)** — one central asset feeds outward into every channel so the audience perceives huge output while you only made one thing.
- **The 5 P's of a Sustainable Community (Anne-Laure)** — **Pact** (the give-get co-creation deal, not just a one-way *promise*) → **People** (who, and what relationships: 1:1, masterminds, peer vs mentor, live vs async) → **Platform** (owned vs non-owned; private vs public) → **Promotion** (where your ideal members hang out; ambassadors, referrals) → **Profit** (who pays + how: members, customers, sponsors, ads; recurring vs one-off). Use before launching a paid community.
- **Super Fans / The Inverted Funnel (Pat Flynn)** — flip the SEO funnel. Take people up the pyramid: **casual audience → active audience → community → super fans**. Invest in the experiences of the few at the top; they become your marketing team and bring new people in *from the inside*. Use when chasing reach is producing weak engagement.
- **The MILES Framework (Hasan Kubba)** — find your unfair advantages, built on a foundation of **Mindset**: **M**oney · **I**ntelligence & insight · **L**ocation & luck (right place, right time) · **E**ducation & expertise · **S**tatus (how you come across + who you know + inner confidence). Use to choose what to double down on and what to delegate.
- **The Niche Checklist / Niche Test (Hasan Kubba)** — to blow up fast, a niche should pass: (1) target audience has a **painful and urgent problem** (or an irrational passion); (2) they are **actively seeking a solution**; (3) they have **few or no *perceived* options** (achieved by narrowing). Use to gut-check a channel/product idea against a "blow up fast" goal.

## Key Concepts
- **Distribution is the other 95%** — the video is 95% of the *effort* but distribution can carry 95% of the *impact* (reach). Don't post and pray.
- **Sell your book by the sentence (Jordan Peterson clip)** — a long-form recording can be chopped into 30s/1m/5m/20m pieces, each finding a specialised home.
- **Organic vs paid reach; uphill vs downhill** — favour high-organic-reach platforms (LinkedIn — only ~2% of users post; TikTok; Reels; Shorts) over saturated ones (running downhill vs uphill for the same distance).
- **Pact vs promise (Anne-Laure)** — a newsletter is a *promise* (you give, they consume); a community needs a *pact* (members co-create value in a virtuous cycle).
- **One inch wide, one mile deep (Pat Flynn)** — a small creator's advantage is deep relationships, not "100 inches wide, 1 inch deep" reach.
- **Own your audience (Pat Flynn)** — email is owned; YouTube/IG/Amazon are rented and can be zeroed by an algorithm overnight. Use a **content upgrade** (free PDF behind an email) to convert viewers.
- **Jab, jab, jab, right hook (Gary V, via Pat)** — give value repeatedly, then make the ask; the "right hook" only lands after years of jabs.
- **Niches are needs; start with WHO not WHAT (Hasan)** — define the person and the unmet need, not the subject area. A *younger version of yourself* is a powerful target (best access to the insight).
- **Painkiller vs vitamin (Hasan)** — solve a need-to-have, not a nice-to-have; painkillers sell faster.
- **Storytelling as the niche gap (James Jani)** — in an info-dense niche (finance), documentary-style storytelling — hook → questions → "finding the truth" → stakes/pacing → resolution — was the unfilled gap that drove 0→700k in ~12 videos.
- **Package a personal story as a broad topic (James)** — "my experience with self-help" becomes "The Toxic World of Self-Help": more emotive, more general, more clickable.
- **Script as you research (James)** — write a deliberately crappy first draft while gathering sources; box each research question to ~10–15 min.

## Mental Models
- **Tortoise vs hare (Joe)** — repurposing is the tortoise: consistent, sustainable output beats a burnout burst of three videos in a week.
- Think of **passive income as making you passive** (Hasan) — once systems run, motivation to do even tiny tasks drops; design for that.
- Use the **two-ladders** model (Pat) — you can't climb the new (entrepreneurial) ladder while one hand grips the old (corporate) one; commitment unlocks actions you'd otherwise avoid.
- Treat failure as **"the result you wanted or the lesson you needed" (Pat)** — true failure is giving up; be the "crash test dummy" who experiments openly.
- **Disaster before master (Pat)** — your first ~100 cringe videos teach titles, thumbnails, hooks, and storytelling; ship through the mess.

## Anti-patterns
- Growing each social platform in isolation instead of feeding them from one pillar asset (ignores the Big Domino).
- Burning out by front-loading content instead of sustainably repurposing (hare, not tortoise).
- Posting clips without titles or subtitles — ~80% watch social video on mute; a template + title is what stops the scroll.
- Obsessing over the community **platform** while neglecting the Pact, People, Promotion, and Profit.
- Chasing maximum reach/SEO with no super-fan experience — see the 2M-follower creator who sold 28 t-shirts.
- Building only on rented platforms with no owned email list (one algorithm change or hack can zero you).
- Defining a niche as a *subject* ("tech") rather than a *who + need*; targeting people who have a problem but aren't *seeking* a solution (e.g. posture vs "pain-free back").
- Chasing someone else's overnight numbers — treat YouTube as a 2–3 year game (James grew fast *because* of prior skills, not despite the timeline).
- Researching for hours, then scripting from cold notes (script as you research instead).

## Key Takeaways
1. Find your **big domino** (the long-form video) and make everything else a byproduct via butchering and fire moments.
2. Repurpose into **titled, subtitled square + portrait clips** dropped into a reusable template — the highest-leverage 80/20 distribution move.
3. Favour **high-organic-reach** platforms you actually enjoy and where your audience lives; run downhill, not uphill.
4. Build **super fans** with deep experiences, and **own** the relationship via an email list and content upgrades.
5. For a community, design all **5 P's** before launch; the Pact (co-creation) matters more than the platform.
6. Niche on **who + a painful, urgent problem they're actively solving with few perceived options**; target a younger version of yourself.
7. Audit your **MILES** unfair advantages on a Mindset foundation — double down on strengths, delegate weaknesses; status and insight are the heaviest levers.
