# Chapter 11: Bonus — Speaking, Notion & Copyright

## Core Idea
Three creator-skill bonuses, each built on one liberating realization. Speaking: the audience cares about themselves, not you — get out of your own head and just be a louder, smiling, personality-led version of how you talk to friends. Notion: it has only **two features** — **pages** and **databases** — and everything else is you over-complicating it. Copyright (guest: Devin Stone / Legal Eagle): almost everything you see online is copyrighted the instant it's created, and the only safe ground for using others' work is the **fair use** exception, not "it's small / free / public / I changed it."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Get The Hell Out Of Your Own Head** (speaking tip #1) — Use when nerves or self-consciousness are throttling a talk or to-camera take. How: remember "the audience does not care about me… the audience only cares about themselves" — they care about the value you provide, so worrying about how *you* come across is counterproductive and gets in the way of the message.
- **Speak Through A Smile** (tip #2) — Use whenever a take sounds flat. How: physically smile while talking; the same words instantly read as more engaging and enthusiastic.
- **Avoid The British Airways Mode** (a.k.a. "primary school teacher voice") (tip #3) — Use when you catch yourself in a fake, sing-songy "I'm giving a speech" voice that goes *up* at sentence ends. How: speak matter-of-factly and let your pitch trend *down*; talk how you'd talk to friends, not like an air-hostess announcement.
- **Let Your Personality Shine Through** (tip #4) — Use for any presentation, even serious/medical/business ones. How: ramp the quirky stuff *up* not down — toilet jokes, Harry Potter references, banter; the audience wants a personality, not formality.
- **Add Banter, Humor & Stories** (tip #5) — Use to keep an audience engaged. How: assume the only thing they truly want is to have fun; insert jokes, banter, storytelling (book ref: *Storyworthy* by Matthew Dicks).
- **Don't Read From A Script — Use Talking Points** (tip #6) — Use for speeches and to-camera. How: prepare an outline (how you'll start, how you'll finish, broad bullet points) but not a full script; over-preparation forces you back into British Airways mode.
- **Speak Louder (5→7 out of 10)** (tip #7) — Use to inject energy. How: treat your casual baseline as a 5/10 and deliberately push to a 7/10 for video/stage (a 7 would feel excessive at a dinner table — that's the point).
- **Speak Slower & Pause More — The Snow Globe** (tip #8) — Use when you race through material. How: a pause feels far shorter to the audience than to you; pauses let the "snow globe settle" so the audience can appreciate what you just said. (Learned via the *Ultra Speaking* course.)
- **Play Public-Speaking Games** (tip #9) — Use to practice deliberately. How: e.g. the **Triple Step** game (give a speech on a random topic while integrating random words as they appear on screen).
- **Pages + Databases (the only two Notion features)** — Use to learn or teach Notion without overwhelm. How: a **page** is "basically a Google Doc" you can nest infinitely (pages within pages); a **database** is "basically a spreadsheet" — and crucially "a database is just a collection of pages." Master these two and you can build anything.
- **Database Templates** — Use when every record needs the same starting structure (e.g. a video brief: title, thumbnail, hook, intro, conclusion). How: in any database, hit the *New* dropdown → *New template*; Notion then does the copy-paste for you on each new entry.

## Key Concepts
- **The audience only cares about itself** — the foundational reframe behind nearly every speaking tip.
- **Talking points, not scripts** — an outline beats a script because scripts can't be made to *not* sound like scripts.
- **Page** — Notion's atomic unit; an infinitely nestable Google-Doc-like blank slate. "Everything in Notion is just a page."
- **Database** — a spreadsheet of pages; same data, multiple **views** (table, board/Kanban, calendar, list, gallery, timeline) — the view is just a different presentation of the same pages.
- **Property type** — a database column's data type (text, date, person, etc.); the *person* type lets you tag/notify a teammate (e.g. assign an editor).
- **Fair use** (US, 17 USC §107; "fair dealing" in the UK) — a *right*, not a permission-based exception: a complicated four-factor analysis you must run before using anyone's work.
- **Tangible medium** — copyright attaches only to expression fixed in a recorded/written form, not to ideas.
- **Derivative work** — any adaptation, transformation, trace, cover, or recreation-from-scratch of a copyrighted work; controlling it is the original owner's exclusive right.
- **Public ≠ public domain** — being accessible online doesn't strip copyright; works enter the public domain only when copyright expires (often 120 yrs after death; anything pre-1924 is safe).

## Mental Models
- **Think of public speaking as "how I talk to my friends," not "I'm giving a speech."** The moment it becomes "a speech," you go stuffy, formal, and sing-songy.
- **Treat a pause like shaking a snow globe** — silence lets the audience's attention settle on the point you just made; it's never as long to them as it feels to you.
- **Don't be intimidated by a Notion power-user's setup** — like a finished painting, you only see the final layers, not the months of foundation. Start simple; build up over weeks.
- **Assume anything online is copyrighted until proven otherwise** — "small," "free to make," "I changed it," and "it's public" are all *not* defenses. The only real defense is fair use.

## Anti-patterns
- Getting in your own head about body language/sweat/judgment — it sabotages the message.
- The **British Airways / primary-school-teacher voice**: fake, exaggerated, pitch rising at every sentence end.
- Reading from a script, or over-preparing until you sound like you're reading one.
- Racing through delivery to "respect people's time" — it just makes the talk weaker.
- Copying a Notion guru's complex template on day one — "trying to run before you can walk." (Maxim from the chat: *"Premature organization is the queen of procrastination."*)
- Over-investing in keyboard shortcuts as a beginner — "just another way to get overwhelmed"; use slash-commands and click, learn shortcuts later by Googling them.
- Believing the copyright myths: ideas are protectable (no — patents); only fine art counts (no — selfies/cat videos qualify); you must register/notice it (no — copyright is automatic); online = public domain; "less than 5 sec / 200 words / 10%" is safe (no bright-line rule); altering/transforming or recreating from scratch makes it yours; non-commercial or "I'm a business so fair use doesn't apply" — all false. Note: **small use ≠ fair use**, and fair use does **not** require the rights holder's permission.

## Key Takeaways
1. Make every talk about the audience, then make yourself louder, smiling, and more *you* — ramp personality up, not down.
2. Use talking points and pauses (snow globe); avoid scripts and British Airways pitch.
3. Practice speaking deliberately with games (Triple Step) and courses (Ultra Speaking).
4. Notion is only pages + databases; a database is just a collection of pages viewed different ways. ~95% of a real setup is pages-in-pages, ~5% pages-in-databases.
5. Keep Notion to ~2 layers as a beginner; add database templates only when you need repeatable structure.
6. Copyright is automatic and near-universal; the *only* safe path to using others' work is genuine fair use (or licensing/permission) — run the four-factor test, don't trust myths.
