# Glossary — Part-Time YouTuber Academy (Ali Abdaal)

**A.B.C.D.E. of Personalization** — five dimensions that elevate a video to a lovable channel: Aesthetic, Branding, Confidence, Delivery, Editing (Ch 4).
**A-roll / A-cut** — A-roll is talking-to-camera footage; the A-cut is the first rough pass (~1–2 hrs), the "gateway drug" to outsourcing whole videos (Ch 5).
**Architect vs Archaeologist** — two routes to a niche: the up-front planner (~2%) vs the digger who discovers it by making videos (~98%) (Ch 2).
**Audience funnel direction** — the flow YouTube → social → free newsletter → paid Patreon; the free tier funnels into the paid (Ch 9).
**Audio Level 2** — the near-term target: a shotgun mic on a cheap stand + a $10 extension cable, a hand's-breadth from the mouth, out of frame (Ch 10).
**Batch Filming** — filming several videos in one recurring session to bank a buffer and protect consistency (Ch 9).
**Benevolent coercion** — Dicks's idea of drawing people emotionally into your story, earning the right to teach them a lesson (Ch 8).
**The Big Domino** — the single asset (your YouTube video) that, once done, makes all other channels grow as byproducts (Ch 7).
**Bowens mount** — a standard light-to-softbox mount that lets you mix and match brands (Ch 10).
**British Airways Mode / voice** — the fake, sing-songy delivery that rises in pitch at sentence ends; avoid by speaking matter-of-factly, trending down (Ch 4, Ch 11).
**B-roll** — supporting footage that lifts production value; a reusable B-roll library is an efficiency hack (Ch 4, Ch 5).
**Butchering** — chopping pillar content into many derivatives (e.g. one clip per point of a "15 tips" listicle) (Ch 5, Ch 7).
**C.L.E.A.R.** — Perell's clear-writing acronym: Create a rhythm, Link sentences, Eliminate confusion, Add colorful details, Remove unnecessary words (Ch 8).
**Camera Talker ↔ Editing Jedi Master Spectrum** — range from personality-led, fast-to-produce creators to production-value-led, slow-but-distinctive ones (Ch 4).
**Cinema of Storytelling** — Dicks's principle that location is key: tell a story in distinct scenes, as movies always show you where you are (Ch 8).
**Clickbait vs intrigue** — a title is only clickbait if the promise isn't delivered; intrigue is a bold promise you actually keep (Ch 3).
**Compounding** — growth that is near-flat then suddenly vertical, so consistency now matters more than visible results now (Ch 1).
**Content Upgrade** — a free resource (e.g. a PDF) gated behind an email to convert viewers into an owned list (Ch 7).
**CPM** — cost per mille: what an advertiser pays per 1,000 ad impressions (Ch 6).
**Cross-Platform Pollination** — repurposing one titled, subtitled clip in square and portrait so it fits and grows across many platforms (Ch 5, Ch 7).
**Crop vs full-frame sensor** — crop sensors are cheaper/smaller; ×1.5 the focal length for the full-frame equivalent (16mm crop ≈ 24mm) (Ch 10).
**CTR (Click-through rate)** — clicks ÷ impressions; what title/thumbnail optimize and A/B tests judge (Ch 3).
**Database (Notion)** — a spreadsheet whose rows are pages; the same data shown through different views (table, board, calendar) (Ch 11).
**Database Template** — a reusable page format inside a Notion database (e.g. a video brief), auto-copied into each new record (Ch 11).
**Dead cat** — a fluffy windscreen over a shotgun mic that dampens wind noise outdoors (Ch 10).
**Depth of field** — background blur from aperture; lower f-number + more distance to the back wall reads as professional (Ch 10).
**Derivative work** — any adaptation, trace, cover, or recreation-from-scratch of a copyrighted work; the owner's exclusive right (Ch 11).
**Disaster before master** — your first ~100 cringe videos build the title/thumbnail/hook/storytelling skills before you strike gold (Ch 7).
**Earning the click** — winning the click using the only two levers: title and thumbnail (Ch 3).
**Fair use** — a right (US 17 USC §107; UK "fair dealing"), a four-factor test, that lets you use copyrighted work without permission, even commercially (Ch 11).
**Fire Moments** — the standout 30–60s segments of long-form content; tighten and thumbnail them into micro content (Ch 5, Ch 7).
**Five-Second Moment** — the instant of transformation/realization every story is really about; used as the ending, with its opposite as the beginning (Ch 8).
**Format-borrowing** — taking a popular format from broad YouTube and twisting it into your own niche (Ch 9).
**Game of access, not skill** — Ultraspeaking's reframe that you're already capable; confidence is accessed in the moment, not learned from scratch (Ch 8).
**Get The Hell Out Of Your Own Head** — speaking tip #1: the audience cares about itself and the value you provide, not how you come across (Ch 11).
**Getting-better metric** — track improvement in talking/filming/editing rather than views/subscribers as the right early-stage measure (Ch 9).
**HIVES Framework** — video structure: Hook, Introduction (who + value proposition), Value, End-screen Sales pitch (Ch 4).
**The hook** — the first 10–30s of a video, front-loaded with intrigue to flatten the early retention drop (Ch 3).
**Hobbyist-to-Business Spectrum** — a scale where gear importance rises with how entrepreneurially you treat the channel; hobbyists can use a phone (Ch 10).
**Homework for Life** — Dicks's daily 5-minute practice of writing one line capturing the most story-worthy moment of the day (Ch 8).
**Ideal Viewer Avatar** — a named, specific persona (e.g. "Medic Mo") answering who they are, what they want, what's at stake, and their fears (Ch 2).
**Implicit vs Explicit Value Proposition** — explicit = what you literally say in your intro; implicit = what you hope the ideal viewer silently thinks (Ch 2).
**Impressions** — how many times YouTube offered your video; proof the algorithm is working — converting to a click is your title/thumbnail's job (Ch 3).
**Intrigue not agreement** — put an unanswered question in the viewer's mind instead of a claim they nod at and scroll past (Ch 3).
**Inverted Funnel / Super Fans** — walk people from casual audience → active → community → super fans, who then market for you (Ch 7).
**Jab, jab, jab, right hook** — give value repeatedly (jabs) before the ask (right hook = buy/subscribe/donate); the ask only lands after sustained value (Ch 6, Ch 7).
**Key light** — the single light Ali deems necessary: as big and soft as possible, 45° to the side and slightly above the face (Ch 10).
**Luck-Work-Unfair Advantages (Secret Sauce)** — the three inputs behind fast growth: uncontrollable luck, controllable work (video volume), and a compounding pre-existing edge (Ch 1).
**Manufactured Luck** — engineering the lucky break: ~100 reps plus obsessive prep before going viral, rather than waiting for it (Ch 1).
**Me but younger and more incompetent** — target-audience shortcut: aim at yourself a few years ago, plus struggles you didn't actually have (Ch 2).
**Me-Mindset to Audience-Mindset shift** — moving from "what do I want to make?" to "what best serves the audience?" to convert a habit into growth (Ch 1).
**Micro content** — short-form derivatives (clips, threads, quotes) atomized from one pillar piece, ideally published ~1 min after it (Ch 5).
**The MILES Framework** — unfair advantages: Money, Intelligence/insight, Location/luck, Education/expertise, Status — on a Mindset foundation (Ch 7).
**The Million Dollar Formula** — the entire job of a YouTuber: get people to click, and keep them watching (Ch 3).
**Minimum viable kit (~$1,000)** — Canon M50 + Sigma 16mm f/1.4 + Rode VideoMicro + ~$20 stand (Ch 10).
**Niche Commitment Threshold** — committing ~20–40–50 videos to one niche before judging whether it works (Ch 9).
**Niche depth illusion** — a niche looks exhausted from outside but reveals more sub-topics the deeper you go in (Ch 9).
**The Niche Checklist** — a niche should be a painful/urgent problem the audience is actively solving with few perceived options (Ch 7).
**The Niche Formula** — Topic + People: "I make videos about [this topic] for [this type of people]" (Ch 2).
**One inch wide, one mile deep** — a small creator's advantage: deep relationships with few people beat shallow reach with many (Ch 7).
**One Video A Week** — the single behavioural commitment: consistent weekly shipping that builds the habit everything else rests on (Ch 1).
**Own your audience (public vs private)** — public (YouTube/Twitter/TikTok) = rented, algorithm-dependent; private (website/email/community) = owned; grow on public, funnel to private (Ch 5, Ch 7).
**Page (Notion)** — Notion's atomic, infinitely nestable unit — "basically a Google Doc" (Ch 11).
**Painkiller vs Vitamin** — solve a need-to-have (painkiller) rather than a nice-to-have (vitamin); painkillers sell faster (Ch 7).
**Performative Speaking** — Crabtree's term: performance art + words that cause change; speaking as art that shifts a viewer's thinking (Ch 8).
**Pillar Content / Aorta** — the single long-form piece (weekly video/keynote/podcast) from which all other content is derived (Ch 5, Ch 7).
**Practical lights** — visible in-frame light sources (lamps, Edison bulbs, Hue) that add ambient production value and personality (Ch 10).
**Primary school teacher mode** — talking-down-to-five-year-olds delivery; fixed by lowering pitch and going monotone (Ch 4).
**Primal Branding (Values, Rituals & Icons)** — three branding components: what you believe, repeated experiences, and distinctive associated items/traits (Ch 4).
**Probabilistic platform** — YouTube outcomes are odds you improve, never results you can secure (Ch 1).
**Public vs public domain** — being accessible online doesn't remove copyright; works enter the public domain only when copyright expires (pre-1924 is safe) (Ch 11).
**Quantity game** — not making enough videos is the dominant constraint for beginners — volume precedes quality (Ch 1).
**Rapid Fire Analogies / Conductor / Triple Step** — Ultraspeaking practice games training access, range, and staying in character (Ch 8, Ch 11).
**Reps over secret sauce** — comfort and skill come from forcing yourself through ~100 videos, not from a hidden trick (Ch 2).
**Retention graph** — curve of viewers remaining over time: a steep early drop then a slow trickle; the hook shrinks the early drop (Ch 3).
**Retention reality** — retention naturally drops through a video (~20–25% normal early), improves with skill, and isn't always personal (Ch 9).
**RPM** — revenue per mille: what you keep per 1,000 views after YouTube's ~45–50% cut; the number to model income on (Ch 6).
**Riches are in the niches** — a targeted channel grows far more easily than a broad one (Ch 2).
**Script vs Bullet Points** — bullets = conversational, fast, connection-led; full scripts + teleprompter = presentational, for expert channels with writing teams (Ch 4).
**Search vs Browse** — browse (homepage/suggested, ~90% of traffic) drives viral growth via clickbaity titles; search (~10%) gives niche long-tail (Ch 3).
**Self-Imposed Deadline** — a fixed publish cadence that ships work without waiting for motivation (Ch 9).
**Serendipity surface area** — being present on more platforms increases the chance of valuable random connections (Ch 5).
**Sell the result, not the process** — title the outcome the viewer wants (e.g. "remember everything you read") rather than the means (Ch 3).
**The Snow Globe** — pausing model: a pause feels far shorter to the audience than to you and lets attention settle on your point (Ch 8, Ch 11).
**Speak Through A Smile** — physically smiling while talking makes identical words read as more engaging and warm (Ch 4, Ch 11).
**Stack The Deck (Time · Money · Systems)** — tilting a probabilistic platform in your favour via three controllable levers; "no such thing as need" in gear (Ch 1, Ch 10).
**Stay in character** — Ultraspeaking's rule: never signal nervousness, because audiences can't tell unless you tell them (Ch 8).
**Super-fans / zealots** — loyal repeat-watching fans who matter more than raw subscriber count because they train the algorithm (Ch 4, Ch 7).
**Tangible medium** — the recorded/written form expression must be fixed in for copyright to attach; copyright protects expression, not ideas (Ch 11).
**The 3 Levels of Audio** — L0 built-in mic (never), L1 shotgun on camera, L2 same mic off-camera near the face, L3 XLR into an external recorder synced in post (Ch 10).
**The 3,873 number** — TubeBuddy finding that the average 1M-subscriber channel has ~3,873 videos — volume builds channels, not one hit (Ch 1).
**The 4 Budget Tiers** — every gear purchase framed as budget / mid / premium / executive; almost always recommend budget or mid (Ch 10).
**The 4 Levels of Being a YouTuber** — L1 a video at a time, L2 one video a week, L3 one good video a week, L4 scaling across platforms + a team (Ch 5).
**The Four Delivery Tactics** — Crabtree's tactics: slow down, the camera is a person, less is more (10-word answer), open hands / anchor by location (Ch 8).
**The Three Levels of Finding Your Niche** — who am I & what do I want → who is my audience & what do they want → what value am I giving (L3 = L1 + L2) (Ch 2).
**The 5 P's of a Sustainable Community** — Pact, People, Platform, Promotion, Profit — design before launching a community (Ch 7).
**Tell, Title, Tension** — Perell's scripted-intro formula: tell what's coming, name the title, open a tension question — in the first ~50 words (Ch 8).
**10-second rule (pattern interrupt)** — something should change on screen roughly every 10 seconds to hold attention (1-second = the extreme version) (Ch 4).
**Try-then-refine** — force a rough attempt before teaching the refinement; trying first makes the lesson land harder (Ch 2).
**Vibe attracts tribe** — thumbnail/channel aesthetic should match the target audience; for sophisticated viewers a classy "Instagram-feed" look beats the shock-face (Ch 3).
**Watch time** — total minutes viewers watch; maps onto revenue more strongly than views, so longer high-retention videos earn more (Ch 6).
**YouTube Partner Program** — ad monetisation unlocks at 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in the trailing 12 months (Ch 6).
