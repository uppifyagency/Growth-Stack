# Chapter 2: VSL Structures

## Core Idea
A VSL works because it answers the prospect's questions *in the exact order their mind raises them*. Map the script to that question-order and you remove objections before they're conscious. Two script skeletons deliver this: the **one-secret framework** (start here) and the **three-secrets framework** (a small conversion bump, do later).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Prospect's Question-Order** — the spine every VSL follows. In order, the prospect silently asks: *Who is this person? What do they do? How do they do it? Is what they're saying true? Can I trust them? Does he get results? Will this make sense for my situation? What's the next step?* Use it: answer each, in sequence, and the video feels inevitable. Self-check by observing your own thoughts the next time *you* shop for a service.
- **The One-Secret VSL Framework** — the base of selling anything: you hold a "secret" (specific info applied for a specific result) the prospect doesn't. Sequence: **backstory (who you are / how you figured it out)** → **reveal the secret (what it is, how/why it works)** → **examples (bad vs. good)** → **results (ideally quantifiable)** → **how to do the secret (over-exaggerate the difficulty)** → **logical breakdown (the numbers)** → **component breakdown (the many pieces required)** → **CTA**.
- **The Three-Secrets VSL Framework** — coined by **Russell Brunson** (founder of ClickFunnels; read *Traffic Secrets*, *DotCom Secrets*). Same engine, but the middle is three pain-point→resolution beats instead of one. The **whiteboard webinar** delivery is **Dan Henry**'s; study **Alex Becker** for video + ultra-simple landing pages. Use it: when you want a slightly more entertaining, shareable video — but disguise the structure (say "three things I do," never literally "three secrets," because audiences recognize the cliché).

## Key Concepts
- **The bridge metaphor** — the prospect is on one side of a canyon, the result on the other; *you are the bridge*. They come to associate getting the result with hiring you.
- **Over-exaggerate difficulty** — make the secret and the component list look slightly harder than reality so DIY feels unappealing ("I'd rather pay this person").
- **The down-sell** — if a done-for-you prospect balks on the call, offer a **done-with-you** coaching version (e.g., weekly call for 8 weeks), priced slightly lower. Not everyone wants done-for-you.
- **Pricing-in-VSL decision** — high-ticket / non-productized: leave exact price OUT (say "minimum project size 3k+"); put any price on its own end slide so you can swap it in Camtasia without re-filming. Productized / cheap subscription: leave a **pay-now** price on the page (e.g., $149/mo) — people just buy.

## Mental Models
- **Answer questions in their native order** — friction is just an unanswered question arriving early.
- **You must be different** — the "secret" is whatever you do that competitors don't; with so many competitors, undifferentiated = invisible.

## Anti-patterns
- Saying "three secrets" out loud — it's a recognized webinar-funnel tell and feels robotic.
- High production value — unnecessary; content beats polish unless you're a design agency.
- Forcing exact pricing into a complex/high-ticket VSL — it deters and creates unqualified pay-now buyers you must refund.

## Key Takeaways
1. Script in question-order: who → what → how → proof → trust → results → fit → next step.
2. Start with one-secret; graduate to three-secrets for a marginal lift.
3. Decide pricing display by offer type: hide it for complex high-ticket, show pay-now for simple productized.
