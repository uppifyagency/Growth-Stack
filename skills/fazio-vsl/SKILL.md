## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The thesis.** A VSL is a recorded video on your landing page whose only job is to manufacture **trust** at scale. Cold-traffic buyers agree to a call without knowing what you look or sound like; the VSL removes that mystery so the call feels like a *second* conversation. It books more calls and makes them easier. The two real levers in the whole system are the **VSL** and **live chat**; retargeting and design are secondary. Bar to clear: "Are you a real person worth a call?" — not production value.

**The Prospect's Question-Order** (the spine of every VSL and landing page). A buyer silently asks, in this fixed order: *Who is this? What do they do? How do they do it? Is it true? Can I trust them? Does he get results? Will this fit my situation? What's the next step?* Answer each in sequence and the video feels inevitable; friction is just a question answered late or not at all.

**The One-Secret VSL** (start here). Every sale rests on a "secret" — specific knowledge you hold that the prospect doesn't. Beats: **backstory** → **reveal the secret** (what/how/why it beats the norm) → **bad-vs-good examples** → **quantifiable results** → **how to do it** (over-exaggerate the difficulty) → **logical breakdown** (the numbers) → **component breakdown** (the many pieces required) → **CTA**.

**The Three-Secrets VSL** (a small lift, do later). Same engine, but the middle becomes three **pain-point → resolution** beats. Coined by **Russell Brunson** (ClickFunnels; *Traffic Secrets*, *DotCom Secrets*); whiteboard delivery from **Dan Henry**; study **Alex Becker** for video + minimal pages. Disguise it — say "three things I do," never "three secrets."

**The bridge metaphor.** The prospect is on one side of a canyon, the result on the other; *you are the bridge*. Over-exaggerating difficulty (in the how-to and component breakdown) makes DIY unappealing so paying you becomes the rational choice.

**The Big Win Follow-Up Ladder.** When a cold prospect doesn't reply: day-3 **bump** ("just bumping this to the top of your inbox" — drives ~30% of calls) → then **Big Win #1, #2, #3…**, each sharing a fresh client result, indefinitely. Good work makes case studies an infinite pool.

**The 100-emails → 1-client math.** 4% book a meeting (4/100), close 25% → **100 emails = 1 client**; at 5 min × 100 prospects = 8.3 hrs/client. Frame: "Would you spend 8.3 hours to get a client?" → therefore send an infinite number of emails. Personalization (a **concrete compliment** that passes the say-it-out-loud test) ≈ 10× reply rate vs. generic.

**The down-sell.** If a done-for-you prospect balks, offer a **done-with-you** coaching version (e.g., weekly call × 8 weeks) priced slightly below DFY.

**Filming standard.** Convey, don't produce. Daylight on the face (source behind camera), look presentable, show your face, talk fast enough (interest is set in the first 3 seconds), expect 3–10 takes. Pipeline: **Loom** (presenter view) → **Camtasia** (1920×1080, stitch) → **YouTube unlisted** → embed.

**Landing page.** One continuous scroll, no nav, pure white/black background: Headline → VSL → client interview → results → how it works → disqualification → Calendly. **Carrd** ($19/yr) for high-ticket/complex; **ClickFunnels/Kajabi** for cheap productized checkout; **Webflow** when it must look polished. Content > design.

**Pricing-display decision.** High-ticket/complex → omit exact price ("min project 3k+"), keep any price on a swappable end slide. Productized/cheap → show a **pay-now** price (people just buy).

**Live chat + retargeting.** Live chat (**ManyChat**) is a *primary* lever — in-market buyers want to talk now and convert far higher; reply via the Facebook Business Suite app. Retargeting is a backend optimization: Facebook **Pixel** + a **Zapier → Conversions API** lead event on each Calendly booking (beats iOS 14.5 blocking), exclude bookers, run cheap (~$1/day) **Story ads** with manual placements.
