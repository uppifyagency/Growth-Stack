## D-019 1. Channel niche + ideal viewer brief
- ask: "Help me define my YouTube niche and who it's for."
- inputs: the user's strengths, interests and familiar groups, what they wish they'd known 3/5/10 years ago, and their goal on the blow-up ↔ enjoy spectrum.
- data: none (optional: viewer comments once posting).
- upstream: none
- downstream: feeds titles (2), scripts (3), branding (5).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Run timed two-minute brain-dumps for Level 1 (who am I, what do I want), in writer mode, aiming for quantity. [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
2. Level 2: build the Ideal Viewer Avatar (named; wants, blockers, stakes, transformation, fears, top-3 frustrations, beliefs). With no audience yet, use yourself 3-5 years younger. [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
3. Compress to the Niche Formula: "I make videos about [topic] for [people]". [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
4. Gut-check with the Niche Checklist: a painful, urgent problem, actively being solved, with few perceived options (painkiller over vitamin). [chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
5. Pick an angle from the MILES unfair advantages. [chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
6. Write the explicit and implicit value propositions, adding the "how". [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
7. Set the commitment: 20-40-50 videos before judging. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
### criteria
- The niche fits "videos about [topic] for [people]". [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
- The avatar has a name and answers all 9 avatar questions. [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
- The niche is defined by a who + need, not a subject alone. [chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
- The problem passes all 3 checklist items. [chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
- The explicit value prop includes a "how" (not generic life-coach wording). [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
- An implicit value prop is written. [chapters/ch02-conceive-your-ideas.md]
- A video-count threshold is stated. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]

## D-020 2. Packaging set (titles + thumbnail brief)
- ask: "Give me 20 titles and a thumbnail concept for this video idea."
- inputs: the video idea, the avatar, the channel vibe and colour palette, and whether the video targets browse or search.
- data: optional TubeBuddy (A/B), thumbsup.tv (preview), and the channel's past CTR.
- upstream: niche brief (1)
- downstream: gates the script (3); thumbnail photo needs the gear/setup (9).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Decide browse or search before titling (default to browse for growth). [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
2. Brainstorm ≥20 titles in ~10 minutes. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
3. Filter by the 5 rules: sell the result, intrigue not agreement, don't describe, optimize for humans, A/B test (pro only). [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
4. Pick a title you'd click. If none qualifies, don't make the video. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
5. Brief the thumbnail early: a real photo (manual, wide aperture, ~1/200), heavily edited at small size, matched to the vibe, with emotion only if it fits. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md, patterns.md]
6. Make the title and thumbnail tell one story with no duplicated text, in the channel palette. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
### criteria
- There are ≥20 distinct title options. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The chosen title names a result, not the process or method. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The chosen title raises a question rather than stating the answer. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The title is not a content description ("10 tips for…"). [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The title style matches the declared intent (browse: emotional/intriguing; search: specific/question-answering). [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The thumbnail brief specifies a shot photo, not a screen-grab. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The thumbnail text does not repeat the title text. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
- The video delivers the promise (no true clickbait), checked against the script. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]

## D-021 3. Video script (HIVES + Tell-Title-Tension)
- ask: "Write the script for my video on X."
- inputs: the chosen title and promise, the avatar, the content points, and the channel type (camera talker vs expert/presentational).
- data: none (optional: the retention graph of past videos).
- upstream: packaging (2)
- downstream: feeds filming, repurposing (6) and the story segment (4).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Structure with HIVES: Hook · Introduction (who you are + value prop) · Value · End-screen Sales pitch. [chapters/ch04-keep-them-watching-retention.md]
2. Hook for the first 10-30s: a question, a surprising fact, a transformation, a tip, B-roll with music, or a flash-forward. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
3. Write the intro with Tell, Title, Tension in ≤50 words: reference the title in the first two sentences, stack stats, and end on a question disguised as a statement. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
4. Default to bullet talking points for camera talkers. Use a full script only for expert/presentational channels. [chapters/ch04-keep-them-watching-retention.md, chapters/ch11-bonus-speaking-notion-copyright.md]
5. Edit with C.L.E.A.R.: rhythm, linked sentences, no confusion, specific details, no consulting words. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
6. Mark a pattern interrupt (B-roll, zoom, sound) roughly every 10 seconds. [chapters/ch04-keep-them-watching-retention.md]
### criteria
- All four HIVES sections are present and labelled. [chapters/ch04-keep-them-watching-retention.md]
- The title is referenced within the first two sentences. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- The intro is ≤50 words and ends on an open tension. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- The hook uses one of the 6 named recipes. [chapters/ch03-crack-the-algorithm.md]
- The answer to the title's question is not given in the hook. [patterns.md]
- The script contains none of the consulting words (synergy, disruptive, empower). [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- Sentence lengths vary (no long staccato runs). [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- The script ends with an end-screen pitch. [chapters/ch04-keep-them-watching-retention.md]

## D-022 4. Personal story segment
- ask: "Turn this experience into a story for my video."
- inputs: a Homework-for-Life log or a remembered moment of change.
- data: none.
- upstream: none
- downstream: slots into the script (3).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Keep a daily Homework for Life (one line on the most story-worthy moment of the day). [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
2. Find the five-second moment of transformation or realization, and make it the ending. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
3. Write the beginning as its opposite. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
4. Tell the middle in distinct scenes, each with a location. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
5. Begin with forward movement, and package the story as a broad, emotive topic. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md, chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
### criteria
- A single moment of change is identifiable. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- The opening state is the opposite of the ending state. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- Every scene names a location. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]
- It is not a report of events without change. [chapters/ch08-guest-workshops-writing-speaking.md]

## D-024 6. Repurposing plan
- ask: "Turn my weekly video into content for other platforms."
- inputs: a published long-form video (with transcript), the user's level (1-4), and the target platforms.
- data: the video file and transcript, and the platform accounts.
- upstream: script/video (3)
- downstream: feeds the email list and monetisation (8).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Check the 4 Levels of Being a YouTuber. If not shipping one good video a week, stop. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
2. Choose levels from the 9 Levels menu. Beginners do L1 (thread) and L2 (Shorts) only. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
3. Write the Twitter thread (~10 min). [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
4. Cut Shorts: a vertical cut, a slightly different thumbnail, ending with "for more… linked below". [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
5. For higher levels: butcher listicles into one clip per point, and find fire moments (30-60s). [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md, chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
6. Make titled, subtitled clips in square and portrait from one template. [chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
7. Publish the micro content ~1 minute after the pillar. Funnel public platforms toward the email list. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
### criteria
- The user's level is stated, and the chosen repurposing levels match it. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- Every clip has a title and subtitles. [chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]
- Each Short ends with a pointer to the long video. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- Every derivative traces to the one pillar video. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- At least one path leads to an owned channel (email list or content upgrade). [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md, chapters/ch07-guest-workshops-growth-niching.md]

## D-025 7. Editor hiring brief
- ask: "Help me hire a freelance video editor."
- inputs: ≥5 published videos, reference channels, and a budget.
- data: freelance marketplaces.
- upstream: branding audit (5)
- downstream: frees time for (6, 8).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Start after ~5 videos. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
2. Post one project on Fiverr, Upwork or People Per Hour, and accept ~10 freelancers ($5-10/hr) as a paid audition. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
3. Pay per hour, with no fixed revision caps. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
4. Give a mood board of reference channels. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]

### criteria
- The brief uses the same raw footage for all auditions. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- ~10 candidates are planned. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- It is hourly, with no revision cap. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- A mood board with named channels is attached. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]
- A feedback loop is specified. [chapters/ch05-build-your-empire.md]

## D-026 8. Monetisation plan
- ask: "Model how much my channel could earn and what to sell."
- inputs: average views, niche, video length, audience geography and wallet, subscriber count, and the products used on camera.
- data: YouTube Analytics (RPM, views, geography, watch time) and affiliate programs.
- upstream: niche (1), audience/email (6)
- downstream: downstream to a product-launch playbook (welsh-idea-audience 10-11).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Check YPP eligibility (1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months), and turn ads on. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
2. Model AdSense as (views ÷ 1,000) × RPM, not CPM. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
3. List the levers: niche, watch time (length), geography. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
4. Set affiliate links in the upload defaults with disclosure. For products used without a program, cold-email the founder to create one or bump the rate. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
5. Defer sponsors until ~20k subs / 100 videos. Sponsor rate ≈ $15 per 1,000 views on the last-10 average. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
6. Match the personal product to the audience's wallet (Skillshare / $150-500 course / coaching), with jab-jab-jab before the right hook. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
### criteria
- The model uses RPM, and the formula is shown. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
- ≥2 revenue pillars are planned (no single-stream dependence). [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
- Sponsors are not pursued below the stated threshold. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
- The product price model matches the stated audience wallet. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
- An affiliate disclosure line is present. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]
- The email list is named as the base of the funnel. [chapters/ch06-monetise-your-empire.md]

## D-027 9. Gear and setup plan
- ask: "What gear should I buy and how do I set up my room for YouTube?"
- inputs: budget, room size, and position on the hobbyist ↔ business spectrum.
- data: current prices (the gear models date fast, per `SKILL.md` Scope).
- upstream: none
- downstream: enables the thumbnail photo (2) and filming.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Locate the user on the hobbyist → business spectrum. Hobbyists use a phone. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
2. Audio first: reach Level 2 (shotgun mic off-camera on a stand, a hand's-breadth from the face, with a $10 extension). [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
3. Lighting next: one big soft key light at 45° and slightly above, softened by a softbox or a bounce off a white wall. Close the curtains. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md, chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
4. Camera and lens last, at the budget/mid tier. Use a wide aperture and maximize background distance. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
5. Minimize workflow friction (mains adapter, flip screen, auto white balance). Shoot slightly dark. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
### criteria
- The buy order is audio → lighting → camera. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
- The mic is off-camera and close to the face (Level ≥2). [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
- The plan has exactly one key light at ~45°, with no ring light and no three-point setup. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
- No lavalier unless roaming is required. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
- Each item is at the budget or mid tier, or a reason is given for going higher. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]
- The subject is not flat against a wall. [chapters/ch10-gear-and-setup.md]

## D-028 10. Publishing cadence system
- ask: "Help me actually post one video every week."
- inputs: available hours, a preferred day, and a potential accountability partner.
- data: Notion (or an equivalent).
- upstream: niche (1)
- downstream: the container for (2, 3).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Commit to one video a week as the non-negotiable. [chapters/ch01-youtube-foundations.md]
2. Fix a self-imposed publish slot (e.g. every Tuesday). [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
3. Pair with an accountability partner or group, built around concrete commitments. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
4. Batch-film on one recurring day at a sustainable rate, prep B-roll in the same pass, and bank a buffer. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
5. Track the pipeline in Notion pages + databases (≤2 layers), using a database template for the video brief (title, thumbnail, hook, intro, conclusion). [chapters/ch11-bonus-speaking-notion-copyright.md]
6. Measure getting better at talking, filming and editing, not views, until the 20-40-50 threshold. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
### criteria
- A specific weekday publish slot is named. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
- A batch day and a buffer target are named. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
- An accountability check-in has a concrete commitment format. [chapters/ch09-office-hours-qa.md]
- The brief template has the title/thumbnail/hook/intro/conclusion fields. [chapters/ch11-bonus-speaking-notion-copyright.md]
- The Notion setup is ≤2 layers deep. [chapters/ch11-bonus-speaking-notion-copyright.md]
