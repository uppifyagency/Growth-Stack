# neville-copywriting

## C-001 N1. Headline and subject-line set
- ask: "Give me 10 headline options for this page" / "Write subject lines for this email."
- inputs: product, Bob, the main benefit to the reader, channel (page headline vs email subject).
- data: optional upvoted titles from social sites and Google related searches; none required.
- upstream: Bob
- downstream: feeds N3, N4, N7, N12 (revamp step 2), L2.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Write one decent headline that sells what the product does for *them*, not the product. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
2. Rewrite it through the **CBI** lenses: Competitive, Benefit-driven (stat or testimonial), Inspirational ("you can too"). [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, patterns.md]
3. Mine **socially-proven headlines**: search the product keyword on Reddit/Quora/Digg/Twitter sorted by top, plus Google "searches related to"; adapt to your angle. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, patterns.md, cheatsheet.md]
4. Use the **"Overheard on the Street"** trick: list lines that would make a passer-by say "WHAT?", fuse one with the product. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
5. For email subjects: shorten, lowercase, casual, front-load meaning for mobile truncation. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, cheatsheet.md]
6. For a sales page, brainstorm many (the source says ~100) and pick the best; headline + subhead must carry the whole gist. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
7. Test; never ship one winning formula forever. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, patterns.md]
### criteria
- Every option is about the reader's outcome, none names the company/product as subject. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
- Options cover all three CBI lenses. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
- No clickbait ("Re:", "hey…", "you won't believe…"), and each is relevant to the body. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Email subjects: short, lowercase, no coupon and no "Welcome to [App]". [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Each hits at least one trigger: new, novel, unique, helpful. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Page version: headline + subhead together state the whole gist. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]

## C-002 N2. Cold sales email
- ask: "Write a cold email to [prospect] to book a call."
- inputs: recipient name/company/title, one quantified benefit for them, the single ask, whether you have a "value-bomb" to give.
- data: recipient research (public profile, company info).
- upstream: Bob, offer
- downstream: feeds the reply/call; reused in N13.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Research the recipient (company, title, LinkedIn) to personalize; never "to whom it may concern". [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
2. Apply the **give-or-keep-it-short** gate: real value to give → give it; nothing → one or two sentences with the direct ask. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md, patterns.md]
3. Lead with one quantified benefit to them ("$24k/year saved"), ideally backed by a custom proof artifact built from public info. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md, patterns.md]
4. Structure on the **Cold Email Outline**: intro → what it does for them → what happens if they get it → when can we talk. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md, cheatsheet.md]
5. If you are the product, put "what I'll do for you" first and demote credentials to bullets. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
6. End with exactly one bolded ask. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
7. Cut every sentence that does not advance the ask (worked examples cut 60–80%). [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md, patterns.md]
### criteria
- Uses the recipient's real name. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
- No company history (founding year, city, "we have been…"). [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
- Contains a quantified benefit to the reader, or the whole email is ≤2 sentences. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
- Exactly one ask, bolded; no second link or option. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
- Four outline blocks present in order (when not using the 2-sentence form). [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]
- No benefit restated twice. [chapters/ch04-cold-sales-emails.md]

## C-003 N3. Single sales email or short letter (AIDA)
- ask: "Write an email that sells X" / "Draft a one-page letter for this offer."
- inputs: Bob, the one action, 3–7 facts/benefits, ROI math and emotional payoff.
- data: none.
- upstream: Bob, N1
- downstream: reused inside N8 and N10.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Decide the single action first and write the CTA before the rest. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
2. Write 4–5 subject lines and a one-line "purpose of email". [patterns.md]
3. Type bold labels A/I/D/A and write one sentence under each: Attention (headline/question/name), Interest (3–7 facts), Desire (logical ROI + emotional status/relief/fear), Action (one instruction). [patterns.md, SKILL.md]
4. Expand each, then delete the labels; every line's job is to get the next read, fueled by a story or education (**Slippery Slide**). [patterns.md, chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
5. For newsletters and deal emails, keep ~70% useful content, 30% pitch (**70/30**). [chapters/ch06-content-marketing-writing.md, patterns.md]
6. Format pass: bullets, bold, ~500px column, captions, a **BFB** CTA, a bolded P.S. [patterns.md, chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
7. Cut any line that fails "Does it add to the message?" or bores you. [SKILL.md, chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
### criteria
- Exactly one CTA/link. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, patterns.md]
- Interest section lists no more than ~7 facts. [patterns.md]
- Desire contains both a number (ROI) and an emotional payoff. [patterns.md]
- Opens on the reader's problem, not the founder story (Reader-First Rewrite). [patterns.md]
- Bolded P.S. present. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]
- Plain/minimal look, not "newslettery" graphics. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
- Written with "I"/"you" to one person. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md]

## C-004 N4. Long-form sales page (Modified AIDA + T)
- ask: "Write a sales page for my course/service."
- inputs: product, price, how much the buyer already knows, testimonials, proof/case studies, exact buying steps.
- data: none.
- upstream: Bob, N1, testimonials
- downstream: feeds N10 (copy reuse), N6 (review).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Size the page to buyer knowledge and price (**Sugarman's rule**): known/cheap → buy button; unfamiliar/expensive → full sequence. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, patterns.md]
2. Name the single goal (call/apply/buy) (Work-Backwards). [patterns.md]
3. Write each block separately with the **Sales Page Outline Template**: headline(s) + subhead + image → Interest (easier than they think) → Desire (the new world) → Testimonial → product shot → comparison → exact how-to-buy → Action with light P.S. scarcity. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, patterns.md]
4. Pair every feature with its benefit for first-time buyers. [chapters/ch06-content-marketing-writing.md]
5. Add show-don't-tell proof: varied case studies (big/medium/small), collective testimonials, a believable reason-why/mechanism. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, patterns.md]
6. Stack the blocks verbatim into one page; put the CTA in every section and the buy block at the bottom. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
### criteria
- One goal; CTA repeats per section and closes at the bottom. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Testimonials shown as a mass, not one quote. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- Nothing below the checkout block; no mid-page checkout. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- No sliders/carousels, no clever labels, no buzzwords. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Explicit plain ask ("Order yours today" / "Apply"). [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- Each feature has a stated benefit. [chapters/ch06-content-marketing-writing.md]
- Contains a reason-why the product works. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- Phone number captured when a call is the money step. [cheatsheet.md]

## C-005 N5. eCommerce product page (Amazon field set)
- ask: "Write/audit the product page for SKU X."
- inputs: product specs, price, reviews, stock, shipping, box contents, compatible products, customer questions (support log).
- data: support tickets/FAQ log (the source cites 80% of one company's calls being one unanswered spec) `chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md`.
- upstream: product data
- downstream: is itself a landing page (feeds N6, L1 ideas).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Open a top Amazon page in the category and copy the field list. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
2. Fill: name, price, reviews, stock-left, one-line summary. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, patterns.md]
3. Full description: what it is, what it does, special features, why it is better. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
4. The "boring" block: dimensions, weight, batteries/power, what's in the box, shipping weight/speed, complementary products. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, cheatsheet.md]
5. List every showroom/phone question and answer it on the page. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
6. Use photo/video for what is visual; text only for what is not. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
7. Put differentiator, "sciencey" mechanism and proof on this page as text, not baked into images. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
### criteria
- All Amazon fields present, or the missing one is one you genuinely cannot fill. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, patterns.md]
- Boring block complete. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- No differentiator offloaded to "another page". [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- Key claims are live text, not inside images. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]
- Plain-English labels, no buzzwords. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]

## C-006 N6. Page teardown / conversion review
- ask: "Review my landing page / homepage and tell me what to fix."
- inputs: page URL or copy, the page's intended goal, optional heat map, ad CTR and order data.
- data: heat map (Crazy Egg) and live traffic optional; ad CTR/orders for the scent check.
- upstream: an existing page (N4, N5, L1)
- downstream: feeds rewrite.
- depth: full

### steps
1. **Bleary-Eyes Read**: in ~5 seconds, is "what is this / what do I do" clear? Fix that first. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md, patterns.md]
2. Decide page type: landing page (one CTA, top and bottom) vs homepage (more breadth). [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
3. Work backwards from the single goal: confirm the CTA repeats per section and closes. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md, patterns.md]
4. Strip buzzwords, clever labels, sliders/carousels, mid-page checkouts. [patterns.md, cheatsheet.md]
5. Confusing offer → add a literal **1-2-3 how-it-works** block with an image. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
6. With traffic: read the heat map, find the **deep-blue-of-death** zone, fix that section, leave warm pages alone. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
7. High CTR + low orders → check ad scent, page headline, buried buy button. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md, cheatsheet.md]
8. Copytest with the real audience and run the caveman skim test (Test and De-Buzzword). [patterns.md]
### criteria
- States the page's one goal. [patterns.md]
- Gives a pass/fail on the 5-second test. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
- Every recommendation names the section it applies to. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
- Recommends content/close fixes before layout redesign. [patterns.md]
- Flags every buzzword/clever label found. [chapters/ch05-sales-pages-ecommerce.md]

## C-007 N7. Google search ad ("super ad")
- ask: "Write a Google Ads text ad for keyword X."
- inputs: target keyword, competitor ads on that SERP, your offer differentiators (price, guarantee, service area), landing page.
- data: live SERP paid results for the keyword (see DF2, DF15).
- upstream: DF2/DF15
- downstream: feeds landing page (N4, L1), ties to L8.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Read the competitor ads ranking for the keyword; list the priorities they all promise. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md, patterns.md]
2. Fuse every priority into one concise **super ad**. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
3. Keep it to the point, no cleverness. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
4. Word it for buyers only (price, "enroll today", same-day, service area). [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
5. Differentiate on the offer, not the wording. [patterns.md]
6. Match the landing-page headline to the ad promise (**ad scent**). [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
### criteria
- Covers every shared competitor priority. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
- No puns or clever lines. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
- At least one qualifier that repels tire-kickers. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
- Landing headline repeats the ad's promise. [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]
- Fits the text-only format (~270 chars in the source). [chapters/ch08-ads-website-images.md]

## C-008 N8. Autoresponder (follow-up) sequence
- ask: "Write my welcome email sequence" / "Build a nurture sequence for new subscribers."
- inputs: list source, case studies, offer to hint at, whether the business is "boring" (needs an ethical bribe).
- data: ESP with interval scheduling.
- upstream: lead magnet/opt-in (DCR C8)
- downstream: feeds sales (N4/N10); N9 converts into it.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Plan the whole arc on one page so each email knows what tomorrow brings. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
2. Decide on an **ethical bribe** (yes for boring/B2B, skip for an already-interesting blog). [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
3. Write each email with the value inside it (**email AS the content**); fuel with case studies and **Subject + Life** ideas. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
4. Schedule as follow-ups at 0, +1, +2, +3 days. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md, cheatsheet.md]
5. Add the **whitelisting PS** ("reply and tell me your problems") in one of the first 3 emails; route overflow to a Google Form. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
6. **Drop hints**: no offer link in emails 1–3, one in email 4, again around email 6. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
7. End each email on a **cliffhanger** naming the next email's payoff. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
8. Optional demand test: "reply: I want it" PS. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
### criteria
- Plain text or minimal centered layout. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
- No email is a bare notification ("check out my post"). [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
- Every email except the last ends with a cliffhanger. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
- Whitelisting PS appears within emails 1–3. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
- Offer links appear in only a few emails and none in the first three. [chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]
- One action per email. [chapters/ch03-headlines-and-quick-copy.md, chapters/ch07-email-writing-autoresponders.md]

## C-010 N10. 3-email promotion sequence (Black Friday formula)
- ask: "Write a 3-email sale sequence for my offer ending Friday."
- inputs: offer, discount, deadline, existing testimonials/reviews/numbers, list type (warm, cold, none).
- data: none.
- upstream: N4 copy, testimonials
- downstream: feeds N9.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Email 1 (~2 days before deadline): name the product, picture, a few features, discount, deadline, one CTA. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md, patterns.md]
2. Email 2 (**Cool Angle**): clip existing proof: testimonials, review screenshots, big numbers, backstory, or a price-tier table. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
3. Email 3 (**close the door**): one or two sentences, deadline tonight, offer, CTA. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
4. Too cheap to discount → run a **Reverse Black Friday** (price going up). [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
5. No list → post the same 3-step structure to friends/family with "DM me"/"comment" as CTA. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
6. Reuse the copy on sales page/blog; pre-load into the autoresponder; next year change the dates. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md, patterns.md]
### criteria
- Email 1 has product name, picture, features, discount, deadline, one CTA. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
- Email 2 proof is existing material, nothing invented. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
- Email 3 is ≤2 sentences. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md, patterns.md]
- Same deadline in all three. [chapters/ch10-building-selling-courses.md]
- Cold list version adds a polite one-liner. [patterns.md]

## C-011 N11. Blog post (Research → Write → Promote)
- ask: "Help me write a blog post about X."
- inputs: Idea File or topic, your credibility on it, the next action the post should drive.
- data: optional AlsoAsked / "people also ask" for topics `chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md` (see DF2).
- upstream: DCR C4 brief (if SEO-targeted)
- downstream: feeds N12 later, social posts.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Keep an **Idea File** with 4 buckets (topics I enjoy, things people ask me, interesting personal stories, possible articles); pick the one you are most excited to write. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
2. **Credibility test**: credible → broad guide; not credible → specific "how I did it". [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md, patterns.md]
3. Pick one of the **6 article types**. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
4. Write the **Skeleton Outline** (title + subheads); toss the idea if it reads weird. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
5. Make each subhead specific; end each section with an **action step**; bundle all steps into a **cheat sheet**. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
6. First draft bans: no SEO, no selling, no perfectionism; intro and conclusion last; one image per section. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
7. End with one next action; chop into social posts to promote. [chapters/ch06-content-marketing-writing.md, chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
### criteria
- Every subhead is specific (not "Eat better"). [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
- Every section ends with a concrete action step. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
- Cheat sheet contains all action steps. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
- Broad "how to" framing only if author credibility is stated; otherwise "how I". [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]
- Exactly one CTA at the end. [chapters/ch06-content-marketing-writing.md]
- One image per section. [chapters/ch11-best-blog-post-bootcamp.md]

## C-013 N13. First-gig service offer (getting clients)
- ask: "Help me land my first freelance client."
- inputs: your technical skills, real-world skills, previously-paid experience.
- data: none.
- upstream: none
- downstream: feeds N2.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Build the **Lazy Google Doc Portfolio** (technical skills, real-world skills, previous experience, optional testimonials, contact). [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md, patterns.md]
2. Write one concrete $100 offer with restricted scope ("for $100 you get this, this, this"). [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md, patterns.md]
3. Add **Three-Pronged Pricing**: ~$500 mid, open-ended top. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md, SKILL.md]
4. Post to personal Facebook/LinkedIn first; wait 24–48h; then Craigslist/Gumtree/Thumbtack. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md, patterns.md]
5. Flooded with leads → double the price, do not delete the post. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md]
6. Cold email in 1–2 sentences, then call. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md, patterns.md]
7. Document before/after of every job. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md]
### criteria
- Portfolio is a shareable doc, not a built site. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md]
- Offer lists exactly what the $100 includes. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md]
- Three price tiers stated. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md]
- Cold message ≤2 sentences. [chapters/ch09-becoming-a-copywriter-clients.md]
