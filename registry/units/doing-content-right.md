# doing-content-right

## C-042 C1. Positioning one-liner and niche validation (Personal Monopoly)
- ask: "Help me pick a niche for my newsletter/blog" / "Write my one-line value prop."
- inputs: your experience, skills, obsessions, closest competitors.
- data: search volume (DF1/DF9), community discovery.
- upstream: none
- downstream: feeds C2, C3, S1, and Bob (N-series).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Find the **Personal Monopoly**: "What have you done that 95%+ haven't?", "5+ years you can transfer fast?", "What would you have loved 5 years ago?", "What do you think about in the shower?". [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
2. Check the **Idea Ikigai**: interesting + doesn't exist in that form + your unique exposure. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
3. **Saturation reframe**: is it "the new (if relevant)" or "the old, 1% better"?. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md, cheatsheet.md]
4. Write the **"what for who"** one-liner: biggest pain + differentiator vs closest competitor + core audience. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md, patterns.md]
5. Run the **"most ___ blog I know"** test until one adjective surfaces. [patterns.md, chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
6. Validate: search volume + existing newsletters/blogs/communities + market growth. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md, patterns.md]
7. Define 100 true fans in granular detail; identify 3–5 distribution vectors. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
### criteria
- One-liner is one sentence containing pain, differentiator, audience. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
- Names the specific vector of "better" (funnier, more researched…). [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
- Validation shows evidence for all three checks. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
- Lists 3–5 distribution vectors. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]
- Starts specific (narrow audience). [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md, patterns.md]

## C-043 C2. Publication charter (Unwritten Contract and stack)
- ask: "Set up my newsletter: values, format, tools, paid or free."
- inputs: C1, goals, budget.
- data: none.
- upstream: C1
- downstream: feeds C3, C9.
- depth: full

### steps
1. **Core Values Exercise**: list everything you want, flag conflicts, reorder, circle non-negotiables, resolve conflicts, set guardrails. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md, patterns.md]
2. Decide **paid vs free** ("How much is my solution worth?"); paid only if a painkiller with a clear value prop. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md, cheatsheet.md]
3. Pick the 6-part stack (domain, hosting, platform, ESP, modal software, analytics) in days, not weeks. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md]
4. Set a threshold of excellence below which nothing ships. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md]
5. Run a blog (discoverable) + newsletter (owned) together. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md, SKILL.md]
### criteria
- Non-negotiables written and tied to the value prop. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md]
- Paid/free decision has a stated reason. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md]
- All 6 stack components chosen. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md]
- Both blog and newsletter planned. [chapters/ch01-personal-monopoly.md]

## C-044 C3. Distribution channel plan
- ask: "How do I get readers for my content?"
- inputs: C1 audience, time and budget.
- data: channel analytics; optional DF9 trends.
- upstream: C1, C2
- downstream: feeds C4, C8.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Classify candidate channels with **Brick by Brick**: Bedrock, Viral, Social, Syndicate, Targeted, Paid. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md, SKILL.md]
2. Score each on **CODES** (Cost, Ownership, Dependability, Effort, Scalability). [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
3. **Explore**: 1–3 channels in 2–4 week cohorts; cut those without product-channel fit; exploit winners until 2–5 reliable channels. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
4. Tier channels 0–3 (0 = no-brainers: SEO, referral program, newsletter). [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
5. Under ~100 fans: do things that don't scale (1:1 outreach, reply to all, weak ties, build in the open). [chapters/ch03-distribution.md, patterns.md]
6. Set SMART goals as inputs; avoid hollow metrics; aim for ~50:50 writing vs distribution effort. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
7. Match copy to each platform's vibe (HN matter-of-fact; Reddit full content + soft CTA; no upvote begging). [chapters/ch03-distribution.md, patterns.md]
### criteria
- Every channel has a CODES score. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
- At least one owned bedrock channel (newsletter). [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
- No dependence on a viral channel. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md, SKILL.md]
- Each test has a 2–4 week cohort window and a cut rule. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
- Goals are input-based and SMART. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]

## C-045 C4. SEO article keyword brief
- ask: "Pick a keyword and brief me for this article."
- inputs: the article idea, your domain's authority, keyword tools.
- data: keyword volume, KD, SERP, PAA, GSC (DF1, DF2).
- upstream: C1, DF1, DF2
- downstream: feeds N11 drafting, S9.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Decide if the article is informational (only optimize those; leave inspirational pieces alone). [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
2. Brainstorm: "What problem does this solve?" → "How would someone type it into Google?"; if nobody searches your product, target **Associated Problems**. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
3. Pick ONE primary keyword; validate intent by Googling it and reading the top-3 result types. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
4. Check volume (~200–5,000/month unless a big site). [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
5. Check competitiveness (difficulty = DA + link profile of current rankers, Ahrefs). [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
6. Build the outline from People Also Ask, related/"also rank for" keywords, and ranking articles. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
7. On-page: keyword in title/meta/slug/body, secondaries sprinkled, title ≤60, meta ≤160, length as long as the problem demands. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
8. Publish → GSC URL Inspection → Request Indexing; set canonical. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
9. Build links toward DA ~10 (C7); watch impressions in GSC. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
10. Re-optimize later (C6). [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
### criteria
- Exactly one primary keyword. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
- Top-3 SERP results are the same content type as the planned article. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
- Volume in ~200–5,000/mo or justified. [chapters/ch04-seo.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Difficulty compared to own domain strength. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
- Keyword in title, meta, slug, body; title ≤60, meta ≤160. [chapters/ch04-seo.md, patterns.md]
- Outline cites PAA/ranking-article sources. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]

## C-047 C6. Fresh-domain link-building plan ("Second Chances")
- ask: "My site is new, how do I get Google to start ranking it?"
- inputs: domain authority today, linkable assets, expertise.
- data: backlink tool (DF5, DF6).
- upstream: none
- downstream: supports C4.
- depth: partial

### steps
1. Target ~30 follow links to cross DA ~10 so Google retests the content. [chapters/ch04-seo.md, patterns.md]
2. Sources: linkable assets, interviews/HARO (10–30% hit rate), a free tool, unlinked mentions (Google Alerts/Mention), expired domains. [chapters/ch04-seo.md, patterns.md]
3. Avoid nofollow and paid/bad links. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
### criteria
- Target count and DA goal stated. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
- Only follow links counted toward the goal. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]
- No paid/toxic link sources. [chapters/ch04-seo.md]

## C-048 C7. Email capture system (modals, lead magnets, referrals)
- ask: "Get more of my blog readers onto my newsletter."
- inputs: traffic pages, a lead magnet, ESP/modal tool.
- data: modal/ESP analytics.
- upstream: C2
- downstream: feeds N8.
- depth: partial

### steps
1. Mix capture types: event-triggered (exit), fixed, and landing-page modals. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md, patterns.md]
2. Modal copy: value-aligned, social proof, calm objections, no distractions; never generic "Sign up for my newsletter". [patterns.md, chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
3. Per-article content upgrades as lead magnets. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
4. Referral program with one-click sharing and tiered rewards; gate giveaways behind referrals. [patterns.md, chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
5. One action per page (no competing course + newsletter CTAs). [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
### criteria
- Modal copy names the reader's value, not the action label. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]
- Includes social proof. [patterns.md]
- One CTA per page. [chapters/ch03-distribution.md]

## C-049 C8. Monetization plan
- ask: "How should I make money from my audience?"
- inputs: audience size/engagement, how the audience trusts you, revenue target.
- data: audience metrics.
- upstream: C2
- downstream: feeds C9, C10.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Map trust to method: opinion → affiliates; ability → course; entertainment → sponsored copy. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md, patterns.md]
2. Score candidate methods on **SWEAT** (Scalability, Work, Audience, Timeline). [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
3. Back into the revenue target with several mixes ("many ways to the finish line"). [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
4. Diversify and test until one hits; prefer newsletter sponsorships over display ads. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
5. Check each method against the contract (only promote what you use). [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
### criteria
- Each method has a SWEAT score. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
- Lead method matches the stated trust type. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
- Revenue target decomposed into at least two mixes. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
- No affiliate product the author has not tried. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]

## C-050 C9. Sponsorship rate card and Promote page
- ask: "How much should I charge newsletter sponsors?"
- inputs: subscribers, opens, clicks, audience value to advertisers.
- data: ESP metrics.
- upstream: C8
- downstream: revenue.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Pick a performance metric you control that maps to advertiser goals (CPC closest; CPM/CPO middle; CPS furthest, avoid). [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
2. Set a multiplier from engagement and how easily advertisers monetize your audience. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
3. Calibrate with 5–10 sponsor conversations; adjust by fill rate. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
4. Build a **Promote page** (media kit + direct pay links, e.g. Stripe Checkout). [chapters/ch05-monetization.md, patterns.md]
### criteria
- Price = metric × multiplier, both stated. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
- Not priced on CPS. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
- Promote page has a direct pay link. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]

## C-051 C10. Digital product pre-sell and price ladder
- ask: "Should I build this ebook/course, and at what price?"
- inputs: product idea, audience.
- data: sales/conversion data.
- upstream: C8
- downstream: feeds L1 (page), N10 (launch emails).
- depth: full

### steps
1. **Painkiller test**: relieves a felt pain people will price, not "more of the same". [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md, cheatsheet.md]
2. Pre-sell on Gumroad/PayPal before building. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md, patterns.md]
3. Price on perceived value, not effort; consider "duality" bundles (guide + live sessions). [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
4. **Let the market decide**: start low, raise on a schedule (source: +$5 every 30 sales from $10) until conversion drops, then hold. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
### criteria
- Pain statement in one line. [chapters/ch02-building-your-home.md]
- Pre-sales collected before build. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
- Price-raise schedule and stop rule written. [chapters/ch05-monetization.md]
