# welsh-idea-audience

## D-008 1. Niche of One idea sheet
- ask: "Find me a niche I can own by combining my skills and interests."
- inputs: the user's skills (the source references a ~100-skill worksheet it does not reproduce) and genuine interests.
- data: none (the skills worksheet is missing from the source).
- upstream: none
- downstream: feeds the story (2), Content Matrix (5), service business (8).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Reject product-led ideation, and "solve your own problem" unless distribution already exists. [chapters/ch02-how-to-ideate.md]
2. Brainstorm skills and interests. [chapters/ch02-how-to-ideate.md]
3. Write 3-5 skill × interest combinations, each with a hyper-specific unique idea (content format plus cadence). [chapters/ch02-how-to-ideate.md, chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]
4. For each combination, add at least one freelance service and one 1:1 service extension. [chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]
5. Note the product path (Skill × Interest → Idea → Service → Product). [chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]
### criteria
- There are 3-5 rows, each with skill, interest and idea filled in. [chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]
- Each idea names a concrete output and cadence (for example "weekly rap-song breakdowns"), not a topic. [chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]
- The uniqueness comes from the pairing: no row is a single crowded input on its own. [chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]
- Each row has ≥1 freelance and ≥1 1:1 service extension. [chapters/ch03-niche-of-one.md]

## D-009 2. Background + Transformation story
- ask: "Write my origin story I can tell on podcasts and in my About section."
- inputs: the user's true struggles (inward and outward), the pivot decision, results, and mistakes.
- data: none.
- upstream: Niche of One (1)
- downstream: feeds the profile About (4) and Content Matrix core concepts (5).
- depth: full

### steps
1. List inward struggles (internal states) and outward struggles (visible events) from before the transformation. [chapters/ch04-the-background.md]
2. End the background at the moment you found the solution. [chapters/ch04-the-background.md]
3. Pinpoint the change as an intersection of critical milestones. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
4. Write the 5-step arc: struggling → in a position to change → what you did (what worked and what didn't) → results → how others can follow while avoiding your mistakes. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
5. Add strong, authentic opinions. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
6. Memorize it as one talk track and reuse it verbatim. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
### criteria
- At least one inward and one outward struggle are present. [chapters/ch04-the-background.md]
- All 5 arc steps appear in order. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
- At least one mistake or thing that didn't work is admitted (not a frictionless success). [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
- It ends on helping others, not on bragging. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]
- It does not stay in victim mode: the struggle is balanced with progress. [chapters/ch04-the-background.md]
- It contains at least one opinion that is not vanilla. [chapters/ch05-the-transformation.md]

## D-010 3. Moneyball audience plan
- ask: "Plan how I grow an audience that buys, not just likes."
- inputs: the niche, the candidate platforms, and the creators the audience follows.
- data: the platform itself and SparkToro (or an equivalent).
- upstream: Niche of One (1)
- downstream: sends visitors to the profile (4).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Pick one platform to master first. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
2. Set a daily relevant-content cadence (Welsh: 1×/day LinkedIn, 2×/day Twitter). [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
3. Build the social ecosystem with two questions: adjacent voices (same audience, different solution) and contrarian voices (same audience, different perspective). [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
4. Set up notifications. On LinkedIn, bookmark activity pages and log each person's posting time. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
5. Siphon audience through first thoughtful comments. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
6. Use tools (SparkToro) to find the podcasts, channels and newsletters the audience consumes. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
### criteria
- Exactly one primary platform is named. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
- The ecosystem list has both adjacent and contrarian entries. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
- Each LinkedIn ecosystem entry has an activity-page URL and a posting time. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]
- The plan has no engagement-bait or virality goal. [chapters/ch06-play-moneyball.md]

## D-011 4. Profile Funnel rewrite
- ask: "Audit and rewrite my profile so visitors follow me."
- inputs: the niche, the offer, social proof, and the website or email page URL.
- data: the current profile and a pinned-post candidate with engagement numbers.
- upstream: story (2), capture page (6)
- downstream: the landing spot for (3, 5).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Banner: what you do and who you help, readable in <2 seconds. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
2. Tagline: who you help · what you help them do · how. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
3. Headshot: clean and professional. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
4. About: crisp, cohesive, expands on the offer, with social proof. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
5. Featured (LinkedIn): website leading to email capture. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
6. Pinned tweet (Twitter): both popular and informative. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
7. Check that all elements tell one message. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
### criteria
- The banner text alone names the audience or the outcome. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
- The tagline contains a who, a what and a how. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
- The About section includes at least one proof item (numbers, clients, results). [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
- Featured links to the website or subscriber page. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
- The banner, tagline and About make the same claim. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]
- The jargon matches the audience. [chapters/ch07-the-profile-funnel.md]

## D-012 5. Content Matrix + post batch
- ask: "Build my content matrix and give me 15 posts from it."
- inputs: the mantra (or the material to derive one), the pillars, the user's strong opinions, and the single outcome wanted.
- data: engagement data to "double down on what works" (optional).
- upstream: story (2), Niche of One (1)
- downstream: feeds the Content Web (7), the Moneyball cadence (3), and the launch priming (11).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Write the Mantra (the single highest-level rallying statement). [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
2. Add the Taglines under it. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
3. Define the Pillars by asking "what must go well to succeed at the mantra?". [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
4. Assign a Core Concept (a strong opinion) to each pillar. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
5. List the Structures (motivational, observation, listicle…). [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
6. Mint posts as core concept × structure. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
7. Gut-check each post on Outcome, Focus, Structure and Opinions. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
8. Apply the Scroll Test: line 1 hits hard and line 2 makes the reader crave more. Images are anti-stock, and video is short. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
### criteria
- All 5 matrix layers are filled. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
- Every pillar has a core concept phrased as an opinion someone could disagree with. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
- Every post traces to one concept × one structure. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
- Every post drives the same single specific outcome (sniper). [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
- The batch uses ≥2 structures, with no single structure repeated across the whole batch. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
- The first sentence of each post is a standalone hook. [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]
- The batch has no manufactured "wild takes" (divisive, mean or political). [chapters/ch08-rapid-relevant-content-scroll-test.md]

## D-013 6. Email capture landing page
- ask: "Write my newsletter signup page."
- inputs: what the subscriber gets, social proof (subscriber or case count), and the desired feeling and action.
- data: a page builder (Carrd is named in ch11) and an email tool.
- upstream: none strictly
- downstream: the destination of the profile (4) and Content Web (7), and the list used at launch (11).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Answer the three design questions: what should they feel, what should they do, and why. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
2. Outline the page: primary statement → secondary header → social proof → CTA. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
3. Write a bold primary statement of exactly what they get. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
4. Write a secondary header with the how, often with proof. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
5. Add one obvious CTA, then cut to the minimum viable information. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
### criteria
- The offer is understandable in ≤3 seconds above the fold. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
- The page has exactly one CTA. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
- It shows a social-proof number. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
- It uses no stock imagery. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]
- Every element has a stated reason. [chapters/ch09-the-art-of-the-capture.md]

## D-015 8. MVP service business setup
- ask: "Set up my freelance or consulting service so clients can find, qualify, book and pay."
- inputs: the service from the Niche of One, the price, an aspirational hourly rate, and the tool budget.
- data: Carrd, Airtable, Stripe, Zapier, Calendly, Slack, Drive.
- upstream: Niche of One (1), distribution (3, 7)
- downstream: feeds F.I.T.I (9), and call notes seed the product (10).
- depth: partial

### steps
1. Choose the level: L1 low cost and learn, L2 industry average, L3 high-price micro-niche. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
2. Stand up the 7 components: website, service description, FAQs, inquiry form, booking, rate sheet, payments. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
3. Disclose pricing up front. Write an 18-20 question FAQ and a questionnaire with 2 required checkboxes (understands minimum pricing; is the decision-maker). [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
4. Write 6-8 discovery-call questions, and record calls into a CRM. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
5. Apply the automation rule (task value below the aspirational rate, or >10-15 min/day of routine work) to list what to automate. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
6. Map the pipeline: form → CRM → qualify/disqualify emails → call → pricing email → hidden payments page → recurring charge → auto folder/agenda. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
### criteria
- All 7 components are present. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
- The price is visible before booking. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
- The inquiry form has both disqualifying checkboxes. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
- Payment is collected before work. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
- The discovery script has 6-8 questions. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]
- Each automated step names its trigger and tool. [chapters/ch11-mvp-service-business.md]

## D-016 9. F.I.T.I rate-raise plan
- ask: "Help me niche down my service and raise my rates."
- inputs: current clients, the price, what the user enjoys or dreads, and the existing testimonials.
- data: the CRM and win-rate history.
- upstream: service business (8)
- downstream: feeds the profile/content refresh (4, 5) and the product (10).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Feedback: ask clients where they get the most value and what they want more of, log it in the CRM, and log what energizes or drains you. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
2. Iteration: find the crossroad of love × need, then cut or delegate the rest. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
3. Revisit the profile, capture page and content for the narrower niche. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
4. Testimonials: ask at moments of gratitude or results, and guide clients to name a specific result. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
5. Increase: raise prices as demand rises (double price / half win-rate is acceptable), and push low-value clients to products. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
### criteria
- The feedback questions include both "most value" and "want more of". [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- A love × need list exists with items marked keep or cut. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- The testimonial request asks for a specific result. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- A new price and its trigger are stated. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- A destination for lower-value clients is named. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]

## D-017 10. Digital product MVP build
- ask: "Outline and plan my first small course or e-book in two weeks."
- inputs: one specific problem the user knows cold, the buyers' recurring asks (from calls), and buyer income.
- data: Google Sheets, a slides tool, Loom/Zoom.
- upstream: service learnings (8, 9)
- downstream: built inside the launch (11).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Narrow to one specific problem and time-box it (2 weeks for a first product). [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
2. Outline sections → chapters aligned to 1-2 outcomes, and split the work into daily tasks. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
3. Pass 1: brain-dump onto slides. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
4. Pass 2: move the spoken words to notes and put bullet summaries on the slides. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
5. Pass 3: add media. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
6. Pass 4: add resources, checklists and links. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
7. Get feedback from honest people throughout. Ship low-production, with a short name and a Canva/Fiverr cover. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
### criteria
- The topic is a single narrow problem (for example "order a drink at a Spanish restaurant", not "Spanish"). [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- The outline states 1-2 outcomes, and every chapter maps to one. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- There is a deadline ≤2 weeks out. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- Feedback checkpoints are scheduled before completion. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]
- The price is set against buyer income. [chapters/ch12-fiti-move-fast-cheap.md]

## D-018 11. 14-day pre-sell launch plan
- ask: "Give me a day-by-day plan to pre-sell and launch my product."
- inputs: the product outline, the email list, the distribution network, Gumroad (or equivalent), and the pre-sell and launch prices.
- data: Gumroad (pre-order, versions, workflows), the email list, the distribution channels.
- upstream: product outline (10), capture page/list (6), Content Web (7)
- downstream: feeds testimonials into the next F.I.T.I cycle (9).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Day 1: finish the landing page and artwork, test the buy button, set the pre-sell discount (e.g. $99 → $149), write the email and social copy, then announce through distribution and email. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
2. Days 2-9: run the Four-Pass build (P1 days 2-3, P2 days 4-5, P3 days 6-7, P4 days 8-9), with a behind-the-scenes teaser on day 8. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
3. Day 10: send it to trusted reviewers. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
4. Days 11-12: record screencasts and send a short write-up to distribution and email. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
5. Day 13: request and post testimonials, and run a full buyer-flow check. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
6. Day 14: blitz social, distribution and email, share testimonials, and say it's the last day for the discount. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
7. Post-launch: keep collecting testimonials and raise the price. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
### criteria
- Every day 1-14 has an entry. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
- Day 1 contains the announcement and a dated incentive with both prices. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
- A day-8 teaser exists. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
- A buyer-flow test is scheduled before day 14. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
- Day 14 hits all three channels (social, distribution, email). [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
- The post-launch price increase is stated. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
- It is a pre-sell, not a surprise launch. [chapters/ch13-launch-gumroad-whats-next.md]
