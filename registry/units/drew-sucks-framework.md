# drew-sucks-framework

## C-014 D1. Piece brief (Rule of One)
- ask: "Before I write this post/email, help me focus it."
- inputs: audience, the problem you solve, your story, your numbers.
- data: your own tracked results.
- upstream: none
- downstream: feeds D2 and any draft (N3, N11).
- depth: full

### steps
1. Pick exactly one **True Fan**, one problem, one solution, one story; resist adding more. [chapters/ch02-specific.md, patterns.md]
2. State the payoff the reader gets, up front. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]
3. Choose a "How I" framing and an opening tidbit of your story. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md, patterns.md]
4. List the specific, non-round numbers you will use as proof. [chapters/ch02-specific.md]
### criteria
- Exactly one of each: fan, problem, solution, story. [chapters/ch02-specific.md]
- Payoff is stated in plain words. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]
- Numbers are specific and non-round unless round is literally true. [chapters/ch02-specific.md]

## C-015 D2. SUCKS edit pass (pre-publish audit and rewrite)
- ask: "Edit this draft so it reads well online" / "Run SUCKS on this."
- inputs: a finished draft.
- data: Hemingway app (named in source).
- upstream: any draft (N2–N12, L1, S2)
- downstream: final copy.
- depth: full

### steps
1. Wait 24 hours; change screen and state before editing (laptop→phone). [chapters/ch05-clear.md, chapters/ch08-kept-simple.md]
2. **S**: check Rule of One; replace vague claims with specific numbers. [chapters/ch02-specific.md]
3. **U**: signal the usefulness; convert "How to" to "How I"; add proof (see D3). [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md, chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
4. **C**: line by line ask "What do I mean?" and say that instead; add curiosity triggers; ask "Would I say this in person?". [chapters/ch05-clear.md, chapters/ch06-curious.md, chapters/ch07-conversational.md]
5. **K**: Hemingway app, never above grade 4; small words over big; delete one third (**1/3rd Rule**). [chapters/ch08-kept-simple.md]
6. **S**: format for the skim: bullets, white space, short paragraphs, headings; vary sentence length; read aloud. [chapters/ch09-structured.md]
### criteria
- Readability ≤ grade 4 (Hemingway). [chapters/ch08-kept-simple.md, cheatsheet.md]
- Final length ≤ ~2/3 of the draft. [chapters/ch08-kept-simple.md]
- Every result claim carries a specific number. [chapters/ch02-specific.md]
- Every open loop is closed later in the piece. [patterns.md, chapters/ch06-curious.md]
- Uses headings/bullets/short paragraphs. [chapters/ch09-structured.md]
- Mix of short and long sentences. [chapters/ch09-structured.md]
- Reader, not author, is the hero. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]

## C-016 D3. Proof section (Persuasion Proof Stack)
- ask: "Add proof to this page" / "Make this more believable."
- inputs: studies/stats/endorsements, your own results, customer results/testimonials/case studies.
- data: your proof archive.
- upstream: proof archive (L5)
- downstream: feeds N4, L4.
- depth: partial

### steps
1. Inventory the proof you have; start saving feedback, testimonials, case studies. [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
2. Layer 1, **Authority by Association**: studies, stats, endorsements. [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
3. Layer 2, **Your Results**: show what you are doing. [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
4. Layer 3, **Customer Results**: testimonials and case studies (strongest). [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
5. Quantify each proof point; weave proof throughout, not only at the end. [chapters/ch02-specific.md, chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
### criteria
- High-stakes piece includes all three layers. [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md, patterns.md]
- At least one customer result present. [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]
- Proof appears in more than one place. [chapters/ch04-undeniable.md]

## C-017 D4. Hook and opening set (4 Curiosity Triggers)
- ask: "Give me opening lines for this post."
- inputs: niche, what the reader wants, a Surprise Document of "didn't know" facts, a story.
- data: Surprise Document (your own file).
- upstream: D1
- downstream: feeds N3, N11.
- depth: partial

### steps
1. Write openings that talk about what they want. [chapters/ch06-curious.md]
2. Write openings from what they didn't know (feed from the **Surprise Document**). [chapters/ch06-curious.md]
3. Write an **open loop** to close later. [chapters/ch06-curious.md]
4. Write a story opening; optionally a "How I" tidbit. [chapters/ch06-curious.md, chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]
### criteria
- Each opening uses at least one of the 4 triggers. [chapters/ch06-curious.md]
- Topic is on-niche. [chapters/ch06-curious.md]
- Any opened loop has a planned closing point. [patterns.md]

## C-018 D5. Unique mechanism (named framework)
- ask: "Turn my method into a named framework."
- inputs: an existing idea/method you use.
- data: none.
- upstream: none
- downstream: feeds L3 (Mechanisms filter), N4 (reason-why).
- depth: partial

### steps
1. Take an old idea that solves an old problem. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]
2. Package it into your own branded, named system (SUCKS itself is the example). [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md, patterns.md]
### criteria
- Has a name. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]
- Presented as a new solution to a known problem. [chapters/ch03-useful-unique.md]
